# Domain model

The glossary for Trackpad Launcher. Use these words, with these meanings, everywhere: docs, plans, code, UI.

## Trackpad Launcher
The product. A macOS menu bar app that brings apps to the front with trackpad gestures.

## Gesture
One of the five tap gestures: the thumb anchored in the anchor corner, plus a tap with one, two, three, four or five fingers. Gestures are named by finger count: *1-finger gesture* … *5-finger gesture*. The swipe and the swap are the other ways to reach an app, and the window swipe reaches another window of the frontmost app; none of them is a gesture in this sense.

## Anchor corner
The corner of the trackpad where the thumb rests to arm a gesture, or taps to swap apps: the top 25% of the pad's height and 20% of its width, measured from the thumb-side edge. Top-left in right-hand mode, top-right in left-hand mode. A fixed zone; not user-tunable. Its *corner strip* is the part of the corner closer than 3 mm to the top edge or to the thumb-side edge, where a thumb that sits partly off the pad lands: a contact landing there is an edge contact, never a thumb. The thumb arms, or thumb-taps, by landing fully inside the corner, 3 mm or more from both edges. The strip is 3 mm on every trackpad size, like the other edge bands, and shrinks the corner's usable area; the corner itself keeps its size.

## Anchored thumb
A contact that landed inside the anchor corner, outside its corner strip, and rests in the corner. While a thumb is anchored, the trackpad is in *launcher mode*: taps with the other fingers are gestures. Landing order matters: a thumb that lands under fingers already resting is not anchored while it taps (see *Thumb tap*); one that stays down past the tap window is anchored once the fingers lift. Only the landing point is judged: a thumb that anchored may drift into the corner strip without losing its anchoring.

## Thumb tap
A contact that lands inside the anchor corner, outside its corner strip, does not drag more than 3 mm, and lifts within the tap window (400 ms). Each landing is judged on its own: a thumb that lifts and lands again is a new thumb tap. Under two resting fingers it fires a swap; otherwise it is only an anchored thumb that lifted. A thumb that lands under resting fingers and stays down longer than the tap window never swaps and is an anchored thumb once the fingers lift. A press during the thumb tap, withheld or delivered, is part of it: unlike a tap, a thumb tap is never cancelled by a click.

## Edge contact
A contact that lands where the side of a palm rests beside the pad, or where a thumb sits partly off it: closer than 3 mm to the bottom edge, anywhere along its length; closer than 3 mm to the hand-side edge and more than 40% of the trackpad's height down from the top; or in the anchor corner's corner strip, closer than 3 mm to the top edge or to the thumb-side edge. The 40% line is a share of the height, like the anchor corner's size, so the band sits at the same place on every trackpad size; the 3 mm width is the same everywhere, on every band. A contact landing anywhere else near an edge, the top edge or the thumb-side edge outside the anchor corner, or the upper part of the hand-side edge, is a finger like any other. An edge contact is ignored for as long as it stays down: it neither starts nor joins a tap or a swipe, it never anchors or swaps, and it is not another contact for arming, click blocking, Dock swipe blocking or the thumb touch. A finger that lands farther in and slides into a band stays a finger, and an anchored thumb that drifts into the corner strip stays anchored; a contact that lifts and lands again is judged by its new landing point.

## Tap
Fingers that touch down and lift again within a short window, without dragging. A gesture fires once per tap, when the fingers lift. The *finger count* of a tap is the number of fingers that touched during the tap, not counting the anchored thumb or any edge contact. Two fingers that slide sideways more than 3 mm under the anchored thumb are a swipe, and two that slide up or down more than 3 mm a window swipe when the frontmost app has two or more windows and Trackpad Launcher has the Accessibility permission; either way they are not a tap. Two fingers that rest longer than the window fire nothing but may still become either.

## Swipe
Two fingers sliding left or right, started under an anchored thumb while the swipe setting is on: the thumb anchored first, exactly two fingers landed, and one of them moved more than 3 mm, more sideways than up or down, with no time limit. A slide's direction is decided once, by that first movement past 3 mm, and holds until both fingers lift: a swipe never turns into a window swipe, nor a window swipe into a swipe, and a slide up or down that is no window swipe never becomes a swipe. The thumb only starts the swipe; afterwards it may stay, lift, leave the corner or land again, and nothing changes. A swipe ends when both fingers have lifted; a finger that briefly loses contact and lands again rejoins it. It is cancelled when a third finger lands outside the anchor corner while both are down, or a click reaches apps. Clicks and scrolling stay blocked for the whole of a swipe, whether or not a switcher shows. Nothing further happens until every non-thumb finger has lifted.

## Switcher
The on-screen row shown during a swipe: a Liquid Glass panel centred on the pointer's display, holding one row of at most 10 icons, the frontmost app at the left end followed by the 9 most recently used other running apps that no gesture is assigned to, in their order, a glass highlight that follows the fingers, and the selected icon drawn slightly larger, inside the highlight. No name is shown: the icons say which app is selected. A running app that any of the five gestures is assigned to is left out, by its bundle identifier (every running copy of that bundle, and whether or not the assignment is missing), so its slot goes to the next most recently used app without a gesture; only the frontmost app is shown whatever its gesture. The assignments in place when the swipe begins decide the row; there is no setting for this. Icons are 80 pt, 20 pt apart, and the row sits 22 pt inside the panel on every side, measured to the highlight: 22 pt above and below it, 22 pt from the left edge when it is on the first icon and 22 pt from the right edge when it is on the last, so the panel is 22 pt + the highlight + 22 pt tall; when that row would not fit the display, the icons, the gaps and the highlight shrink by one factor so the whole row fits in one row, and the 22 pt stays on every side. The highlight moves one icon for every 100 points the cursor would travel for the same finger movement, with macOS's own pointer acceleration at the user's tracking speed, whatever the icons' size on screen. The selection starts on the frontmost app; sliding right moves it to apps used longer ago and sliding left back towards the frontmost app, in both hand modes. The selected icon is the nearest one, so the selection changes halfway to the next icon. The ends are hard stops with a small rubber band that adds no dead zone: the moment the fingers turn back, travel past the end is forgotten and the next icon is the usual half icon away. The row is fixed when the swipe begins: an app that launches during the swipe is not in it, and one that quits stays in it. Lifting brings the selected app to the front with the gesture haptic; lifting on the frontmost app, or on an app that has quit, does nothing and is silent. It never takes focus, and shows nothing when there is nothing to switch to, as when every other running app has a gesture: the swipe then still runs its course, blocking clicks and scrolling until the fingers lift.

## Running apps
The apps macOS's ⌘-Tab shows, in its order: every running app that appears in the Dock, whether hidden, minimised, windowless, on another Space or in full screen, most recently activated first; never a menu bar app, and never Trackpad Launcher. Read from LaunchServices at the moment a swipe begins and again when it ends, and at each thumb tap that may swap; the model keeps none of it beyond the last swap of the latest swap series, which is replaced when the next series starts. Should macOS stop revealing the order, the app in front when Trackpad Launcher started and the apps activated since come first, most recent first, and the rest follow in the order they launched.

## Swipe setting
One global setting, on by default, shown as the *Swipe to switch apps* switch in the launcher window's switcher section. While it is off there is no swipe: two fingers sliding sideways under an anchored thumb are not a swipe, no switcher appears, the slide scrolls once the blocking window has passed, and only the tap rules block clicks and scrolling (the blocking window); Dock swipe blocking holds as it always does, since the slide lies inside a thumb touch. Taps, swaps and the window swipe are unchanged: a slide up or down follows the window swipe setting. A change applies to the next swipe and is saved at once, so it survives a quit, a force-quit or a Mac restart.

## Swap
Bringing to front the app used most recently before the frontmost app, like a quick ⌘-Tab, with nothing on screen. Fired by a thumb tap while exactly two fingers rest outside the anchor corner (edge contacts aside), both landed before the thumb, however shortly, whether or not they are moving or have scrolled, while the swap setting is on and gestures are active. The target is the first of the running apps, in ⌘-Tab order, that is not the frontmost app, whether or not a gesture is assigned to it; with no frontmost app, as while Trackpad Launcher itself is active, the most recently used running app. It fires when the thumb lifts: the gesture haptic, bring to front, and the launcher window closes; never the launch animation. With nothing to swap to it is silent. The order is read afresh at each thumb tap; while the last swap's app is still coming to the front (the read still shows the app that swap started from, and the app it brought to front is still running), that app counts as the frontmost app, so rapid taps alternate. Thumb first, then fingers, is a gesture or a swipe, never a swap. Not a gesture in the glossary's sense.

## Swap series
The swaps made while the same two fingers stay down: from the first thumb tap that fires under them until no finger rests outside the anchor corner. A series finger that lifts rejoins if it lands again while the other is still down. The fingers may move freely. The series stops swapping, but keeps blocking, once a third finger lands outside the anchor corner; it ends when no finger rests outside the anchor corner. Clicks, scrolling and the system's trackpad swipes stay blocked for the whole series, as during a swipe. The model remembers only the last swap made, to target the next one while that swap's app is still coming to the front; that memory is replaced when the next series starts.

## Swap setting
One global setting, on by default, shown as the *Thumb tap to swap apps* switch in the launcher window's switcher section, below the swipe setting's switch. While it is off there is no swap and no swap series: a thumb that lands under resting fingers is an anchored thumb that waits for them to lift, as before the swap existed, and only the thumb rules block. Independent of the swipe and window swipe settings. A change applies to the next thumb tap and is saved at once, so it survives a quit, a force-quit or a Mac restart. Upgrading from a version without the setting leaves it on and changes no other setting.

## Window swipe
Two fingers sliding up or down, started under an anchored thumb while the window swipe setting is on: the thumb anchored first, exactly two fingers landed, and one of them moved more than 3 mm, more up or down than sideways, with no time limit. It exists only when there is something to switch to: the frontmost app has at least two windows, counted as the window switcher would list them, when the slide's direction is decided, and Trackpad Launcher has the Accessibility permission, without which no window can be read. Otherwise the slide is no window swipe: nothing shows, nothing pulses, and it behaves exactly as with the window swipe setting off, scrolling once the blocking window has passed and never becoming a swipe; a window that opens later in the slide does not make it one. A window swipe shows the window switcher and moves between the frontmost app's windows. Everything else is as for a swipe: the direction is decided once, the thumb only starts it, it ends when both fingers have lifted, a finger that briefly loses contact and lands again rejoins it, and it is cancelled when a third finger lands outside the anchor corner while both are down, or a click reaches apps; a cancelled window swipe leaves the window in front as it is, silently. Clicks, scrolling and the system's trackpad swipes stay blocked for the whole of it. Not a gesture in the glossary's sense.

## Window switcher
The on-screen list shown on every window swipe: a Liquid Glass panel in the top-left corner of the pointer's display, just below the menu bar, holding one column of 2 to 10 rows: the frontmost app's windows as the window swipe begins, the front window first, then its other visible windows front to back, then its minimised windows in the order the app lists them. The panel is 360 pt wide, whatever its titles. Each row shows a preview of its window, 60 pt tall, at most 120 pt wide, as it looked when the window swipe began, and the window's title, reworded by the app's title rule when it has one and otherwise exactly as the app gives it, on one line beside the preview; a title that does not fit is cut short at the end with an ellipsis, never wrapped; a window with no title reads as the app's name followed by " — Window N", N its row, whatever the app's title rule. A row whose preview is not ready yet is empty, and the preview fades in as soon as it is; a row shows the app's icon in place of the preview when its window could not be captured, without the Screen Recording permission, and always for a minimised window, which is also dimmed and badged "Minimised". A glass highlight marks the selected row. The selection starts on the first row and moves one row for every 100 points the cursor would travel for the same finger movement, sliding down to the next row and up back towards the first, in both hand modes, with the switcher's nearest-row choice, hard stops and rubber band. Each time it lands on a visible window, that window comes to the front with the keyboard focus, behind the window switcher; a minimised window is not restored while sliding. Lifting leaves the selected window in front with the keyboard focus, restoring it if it is minimised, and orders the windows: the selected window, then the front window, then the others as before; the gesture haptic plays when the window in front differs from the front window or a minimised window was restored, and otherwise the lift is silent. The list is fixed when the window swipe begins: a window that opens during it is not added, and one that closes stays listed; lifting on a closed window leaves the windows as they are. A full-screen front window counts as the app's only window, so there is then no window swipe. When the column would not fit the display, the rows and the panel's width shrink by one factor, and titles are cut short against the narrower width. It never takes focus or receives clicks.

## Window
One of an app's normal document or app windows on the current Space, or one of its minimised windows. Panels, palettes, popovers, sheets, toolbars and other floating helpers are not windows in this sense, nor are the app's windows on other Spaces or its full-screen windows other than the front window.

## Front window
The frontmost app's window in front when a window swipe begins: the window switcher's first row. There is none when every window of the app is minimised: the selection then starts on the first row, and lifting on any row restores that window with the gesture haptic.

## Title rule
A built-in rewording of a window's title for the window switcher, keyed by the app's bundle identifier: every window of that app, visible or minimised, shows its title reworded, and an app without a rule shows its titles exactly as it gives them. A rule rewords only a title the app gives, never the untitled fallback. The rules are one table in the code, with no setting; adding an app is one entry. Today one rule, for VS Code (`com.microsoft.VSCode`) and VS Code Insiders (`com.microsoft.VSCodeInsiders`): the title *leads with the project*. VS Code titles the file first and the project second, `main.swift — trackpad-launcher`; the rule swaps the first two parts split by " — " (space, em dash, space), keeps every later part in its order after them, and changes no part's text, so `main.swift — trackpad-launcher — Work` reads `trackpad-launcher — main.swift — Work`, `● main.swift — trackpad-launcher` reads `trackpad-launcher — ● main.swift`, and a title with no separator is unchanged.

## Window swipe setting
One global setting, on by default, shown as the *Swipe up or down to switch windows* switch in the launcher window's switcher section, below the swap setting's switch. While it is off there is no window swipe: two fingers sliding up or down under an anchored thumb are not a window swipe, no window switcher appears, and the slide scrolls once the blocking window has passed, exactly as before the window swipe existed. Independent of the swipe and swap settings. A change applies to the next slide and is saved at once, so it survives a quit, a force-quit or a Mac restart. Upgrading from a version without the setting leaves it on and changes no other setting.

## Frontmost app
The active app at the moment a swipe or a window swipe begins or a thumb tap lifts; never Trackpad Launcher. Trackpad Launcher is active only when macOS makes it so: right after it is opened (from Finder, or Run in Xcode) and around the Other… chooser. It sits at the left end of the switcher's row and the selection starts on it, so the user can stay where they are; it is there even when ⌘-Tab would not show it, such as a menu bar app with a window in front, and even when a gesture is assigned to it. When there is none, as while Trackpad Launcher itself is active, the row is the running apps without a gesture alone, the selection starts on the most recently used one, and lifting on any app, that one included, brings it to the front; a swap brings the most recently used one to the front; and a slide up or down is no window swipe. The window switcher lists the frontmost app's windows.

## Hand mode
*Right hand* (anchor corner top-left, the default) or *Left hand* (anchor corner top-right). One global setting. The *hand-side edge*, where the palm rests beside the pad, is the right edge in right-hand mode and the left edge in left-hand mode; with the bottom edge, it is where a palm's edge contact can land. The *thumb-side edge*, the edge the anchor corner touches at its side, is the opposite one: the left edge in right-hand mode and the right edge in left-hand mode; with the top edge, it is where the corner strip runs.

## Assignment
The app chosen for a gesture, identified by its bundle identifier, not its location. A gesture is *assigned* or *unassigned*. An assignment is *missing* when no copy of the app exists outside the Trash; it recovers by itself when a copy exists again. An assigned app is left out of the switcher's row, missing or not, unless it is the frontmost app; the swap still reaches it.

## Target app
The app assigned to the gesture that just fired.

## Bring to front
What a gesture does to its target app: launch it if it is not running; otherwise unhide, restore minimised windows or open a new window as needed, and make it the active app. A gesture that launches its app shows the launch bounce while the app starts up.

## Launcher window
The window that opens from the menu bar icon: five gesture rows, the switcher section (the swipe illustration, its title and the swipe setting's switch, then the thumb tap illustration, its title and the swap setting's switch, then the window swipe illustration, its title and the window swipe setting's switch), the hand-mode toggle, the launch animation switch, the hint, the Accessibility hint and the screen recording hint while they apply, and Quit.

## Gesture row
One line in the launcher window: a gesture illustration and the app picker for that gesture.

## Installed apps
The apps an app picker lists: every app in /Applications and /System/Applications (with their subfolders), in ~/Applications, and Finder. One entry per bundle identifier. An app anywhere else, such as ~/Downloads, is not an installed app in this sense, even when a gesture is assigned to it through *Other…*.

## Recent apps
The up to 10 installed apps the user opened most recently, newest first, as Spotlight records it (an app's last-used date). Opening means launching; switching to an app that is already running does not count. No age limit. All five app pickers show the same recent apps; they refresh each time the launcher window opens and may lag behind the very latest launches.

## App picker
The popup in a gesture row that sets the assignment: the recent apps, a divider, the other installed apps alphabetically, then *Other…* (choose any app on disk) and *None* (unassign). With no recent apps there is no recent section and no extra divider. The picker opens from a list Trackpad Launcher already holds; installed and removed apps show up in it within 5 seconds.

## Trackpad
A connected Apple trackpad. Its *kind* is *built-in* or *external* (a Magic Trackpad). macOS keeps trackpad settings per kind, so an external trackpad's settings can differ from the built-in one's. A Magic Mouse is not a trackpad.

## Conflicting setting
A system trackpad setting that binds an action to a tap rather than a click, so it cannot coexist with gestures. Today: *Tap to click* and *Look up: Tap with three fingers*. The list is one table in the code; a setting is *conflicting* when it is on for any connected trackpad.

## Gestures active / inactive
Gestures are *active* when Trackpad Launcher is running, at least one trackpad is connected, and no conflicting setting is on for any connected trackpad. Otherwise gestures are *inactive*: nothing is recognised and nothing fires.

## Trackpad settings notice
The message in the launcher window, shown while gestures are inactive, that names the conflicting settings currently on (or says no trackpad is connected) and opens System Settings for the user.

## Feedback
The haptic pulses the trackpad gives: the *gesture haptic* when a gesture fires for an assigned, present target app, when a swipe opens an app, when a swap brings an app to front, and when a window swipe's lift leaves a window in front other than the front window or restores a minimised one; and the *light tick*, a lighter pulse, each time the switcher's or the window switcher's selection changes. A swipe or window swipe lift, or a thumb tap, that changes nothing is silent: a lift on the frontmost app, on an app that has quit, or with no switcher showing; a window swipe lift on the front window, or on a window that closed while the front window is still in front; a cancelled window swipe; a slide up or down that is no window swipe; a thumb tap that is not a swap, or with nothing to swap to.

## Light tick
The lighter haptic pulse that marks each change of the switcher's or the window switcher's selection: one per icon or row the highlight crosses, none while the selection stays put.

## Launch animation
The target app's icon floating up from the pointer when a gesture fires for an assigned, present target app that is running, together with the feedback; for an app that is not running the launch bounce shows instead, and ends with the launch animation played from wherever its icon is. Only gestures play it: a swipe, a window swipe and a swap never do. It appears at once at full size, puffs up for a split second, then rises faster and faster as it shrinks, sways and tilts a little to one side, and fades out within about a third of a second, like a small balloon letting its air out. With the system's Reduce motion setting on, it fades out in place instead. It never takes focus or receives clicks, and every gesture for a running app plays its own. The *launch animation setting* turns it off.

## Launch bounce
The target app's icon bouncing just above the pointer while an app a gesture launched starts up, in place of the launch animation. It plays when a gesture fires for an assigned, present target app that is not running, while the launch animation setting is on. *Not running* means macOS reports no process for the app when the gesture fires: an app still starting up from the Dock or Finder, or a running menu bar app, has one and gets the launch animation. The icon is the launch animation's and appears with the gesture haptic, centred on the pointer's tip, its bottom edge 8 points above it. It inflates upward from there like a balloon: it appears at 24 points, swells to the launch animation's 60 × 60 points within 0.4 seconds, and keeps swelling ever more slowly, with no pause, to 84 points 5 seconds later, where it holds. Its ending's launch animation plays at the size the icon has reached, so it swells on rather than shrinking first. It follows the pointer on every move, onto any display. Near the left or right edge it slides just enough to stay on the pointer's display; the display's top edge cuts it off, and it never moves below the pointer. Several launches bounce side by side in one row above the pointer, newest on the right, 12 points apart, and when one ends the others slide to their new places.

A bounce ends with the launch animation, played from wherever the icon is, when a window of the app first shows (a splash screen counts), or 2 seconds after the app finished starting up with no window. It fades out in place within about 0.2 seconds instead when the launch fails, when the app quits, or when the app is still starting up 10 seconds after the gesture. No bounce lasts longer than 10 seconds, and a window that shows after a bounce ended plays nothing. A gesture for an app whose bounce still plays keeps that one bounce: the haptic plays and the app is brought to front, and nothing else shows. With the system's Reduce motion setting on, the icon pulses its opacity at its resting spot at 60 points instead of inflating, and every ending fades it out in place. It never takes focus or receives clicks. Only gestures play it: a swipe, a window swipe and a swap never do. Debug builds' animation tuner can tune the inflate, switch to a Dock-like jump instead, and preview either with Finder's icon.

## Launch animation setting
One global setting, on by default, shown as the *Launch animation* switch in the launcher window below the hand-mode toggle. It covers both the launch animation and the launch bounce. While it is off, a gesture shows nothing at the pointer, whatever the system's Reduce motion setting; the feedback, bring to front and closing the launcher window are unchanged. A change applies to the next gesture, so a bounce already playing runs its course, and is saved at once, so it survives a quit, a force-quit or a Mac restart. Upgrading from a version without the setting leaves it on and changes no other setting.

## Sway
A launch animation's small sideways veer, to the left or to the right, never more than a third of its rise, with a slight tilt toward that side. The side is random, except that the same side never comes up three times in a row.

## Click blocking
Ignoring trackpad clicks (left-click, right-click, Force Click) for the first 3 seconds after a thumb lands in the anchor corner, while it stays there, another finger is on the trackpad, and gestures are active, and for the whole of a swipe, a window swipe or a swap series. A thumb that lands under resting fingers opens that window like any other, so before a series starts the thumb rules alone apply. A press is blocked or passed whole. It needs the Accessibility permission; without it, clicks are never blocked. Under the same rule, trackpad and Magic Mouse scrolling is suppressed: a scroll suppressed at any point stays suppressed, momentum included, until its fingers lift, and a scroll that began unsuppressed coasts to a stop as usual. Pointer movement and a mouse's scroll wheel are never blocked; a mouse click is blocked only while another finger rests on the trackpad inside the blocking window or a swipe, a window swipe or a swap series is on, when it cannot be told from a trackpad press. The system's own trackpad swipes are blocked too, for longer: see *Dock swipe blocking*. With the swipe setting off there is no swipe, with the window swipe setting off no window swipe, and with the swap setting off no swap series, so only the thumb rules apply.

## Blocking window
The first 3 seconds after a thumb lands in the anchor corner, during which click blocking applies; held open for the whole of a swipe, a window swipe or a swap series, however long after the thumb landed and whether or not it stays. A thumb that was already resting when recognition restarted (after a trackpad change, wake, or a settings change) opens no window: its landing time is unknown, so clicks pass.

## Thumb touch
The stretch of frames from one with a thumb in the anchor corner, whether it anchored or landed under resting fingers, until the first frame with no contact on the trackpad, edge contacts aside. Every gesture, swipe, window swipe and swap series lies inside one. What the thumb does inside it changes nothing about its length: it may roll out of the corner, lift, or land again anywhere, even as a plain finger, and the thumb touch lasts until the pad is empty. A touch no thumb landed in the corner during, such as three fingers landing outside the corner and swiping, is not one. Recognition restarting mid-touch (after a trackpad change, wake, or a settings change) starts a thumb touch only if a thumb is in the corner on its first frame.

## Dock swipe blocking
Ignoring the system's own trackpad swipes to another desktop, a full-screen app, Mission Control or App Exposé on every frame of a thumb touch with two or more contacts down, and for the whole of a swipe, a window swipe or a swap series, with no time limit. macOS counts every contact, the thumb included, so a thumb plus two sliding fingers reads as its three-finger swipe, and a thumb that has drifted out of the corner, or lifted and landed again as a plain finger, is a contact it still counts; that is why the blocking lasts the thumb touch rather than the thumb's anchoring. Outside a thumb touch nothing is blocked, so three fingers that land and swipe with no thumb in the corner reach the system as usual. A system swipe is blocked or passed whole, decided on the event that begins it. If the system sends an early warning of a swipe as the fingers land, before anything moves, it is judged on its own: a warning that passed commits nothing, so a swipe whose warning passed while the thumb was still alone is still blocked from its beginning once the fingers are down, and a warning that was blocked blocks the whole swipe. A swipe first seen under way is never cut short. Like click blocking, it needs the Accessibility permission; without it, nothing is blocked.

## Accessibility hint
The message in the launcher window, shown while Trackpad Launcher lacks the Accessibility permission, that says clicks and scrolling are not blocked during gestures and windows cannot be switched, and opens System Settings for the user.

## Screen recording hint
The message in the launcher window, shown while Trackpad Launcher lacks the Screen Recording permission and the window swipe setting is on, that says window previews need Screen Recording. Its button asks macOS for the permission the first time it is pressed, and opens the Screen Recording page of System Settings on any later press while the permission is still missing. It disappears once the permission is granted. A window swipe never asks for the permission.
