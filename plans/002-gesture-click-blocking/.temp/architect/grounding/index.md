# Grounding index: click blocking and forgiving taps

Produced 2026-10-09 on the development machine: macOS 26.6.2 (25G83), 14" MacBook Pro (Apple silicon), built-in trackpad only, user logged in at the console, Xcode 26.6 at `/Applications/Xcode.app` (SDK 26.5). Probes were built with `DEVELOPER_DIR=/Applications/Xcode.app/Contents/Developer xcrun swiftc`. The process running the probes is **not** a trusted Accessibility client (`AXIsProcessTrusted() == false`), which bounds what could be probed.

## Standing of this grounding

- **Not greenfield.** The change lands on the full app from plan 001 (branch `feature/001-trackpad-launcher-app`, HEAD 7c7f7c2). The `how` skill ran over the frame path, the recognizer, the `Launcher` model and adapters, the shell and views, and the test harness and policy gates: `how.md`. The `why` skill ran on the press rule, the button-state sampling, the permission and timer bans, and the peak-count/300 ms rules, as the precursor to replacing them: `why.md` (with its Preserve / Change / Avoid / Risk set).
- Standing of each fact: `probed` (what a probe printed here today; conclusions marked as conclusions), `sourced` (file and heading), `disputed`/`open` (both sides).
- Probe sources and outputs: `probes/ax-tap-probe/{main.swift,output.txt}` and `probes/ax-notify-probe/{main.swift,output.txt}`. Both leave the machine as found: no prompt, no TCC row, a throwaway LaunchServices registration that was removed afterwards.

## Facts, grouped by design question

### Q1. Blocking a trackpad click: what it takes and what it costs (R4, R5, R6, R9)

- **probed** (`ax-tap-probe`): from an untrusted process, `CGEvent.tapCreate(tap: .cgSessionEventTap, place: .headInsertEventTap, options: .defaultTap, eventsOfInterest: <left/right/other mouse down+up>)` returned **nil**. With `.listenOnly` it returned a tap whose `CGEvent.tapIsEnabled` was **false**. Conclusion: an active (filtering) tap for mouse-button events needs the Accessibility permission; even passive mouse taps are created disabled without a permission. The human's choice of Accessibility is the right permission for a filtering tap.
- **sourced** (`CoreGraphics/CGEvent.h`, comment block above `CGEventTapCreate`, lines 256–292, identical in the CLT and Xcode 26 SDKs): "An active filter may pass an event through unmodified, modify an event, or discard an event." "Taps may only be placed at `kCGHIDEventTap` by a process running as the root user" (so the session tap is the one to use). "The event tap callback runs from the CFRunLoop to which the tap CFMachPort is added as a source." "Releasing the CFMachPortRef will release the tap."
- **sourced** (`CGEventTypes.h` line 451): `CGEventTapCallBack` returns `CGEventRef __nullable`; returning NULL from an active tap discards the event.
- **sourced** (`CGEvent.h` lines 322–333): "If a tap becomes unresponsive or a user requests taps be disabled, an appropriate `kCGEventTapDisabled...` event is passed to the registered CGEventTapCallBack function. An event tap may be re-enabled by calling this function" (`CGEventTapEnable`). Swift names on this toolchain (the probe's first build failed on the C names): `CGEvent.tapEnable(tap:enable:)`, `CGEvent.tapIsEnabled(tap:)`. `kCGEventTapDisabledByTimeout = 0xFFFFFFFE`, `kCGEventTapDisabledByUserInput = 0xFFFFFFFF` (`CGEventTypes.h` lines 128–131). Conclusion: the callback must handle the two out-of-band types and re-enable; a slow callback (the WindowServer's timeout is undocumented) gets the tap disabled and events pass through.
- **sourced** (`CGEventTypes.h` lines 102–132): mouse-button event types `leftMouseDown/Up` (1, 2), `rightMouseDown/Up` (3, 4), `otherMouseDown/Up` (25, 26), and the dragged types (6, 7, 27); masks via `CGEventMaskBit`. No keyboard type is needed by any requirement; R9 forbids reading them, which the mask encodes.
- **sourced** (`CGEventTypes.h` lines 139–142): `kCGMouseEventNumber`: "Matching mouse-down and mouse-up events will have the same event number." This is the handle for R5 (a press is blocked or passed whole): remember the number of a dropped down, drop the up with the same number, pass every other up. `kCGMouseEventButtonNumber` (field 3) gives the button.
- **sourced** (`CGEvent.h` lines 141–144): `CGEventSetType` exists, so an active tap may rewrite an event's type (for example a `leftMouseDragged` during a blocked press into `mouseMoved`, so an app never sees a drag without its down). Whether that is needed is a design choice; the pointer must keep moving (R6).
- **sourced** (`CGEventTypes.h` line 134): `CGEventTimestamp` is "roughly, nanoseconds since startup". `FrameTime` is seconds on the multitouch driver's clock (`Touch.swift:28`); whether the two share a base is **open** (never probed). Conclusion: do not compare event timestamps with frame timestamps; decide "blocking in effect" on the frame clock and let the tap read a decision, not a time.
- **sourced** (001 grounding Q1, `plans/001-trackpad-launcher-app/.temp/architect/grounding/index.md`): frames arrive at ~90–125 Hz while any contact touches, and a resting thumb is a contact. Conclusion: the 3-second expiry after anchoring is observable on a frame within ~10 ms without a timer, because the thumb must still be down for blocking to apply at all; when nothing touches, nothing is anchored and nothing is blocked.
- **Trackpad vs mouse (R6):** no public `CGEventField` names the source device. `kCGMouseEventSubtype` (field 7) takes `kCGEventMouseSubtypeDefault/TabletPoint/TabletProximity` (`CGEventTypes.h` 394–399); `IOKit/hidsystem/IOLLEvent.h` lines 163–168 add `NX_SUBTYPE_MOUSE_TOUCH = 3` ("sub types for mouse and move events"), surfaced by AppKit as `NSEventSubtypeTouch`. Whether trackpad-origin button events carry subtype 3 is **open** (a real trackpad click could not be generated here). The plan's only mouse scenario (thumb anchored, no other finger on the trackpad, mouse click in the other hand) is satisfied by R4's "while at least one other finger is on the trackpad" condition alone. The strict reading (a mouse click while the thumb and another finger rest on the trackpad) is a **risk**, not a scenario.
- **Force Click (R4 scenario):** **sourced** (`AppKit/NSEvent.h` lines 59, 264, 299–305): `NSEventTypePressure = 34`; "pressure gestures begin when stage reaches 1 and end when stage reaches 0. This corresponds to the simultaneously generated mouse down/up events"; `NSPressureBehaviorPrimaryDeepClick` ("e.g. Force Click") actuates on "mouseDown, mouseUp, 1->2, 2->1". `NSResponder.h` line 80: Look up is `quickLookWithEvent:`, driven by the app from `pressureChangeWithEvent:`. **open**: whether dropping the left mouse down/up alone stops Look up, or whether the pressure events (mask bit 34, no public `CGEventType` case) must also be dropped during a blocked press. This is a manual check; the design should leave room for adding bit 34 to the mask.
- **Blocked press and `CGEventSource.buttonState` (R8):** **open**, not probeable here (needs an active tap, so Accessibility). Today's `anyMouseButtonDown()` samples `.combinedSessionState`; whether a press dropped at the session tap still reads as down is unknown. Conclusion: the recognizer must not rely on `buttonDown` to tell a blocked press from a passed one. The recognizer already knows when blocking applies (its own anchor timing and finger set) and only needs to know whether blocking is *in effect* (permission granted and the tap live); with that input a press during a blocked window does not spoil regardless of what the sampler reports.
- **Race to note:** the frame showing the pressing finger's landing travels through MultitouchSupport to the frame thread; the click travels through the HID system to the WindowServer and the tap. Their relative latency was not probed. A firm, very fast tap could reach the tap before "another finger is on the trackpad" is true on the frame side, and then pass as a click. The plan's non-goals rule out any delay, so this is an accepted risk to record.

### Q2. Following the permission live without polling (R7, R11)

- **probed** (`ax-notify-probe`): `notify_register_dispatch("com.apple.tcc.access.changed", &token, DispatchQueue.main)` returned status 0; a TCC change (`tccutil reset Accessibility <registered throwaway bundle id>`, exit 0) delivered the notification **30 ms later on the main thread**; 0 notifications in the 2 s baseline before it. The distributed notification `com.apple.accessibility.api` (observed through `DistributedNotificationCenter` in the same run) was **not** posted for this change.
- **sourced** (`strings` of `/System/Library/PrivateFrameworks/TCC.framework/Support/tccd`): `com.apple.tcc.access.changed` and "Failed to post com.apple.tcc.access.changed notification (%u)". Conclusion: tccd posts this Darwin notification after access changes. It is a global broadcast (any service, any client), so the handler re-reads `AXIsProcessTrusted()` and acts only when the value changed. This is a push, so the no-polling rule holds and idle cost stays zero.
- **conclusion, not probed:** the grant or revoke of *this app* in System Settings posts the same notification. The probe changed TCC through `tccutil`, not through System Settings, and for a bundle that had no row. R7's two scenarios and R11's two live scenarios are the manual check. If a real grant were ever not broadcast, the fallback inside the rules is to re-read trust at every existing push (window open, reconcile), never a timer.
- **sourced** (`HIServices/AXUIElement.h` lines 55–66, Xcode 26 SDK): `AXIsProcessTrustedWithOptions(options)` with `kAXTrustedCheckOptionPrompt = true`: "the user will be informed if the current process is untrusted … Prompting occurs asynchronously and does not affect the return value." `AXIsProcessTrusted()` reads the current trust without prompting. Conclusion for R10: the prompt call returns at once; "once the prompt is dismissed, the launcher window MUST be open" is met by opening the window in the same `start()` (the window is a non-activating panel; the prompt is a separate system alert). Whether the alert visually covers the panel is a manual check.
- **open:** after a revoke, whether an already-created tap keeps receiving events. The design must not depend on it: on a trust change to false it tears the tap down itself (invalidate the CFMachPort) so blocking stops within the bound either way. After a grant, `AXIsProcessTrusted()` is expected to flip without relaunch (common practice; manual check).
- **sourced** (`Config/*.xcconfig`, `project.yml`): Debug signs with Apple Development, Release with Developer ID, same Team ID and bundle ID; no sandbox, hardened runtime on, no entitlements. TCC keys a grant to the code-signing requirement, so a Debug build and a Release build may hold separate grants (conclusion; manual). No usage-description Info.plist key exists for Accessibility; the prompt text is the system's.

### Q3. The prompt and the hint (R10, R11)

- **sourced** (`/System/Library/ExtensionKit/Extensions/SecurityPrivacyExtension.appex`, `strings` lines 708 and 1736 of its binary; Info.plist `CFBundleIdentifier com.apple.settings.PrivacySecurity.extension`): the pane identifier `com.apple.preference.security` and the anchor `Privacy_Accessibility`. The URL is therefore `x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.preference.security?Privacy_Accessibility`, the same scheme the Trackpad pane button already uses (`MenuBarShell.swift:121`). Not opened here (it would switch the user's running System Settings); R11 "Button opens the right pane" is manual.
- **sourced** (`how.md`, shell section): the window shows `HintText` or `TrackpadSettingsNotice` by switching on `launcher.activity`; a hint "below" either is a sibling after the switch in the same `VStack`. The panel is resized only by `refit()`, called from `show()`, the `activity.isActive` observation loop and the status window's move notification; a new observed fact that changes the view's height while the window is open needs its own refit trigger. The icon reads only `activity.isActive`; keeping the permission out of `GestureActivity` satisfies R11's "icon MUST NOT change".
- **sourced** (`how.md`, model section): "launched before" is derived from the stored settings record; R10's "first launch" and the non-goal "installs that already launched an earlier build see only the hint" make "prompt once" the same fact as `isFirstLaunch`; no new stored field is needed.

### Q4. Forgiving taps (R1, R2, R3)

- **sourced** (`how.md`, recognizer section; `GestureRecognizer.swift`): the count is the peak of simultaneous fingers; a finger lifting in the same frame another lands keeps the peak; a reused ID (`.landing` on a known ID) is lifted-and-landed in one frame. The tap starts on the first non-anchor landing from `armed` and ends on the frame the tracked set empties. `maxTapDuration` is 300 ms, strictly greater, measured from the first landing frame to the lift-detection frame.
- **sourced** (`why.md`; `docs/domain-model.md` "Tap"): the glossary already says "the number of fingers that touched during the tap"; the code diverged to peak-simultaneous in S1 with no recorded reason; 300 ms was a feel guess never tuned (S5 tuning checkbox unticked). Nothing in plan 001 depends on peak over distinct.
- **R1 edge cases, settled by R1's own definition:** a tap ends when the last non-thumb finger lifts. So a lone finger that lifts and re-lands is two taps (the "Two quick 1-finger taps" scenario), which is what `reusedTouchIDLandingAgainStartsASecondTap` already pins; "a finger that lifts and lands again within the same tap counts once" applies only while another finger holds the tap open. A same-frame handover (one finger's lift frame is another's landing frame) ends the tap on that frame and the landing starts the next, as today.
- **open:** whether the driver reuses the ID for a finger that bounces (lifts and re-lands within a few ms) or assigns a new one. With a reused ID, counting distinct `TouchID`s meets R1 exactly; with a new ID the bounce counts twice (a 4-finger tap with a bounce would read as five and fire nothing). The S1 real-touch gate from plan 001 is still unticked, so no frame has been observed; the "Bouncing finger counts once" scenario is the manual check.
- **conclusion** (test fixture): `TouchScript` can express the R1 and R2 scripts that matter (uneven landing with `stagger`/`hold`, rolling five fingers, two taps 200 ms apart via `wait`, a 3 s rest via `wait`, a press across the 3-second mark via `wait` + `click: .onLanding`); it cannot express a bounce, a thumb-only press, a press before the thumb lands, or a press that ends off the last-lift frame; those are hand-built with `HandFrames` (the 001 precedent). `FrameTime` arithmetic is floating-point, so boundary rows straddle 400 ms (for example 390/410) as 290/310 do today.

### Q5. Where the pieces must live (architecture constraints)

- **sourced** (`docs/architecture.md`): `LauncherCore` imports Foundation and Observation only (enforced by `SourcePolicyTests.coreImportsOnlyFoundationAndObservation`), so CoreGraphics, ApplicationServices and `notify` stay in `LauncherPlatform`; every port has exactly two adapters (real in `LauncherPlatform`, in-memory in the tests); ports and the model are `@MainActor`; state shared with the frame thread lives behind a `Mutex` with one writer (`nonisolated(unsafe)` is banned); no timers, no polling.
- **sourced** (`how.md`): the `Launcher(...)` constructor is written in three places (`World.init`, `World.relaunch`, `TrackpadLauncherApp.main`); a new port touches all three plus `InMemoryAdapters.swift`. The frame path's only shared state is the module-level `sessions` registry; the recognizer's only output is `Gesture?`; `DeviceSession.receive` is the one place with both the step result and the session; `run` clears the registry on every reconcile, so any new shared state needs the same reset.
- **sourced** (`SourcePolicyTests.swift` 13–41): the scanner bans `AXIsProcessTrusted` (substring, so also `AXIsProcessTrustedWithOptions`), `tapCreate`, `CGEventTapCreate`, `IOHIDManager`, `CGRequestListenEventAccess`, `CGPreflightListenEventAccess` under rule "permission", and `Timer`, `asyncAfter`, `makeTimerSource`, `Task.sleep` under "timer"/"polling". Plan 002's R9 replaces 001's R20, so the "permission" rows that name the Accessibility APIs the design uses must go, while the rows that keep other permissions out (`IOHIDManager`, the Input Monitoring `CGRequestListenEventAccess`/`CGPreflightListenEventAccess`) stay and the keyboard masks could be added. The timer and polling rows stay: no requirement revokes 001's R22, and both new time bounds (3 s, "within 5 s") are met by pushes (frames, the Darwin notification).
- **sourced** (`docs/architecture.md` "Privacy and permissions"): "The app asks for no permission" is replaced by plan 002 R9; the doc is updated in Phase D.

## What a test can produce for real, what needs a fake, what is manual

**Real, in-process, no hardware, no permission:**
- Every R1, R2, R3 scenario at the recognizer seam, as frame scripts with authored timestamps (`TouchScript`, `HandFrames`): distinct-finger counting, 400 ms boundary rows, fire-on-lift frame, two taps 200 ms apart, a 3 s rest then a tap, a press across the 3-second mark.
- Whether blocking is in effect on each frame (anchored, within 3 s, another finger down), if the recognizer or a sibling pure function reports it: the same scripts, asserting per frame. "Clicks return after 3 seconds", "when the thumb lifts", "thumb slides out", "lone finger in the corner" are frame scripts.
- The tap's per-event decision (R5 whole-press rule, R6 never touching pointer or scroll types) as a pure function of (blocking in effect, event type, button, event number) → pass or drop, if the design keeps it pure.
- R8 at the recognizer seam: a press during a tap with blocking in effect does not spoil; without it, spoils as today.
- The `Launcher` seam through `World`: the permission state as an in-memory port whose setter invokes the port callback synchronously (the `InMemoryTrackpadPreferences.set` pattern): hint shown/hidden, blocking armed only while gestures are active and access is granted (R4 "No blocking while gestures are inactive", R9 "Declined", R7 both scenarios at the model level), the first-launch prompt requested once (R10) through a recording system action.
- The real permission adapter's push path (LauncherPlatformTests): `notify_post("com.apple.tcc.access.changed")` is public API any process may call, so a test can post the Darwin notification itself and assert the adapter's `onChange` arrives on the main actor within the bound, with no `tccutil`, no TCC change and no permission. What it cannot do is change the trust value.
- The source-policy gate: the revised banned list, plus an added self-check that the AX and tap tokens the design uses are no longer listed while the Input Monitoring and IOHID tokens still are.

**Fake at the seam:**
- The event tap (needs Accessibility and real clicks): an in-memory adapter that records what the real one would be told (arm/disarm, the blocking state it reads) and lets a test feed it synthetic press/release decisions if the decision function lives behind the port.
- Accessibility trust (an OS fact): an in-memory adapter with a setter that fires the change callback.
- Frames: `InMemoryTrackpads`, as today.

**Manual only (E2E on hardware, with the permission):** every R4 scenario with real clicks (including Force Click and Look up), R5 "thumb lands during a drag", R6 pointer/scroll/mouse, R7 grant/revoke via System Settings within 5 s, R8 "firm tap opens the app" with the haptic, R9 "no other prompts" (TCC log), R10 first-launch prompt and the window behind it, R11 hint copy, button opening the Accessibility pane, live hint changes and panel refit, the bounce-ID behaviour under R1, and idle CPU with the tap installed.

## Open questions

1. Does `CGEventSource.buttonState(.combinedSessionState)` still read a dropped press as down (Q1)? Not probeable without trust. The design must not depend on either answer.
2. Do trackpad-origin button events carry mouse subtype 3 (touch), which would let the tap pass mouse clicks even while the thumb and a finger rest on the trackpad (Q1, R6 strict reading)? Unknown; treat as a risk, not a mechanism.
3. Does dropping the left mouse down/up stop Force Click's Look up, or must pressure events (type 34) be dropped during a blocked press too (Q1)? Manual; keep the mask extensible.
4. Do real grants and revokes in System Settings post `com.apple.tcc.access.changed` as `tccutil` does, and does `AXIsProcessTrusted()` flip without relaunch (Q2)? Manual (R7, R11 scenarios).
5. Does an existing tap keep receiving events after a revoke (Q2)? Irrelevant if the design tears the tap down on the notification; recorded so no one relies on it.
6. Does the driver reuse a bouncing finger's ID (Q4)? Decides whether distinct-`TouchID` counting meets "Bouncing finger counts once" on hardware. Plan 001's S1 real-touch gate (`mt-probe`) is still unticked; running it with a deliberate bounce answers this too.
7. Do `FrameTime` and `CGEventTimestamp` share a clock base (Q1)? Not needed if the design never compares them; recorded so it stays that way.
8. Does the first-launch prompt alert sit in front of the launcher panel, and does the window refit when the hint appears while open (Q3)? Manual.
9. Are a Debug (Apple Development) build and a Release (Developer ID) build separate TCC clients (Q2)? Manual; affects only the developer's workflow.
10. The latency race between the landing frame and the click event (Q1): accepted risk under the "no grace delay" non-goal; to be felt on hardware.
