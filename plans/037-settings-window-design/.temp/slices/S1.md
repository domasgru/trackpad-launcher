# S1: The Accessibility hint card with its turning gear

## Goal

With the Accessibility permission missing, the settings window opens with the hint as a bordered card above the form, its gear turning once every 30 s (still under Reduce motion, live), its filled *Open Settings…* button opening the Accessibility page; the card goes, and the window shrinks, when the permission is granted. Both permission buttons' copy becomes one *Open Settings…* constant.

## Requirements

*The window resizes to fit its content whenever a hint, the notice or the update card appears or disappears, as it does today.*

- **R1.** While the Accessibility permission is missing, the Accessibility hint MUST show above the form, 20 pt from it. While a newer version is known, the update card MUST stay first, with the hint below it, 20 pt apart.
- **R2.** The hint MUST be a rounded group with a light border, its message limited to 216 pt wide, and a filled blue button under it, titled as R5 says. The button MUST open the Accessibility page of System Settings, with no macOS prompt first. (Decided: "blue" is the system accent colour, `Color.accentColor`.)
- **R3.** The hint MUST show the Figma gear in its top-right corner, 91 pt in diameter, partly outside the card and clipped by its edges. The gear MUST turn clockwise around its own centre at a constant speed of one full turn every 30 seconds. It MUST turn for as long as the card is shown. With the system's Reduce motion setting on, it MUST stay still. It MUST follow Reduce motion live. The gear MUST NOT take clicks or block the button. Idle with the window closed: the app stays at no idle wakeups, as it does today.
- **R5 (the copy and the Accessibility button).** Both buttons MUST read *Open Settings…*. The messages MUST stay word for word: "Allow Accessibility access to make trackpad gestures work reliably." and "Allow Screen Recording access to show previews in the window switcher."

No special disabled look for the filled face beyond what falls out of the shared code.

### `GearView` and `TurningGear` (new file `LauncherKit/Sources/LauncherUI/GearView.swift`)
- Modelled on the private `IconView` at the bottom of `AppPicker.swift`: `final class GearView: NSView`, internal, flipped, layer-backed, 91 × 91 pt, `hitTest` returns nil. Interface: `init(image: NSImage?, reduceMotion: @escaping () -> Bool)`, a `let gearLayer = CALayer()`, and internal statics for the period and the animation key. SwiftPM tests have no app bundle, so the image is passed in rather than loaded inside.
- `gearLayer`: bounds 91 × 91, position (45.5, 45.5), anchor (0.5, 0.5), implicit actions off (as `IconView` does), contents the gear, `contentsScale` following the window's backing scale (`viewDidMoveToWindow`, `viewDidChangeBackingProperties`). Use `image.layerContents(forContentsScale:)` so the SVG rasterises at the backing scale. Keep the contents correct when the scale changes.
- The turn: one `CABasicAnimation(keyPath: "transform.rotation.z")`, `fromValue` 0, `toValue` 2π, `timingFunction` linear, `repeatCount = .infinity`, `duration` the named constant `30` (comment: tuned by eye), `isRemovedOnCompletion = false`. The model transform stays identity, so the gear rests in the frame's pose whenever the animation is absent. Do not reverse the sign: the view is flipped, so a positive angle should be clockwise on screen. That is confirmed by eye later.
- Lifetime, in the view: on entering a window it adds the animation unless Reduce motion is on, and starts observing `NSWorkspace.accessibilityDisplayOptionsDidChangeNotification` on `NSWorkspace.shared.notificationCenter` with a **selector** observer (synchronous on the posting thread). On that notification it re-reads the reader and adds or removes the animation. On leaving a window it removes the animation and stops observing. Adding is idempotent: an animation already on the layer is left alone, so a repeated notification never restarts the turn (that would be a jump), and observing is never doubled. Nothing else turns the gear: the card going removes the view from its window. The window closing does not leave the window property nil, so the animation stays on the render server, by design.
- `struct TurningGear: NSViewRepresentable` makes `GearView(image: BundleImage.nsImage("gear"), reduceMotion: { NSWorkspace.shared.accessibilityDisplayShouldReduceMotion })`; `updateNSView` does nothing.
