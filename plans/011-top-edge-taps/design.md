# Design: Taps near the top edge count

## Implementation Decisions

Two facts about the code shape the decisions. First, the edge rule lives in exactly one module, `EdgeContacts`, whose whole interface is an initialiser and one call per frame that returns the same frame with its edge contacts removed; the recognizer makes that call on the first line of `step` and never sees an edge contact. Where a landing contact counts as an edge contact is one private test inside that module: today, the distance to the nearest of the four edges against the 3 mm band, with the anchor corner's admission zone carved out. Second, the anchor corner already shows how a zone follows the hand mode: a value built from the hand mode and the surface, with the hand mode deciding which side edge is "the anchor side". The feature is a change to that one private test and to the inputs it needs. The memory of which contacts are edge contacts, the judgement at landing, and everything the recognizer does with the filtered frame stay as they are.

### The guarded band follows the hand mode

A landing contact is an edge contact when it is closer than 3 mm to the bottom edge, anywhere along its length, or closer than 3 mm to the hand-side edge and more than 40% of the surface's height down from the top. The hand-side edge is the right edge in right-hand mode and the left edge in left-hand mode: the mirror of the anchor-side edge. Both bounds are strict: closer than 3 mm, more than 40%. The distances to the two edges are millimetres, the normalised position scaled by the surface's width or height, as the anchor corner's hold margin is scaled; the 40% line is the normalised y against a fraction, as the corner's height is. Everywhere else near an edge, the top edge, the edge opposite the hand side, and the hand-side edge at 40% or less, is not in the band, so a contact landing there is a finger like any other (R2, R3, R4).

### The anchor corner carve-out goes

The old band needed the anchor corner's admission zone carved out because the band ran along the top edge, where the corner sits. The new band and the corner never overlap: the corner is the top 25% of the height on the anchor-side edge; the band is the bottom edge and the hand-side edge below the 40% line, and the anchor side and the hand side are opposite edges in both hand modes. So `EdgeContacts` no longer takes the corner. Its initialiser takes the hand mode and the surface, the same two inputs the anchor corner takes, and the module decides the band from them alone. The anchor corner's `admits` and `holds` and their callers are untouched; the non-goal stands.

### Two rule numbers in `GestureRules`

`edgeBandMM` keeps its value, 3 mm, with its doc comment rewritten to say which edges it guards (the width of the band is a non-goal). One new constant joins it in `GestureRules`, beside `edgeBandMM`: the fraction of the surface's height, measured from the top edge, below which the hand-side edge is in the band, `edgeBandTop`, 0.40. Its doc comment says what `cornerHeight`'s says about its kind of number: a share of the height, because the palm rests at the same share of the pad on every trackpad size, so the line is not millimetres. Strictly more than the fraction is inside the band. Each constant has exactly one read, in `EdgeContacts`.

### `EdgeContacts` keeps its interface, with new inputs

Interface: an initialiser taking the hand mode and the surface, and one mutating call per frame, in frame order, that returns the same frame (same time, same press) with its edge contacts removed from `touches`, the rest in their original order. Its rules are unchanged: a contact is judged once, at its landing, by its landing point; it keeps that judgement for as long as it stays down, so a finger that lands outside the band and slides into it stays a finger (R5) and an edge contact that slides inward stays an edge contact; an ID absent from a frame is forgotten; an ID that lands again, with or without an absent frame between, is judged afresh by its new landing point. No query and no geometry are exposed; the recognizer is its only caller.

### The recognizer passes its hand mode

`GestureRecognizer.init` already receives the hand mode for the anchor corner; it now builds `EdgeContacts` from that hand mode and the surface instead of from the corner. No other line of the recognizer changes: the first line of `step` still swaps the frame for the filtered one, and the state machine, the contact book, the blocking flags and the swipe see fingers only, as today. `GestureRecognizer.init` keeps its parameters, the ports are untouched, and both hardware adapters build recognizers as they do now. A hand-mode change already re-runs the devices with fresh recognizers (the model's hand-mode toggle reconciles, which `LauncherTests.handModeDefaultsToRightAndSwitchingMovesTheCorner` proves by tapping under a thumb in the new corner), so the band moves with the anchor corner on the next run, with no new wiring.

### The nearest-edge distance is removed

`SurfaceSize`'s distance-to-nearest-edge helper was added for the old band and has that one caller. With the band no longer symmetric it has none, so it is removed rather than kept as dead API: the one public change in this plan, with no caller to update. `distanceMM` on the same type has no caller either, but it was dead before this plan and stays outside its scope; this plan removes only what it makes dead.

### Existing tests

The edge-contact suite's parametrised band test (thumb, a tap at the edge, a middle tap, `fired == [.one]`) is exactly the shape of T1, so T1 is that test extended, not a second copy of it: it gains a hand-mode argument and T1's rows; its three freed rows, the left edge at mid-height, the top edge at mid-width and the top edge just right of the corner, all in right-hand mode, are deleted, since T2's rows at (1, 53.76), (62.4, 1) and (30, 1) supersede them as fingers; its old guarded rows are replaced by T1's, which cover the same edges at more positions and in both hand modes; and its name says the band rather than "any edge". The 3 mm boundary test's rows all lie on the bottom edge or on the right edge between 52% and 59% down, so they stay as they are, under a name that names the guarded edges. Every other edge contact in that suite, and the palm in `SwapTests.swift`'s test of a swap beside an edge contact, lands on the right edge between 52% and 78% down or on the bottom edge, inside the band that stays, so those rows do not change; the corner-thumb guard at (1, 1) and (123.8, 1) is inside the anchor corner, which was never in the band and still is not. No `@Test` outside the edge-contact suite changes.

### Documentation

`docs/domain-model.md`: the *Edge contact* entry describes the band (the bottom edge; the hand-side edge below the 40% line; the line a share of the height like the corner; the top edge and the upper side edges are fingers) and drops "outside the anchor corner", since the band never reaches it; the *Hand mode* entry names the hand-side edge. `docs/architecture.md` and `docs/product-overview.md` are unchanged: neither describes what the recognizer counts as a finger.

## Further Notes

- **Resolved: the click-blocking scenario's timing.** R5's scenario "A finger resting at the top edge counts for click blocking" says the thumb anchored "more than 3 s ago" and expects the click blocked. Under the glossary's *Click blocking*, no finger, at the top edge or in the middle of the pad, blocks a click once the 3 s blocking window has passed; Dock swipe blocking is the rule with no time limit. T7 therefore proves the requirement's sentence, that a freed finger counts "exactly as a finger in the middle of the pad does", by reading both flags on both sides of the 3 s line for a finger at the top edge and for one in the middle and expecting the same glossary values; its source-of-truth cell says so. The scenario's "more than" was a slip for "less than"; plan.md was corrected to "a thumb anchored less than 3 s ago".
- **A requirement change, so no reproduction is recorded.** The human ruled this a requirement change, not a bug, and is sure their top-edge taps land within 3 mm of the edge, so no recording of them is made. The hardware check that stays is plan 007's palm recording, replayed after the change: it must still fire nothing.
- **Why the geometry stays inside `EdgeContacts`, and no new zone type appears beside `AnchorCorner`.** A `GuardedBand` value with a `contains` query would have one caller and fail the deletion test: delete it and the two comparisons reappear in exactly one place. Plan 007 deliberately exposed no band geometry, and nothing here needs it exposed. The module is as deep as it was; only its inputs change, from the corner and the surface to the hand mode and the surface.
- **Why the hand-side edge is not a shared helper with the anchor corner.** The corner measures from the anchor-side edge, the band from its mirror. Each is a one-line choice on the hand mode; sharing it would mean one of them negating the other's answer.
- **The recorded palm stays guarded.** Plan 007's recording holds 20 palm landings at the right edge (x ≥ 0.97), between 42.1 and 61.6 mm from the top of 76.8, that is 55% to 80% down: the highest is 11.4 mm below the 40% line, inside the band that stays. The reproduction test from that plan keeps passing unchanged.
- **Corners.** In right-hand mode a contact 1 mm from both the top and the right edge is a finger: the right edge is guarded only below 40%. The bottom-left and bottom-right corners are in the band in both hand modes, by the bottom edge, whatever the side. The anchor corner itself is never in the band, so a thumb at the very corner still anchors, as plan 007's guard row keeps proving.
- **A finger that slides into the band.** The landing judgement already keeps a finger that slides from the middle into the band; the 38% landing of R5's last scenario is the same rule at the new line. T8 tells it apart from a filter that re-judges each frame by the frame the two-finger tap fires on.
- **Hovering palms**, as in plan 007: the adapter drops the driver's hovering states, so only the palm's touching bursts reach the recognizer, and only their landing point is judged.
