# Plan: A pressed swipe keeps the pointer still

## Problem statement

The user starts a swipe (thumb in the anchor corner, two fingers sliding sideways) and, landing the two fingers, physically presses the trackpad by accident. The swipe works, but the pointer travels across the screen together with the switcher's selection. The same holds for the window swipe. Without a press, a two-finger slide produces only scroll events, which are already suppressed, so the pointer stays put; the bug needs the press.

Root cause: a confirmed swipe or window swipe blocks clicks (`Blocking.clicks`), so the click tap withholds the press. While a press is withheld, its finger movement reaches the tap as drag events, and `ClickFilter.decide` turns every drag of a withheld press into a plain pointer move (`PointerVerdict.passAsMove`, `ClickFilter.swift`). That rewrite was chosen in plan 002 (its R6, "pointer movement MUST NOT be blocked") for presses during the tap's blocking window, before swipes existed. Plan 006 introduced the swipe without revisiting it, so the drags of a press held during a swipe move the pointer.

Reproduced end to end through the model and the in-memory hardware, which runs the real recognizer and the real tap filters: `LauncherKit/Tests/LauncherCoreTests/ReproPressedSwipeTests.swift` (temporary; replace it with the plan's tests) plays the thumb at (10, 10), two fingers landing, a press 20 ms later, then a 3.5 mm and a 12 mm slide. The switcher (or the window switcher) shows, and `hardware.offer(.pointer(.drag(button: 0)))` reads `.decided(.pointer(.passAsMove))`.

What the reproduction does not show is the on-screen cursor: it proves the verdict, not that the window server leaves the cursor where it is for a drag the session tap dropped. A probe for exactly that question is in the plan folder (`.temp/probes/cursor-freeze.swift`): four phases, each dropping the press at a session tap as the click tap does and treating its drags differently (rewritten to moves, dropped at the session tap, dropped at the HID tap, dropped with the cursor unlinked from the mouse), printing per phase how far the cursor got. It needs a hand on the trackpad and has not been run. Its result decides whether this plan's mechanism holds the cursor; the plan is written as the requirements direct, with the drop as the mechanism and the check on hardware (see Further Notes for the run and the two outcomes).

No existing test should have caught it: the drag rows of plan 002 (`ClickBlockingRecognizerTests`, `TapGateTests`, `TapGateRecoveryTests`) cover only the tap's blocking window, and nothing covers drags during a swipe.

## Requirements

### R1. A press's drags do not move the pointer during a swipe or window swipe
While a confirmed swipe or window swipe is under way on any running trackpad, the click tap MUST drop the drags of a withheld press, so the pointer does not move with the fingers. The press itself MUST stay blocked whole, as today (its down, release and Force Click stages are dropped), and the swipe MUST behave as it does without the press: same selection, same travel, same lift.

#### Scenario: Accidental press while starting a swipe
- **GIVEN** Accessibility is granted, the swipe setting is on and several apps are running
- **WHEN** the thumb rests in the anchor corner, two fingers land, the trackpad is pressed 20 ms later and held, and the fingers slide 3.5 mm and then 12 mm sideways
- **THEN** the switcher shows with its selection moved as without the press, and a drag of the held press is dropped instead of passed as a pointer move

#### Scenario: Accidental press while starting a window swipe
- **GIVEN** Accessibility is granted and the frontmost app has two visible windows
- **WHEN** the same hand slides down 3.5 mm and then 12 mm with the press held
- **THEN** the window switcher shows, and a drag of the held press is dropped

### R2. Outside a swipe, a withheld press's drags still move the pointer
Whenever no confirmed swipe or window swipe is under way on any trackpad (the tap's 3 s blocking window, a slide awaiting the model's window-swipe answer, a swap series, or after the swipe ended while the press is still held), the drags of a withheld press MUST still pass as plain pointer moves, as plan 002's R6 requires. Drags of a passed press, and drags with nothing held, MUST still pass untouched.

### R3. The tap's gating is unchanged
The gated port MUST open and close exactly as today; this change adds no event type to either port's mask and posts no events.

## Non-goals

- **Moving the pointer back or decoupling it from the mouse** (`CGWarpMouseCursorPosition`, `CGAssociateMouseAndMouseCursorPosition`). Dropping the drag at the session tap is the mechanism; whether the window server still moves the cursor for a dropped drag is checked on hardware after the change, and a different mechanism would be its own follow-up.
- **Freezing the pointer for a press in the tap's blocking window or a swap series.** Nothing is being switched there, and plan 002's R6 keeps the pointer moving; the user reported only the swipe. This includes the first 3 mm of the slide itself, before its direction is decided: a press held through them moves the pointer by that much, and it stops once the swipe begins.
- **Unblocking the accidental press.** The swipe owns the press, as today.

## Implementation Decisions

- **One more rung on `Blocking`, above `.clicks`, named for what it blocks like the others: `.pressDrags`** (not `.drags`: in the recognizer's file a drag is a finger's displacement past 3 mm, `drag(of:)` and `dragThresholdMM`; this rung is about the drags of a press, the pointer events). A confirmed swipe or window swipe reads `.pressDrags`; the blocking window and a swap series keep reading `.clicks`. `.pressDrags` blocks everything `.clicks` blocks, and in addition the drags of a withheld press are dropped instead of passed as plain pointer moves. The ordering keeps its by-construction chain, now `blocksPressDrags ⇒ blocksClicks ⇒ blocksDockSwipes ⇒ wantsTap`: `blocksClicks` becomes "at or above `.clicks`" (today "exactly `.clicks`"), so every reader of `blocksClicks` (the scroll filter's flag, `Recognition.blocksClicks`, the session's `blocksClicks`) treats `.pressDrags` as it treats `.clicks`, and the new `blocksPressDrags` is "at `.pressDrags`". `wantsTap` and `blocksDockSwipes` are untouched.

  Why a level and not a second flag beside it: `TapContext.blocking` is the merged maximum of every running trackpad's level, written per trackpad behind one lock and merged by one `max()`, in the real adapter and the in-memory one alike; the tap thread reads that one value afresh on every event. A separate flag would need its own merge (an OR), its own slot in the session, in the merge function, in the fake's per-trackpad record and in `TapContext`. "A confirmed slide blocks strictly more than the window does" is an ordering fact, which is exactly what `Blocking` encodes; and because `.pressDrags` includes `.clicks`, the merge stays right across trackpads (the window open on one, a swipe on the other, merge to `.pressDrags`).

  Why the verdict reads the level current when each drag arrives, rather than remembering at press time whether a swipe withheld the press: in the reported hand the press lands 20 ms after the fingers, inside the blocking window and before the slide has crossed 3 mm, so the press is withheld by the window and the swipe confirms later; R1 wants the drags dropped from then on, and R2 wants them moving the pointer again once the swipe has ended with the press still held. Only a per-drag read gives both.

- **The recognizer sets `.pressDrags` exactly where it sets `.clicks` for a confirmed slide today**, in the state-level computation at the end of `step`: a swiping state that is confirmed reads `.pressDrags`. The swap-series branch stays `.clicks`, the thumb rules stay `.clicks` / `.dockSwipes` / `.possible`, the thumb-touch floor and the final `max` are unchanged, and the state machine and its events are untouched. Consequences, all following from today's shape: a swipe (sideways, confirmed as it begins) reads `.pressDrags` from its `.began` frame; a window swipe reads by the thumb rules on its `.began` frame and `.pressDrags` from the frame after the model's confirmation until the frame both fingers have lifted, whose level is that of the state the slide falls into (the thumb alone, `.possible`; a thumb with a third finger inside the window, `.clicks`); a declined or unanswered window swipe never reads `.pressDrags`.

- **`ClickFilter.decide` takes a second fact beside `trackpadBlocking`, `trackpadBlocksDrags`: the merged level's `blocksPressDrags`, read when the event arrives.** A press is withheld iff `trackpadBlocking`, as today. A release follows its press, both flags ignored, as today. A drag of a withheld press is dropped when `trackpadBlocksDrags` is true and passed as a plain pointer move otherwise; a drag of a passed press, or with nothing held on that button, passes untouched, as today. Pressure and `Holding` are unchanged, so the frame thread's press sampling (`TouchFrame.Press.blocked`) is unchanged, and a blocked press neither cancels the swipe nor the tap, as today. The parameter is required, not defaulted, so every call site states what it reads.

  Why two Bools and not the level: each filter (`ScrollFilter`, `DockSwipeFilter`, `ClickFilter`) takes exactly the Bool or Bools derived from the merged level that it needs, read when the event arrives, and knows nothing of the ordering; `TapFilters` is the one place that derives them. That stays so. (A latch inside the filter was rejected above.)

- **`TapFilters.decide` passes the level's `blocksPressDrags` through as the click filter's `trackpadBlocksDrags` for pointer events, and nothing else in it changes.** The gate predicate (some trackpad wants the tap, or a press is withheld, or a scroll is suppressed, or a Dock swipe is dropping), `follow` and `portsWereDisabled` are untouched. A dropped drag therefore changes no gate state (its decision carries `gate: nil`, like any verdict on an event already in flight), the two port masks are untouched, and nothing is posted: R3 holds by construction.

- **No adapter changes.** The session stores and merges `Blocking` as it does; the frame thread's wake condition stays "`wantsTap` flipped": `.clicks` to `.pressDrags` and back flips nothing and needs no wake, because the tap thread reads the merged level before taking the filters' lock on every event, so the first drag after the confirming frame already reads `.pressDrags`. The accepted race: a drag the window server hands the tap between the confirming frame's step and that session's level write passes as one plain move, the same single-event window plan 002 accepted for a press on the landing frame. `TapContext.estimated(from:)` (a scroll may be under way from `.dockSwipes` up) holds for `.pressDrags` with no change. The in-memory trackpads merge by `max()` and build the context from the level, unchanged.

- **Comments and docs.** Seven comments change, and each of them carries the phrase "withheld press's drags" so one grep finds them all: `Blocking.pressDrags` ("inside a confirmed swipe or window swipe: everything `.clicks` blocks, and a withheld press's drags are dropped instead of passed as pointer moves"); `Blocking.clicks`, narrowed to "inside the blocking window or a swap series: clicks, scrolling and the system's swipes are blocked; a withheld press's drags pass as plain pointer moves"; `Recognition.blocksClicks` (a confirmed swipe or window swipe reads `.pressDrags`, which blocks clicks and drops a withheld press's drags); `Swipe.isConfirmed` and `answerWindowSwipe` in the recognizer (confirmation adds, to clicks, scrolling and the system's swipes, dropping a withheld press's drags); `ClickFilter`'s type comment (`trackpadBlocking` is the merged level's `blocksClicks`, `trackpadBlocksDrags` its `blocksPressDrags`; under the latter a withheld press's drags are dropped); `PointerVerdict.drop` (a press, its release, a Force Click stage, or a withheld press's drag inside a swipe or window swipe). `Blocking`'s ordering comment gains its first link, `blocksPressDrags ⇒ blocksClicks`. `docs/domain-model.md`: *Click blocking* (its "pointer movement … never blocked" sentence gains the one exception, stated as what the tap does and what it is for, since whether the window server honours the drop is the hardware check's question), *Swipe* and *Window swipe* (one clause each), already updated with this plan. `docs/architecture.md` is unchanged: the ports, their masks, the gate rule and the wake rule are untouched, and it describes none of the rewrite.

- **Tests.** `ReproPressedSwipeTests.swift` is deleted; T1 and T2 are its two rows with the expectation flipped and the swipe's outcome asserted. Updated bodies: the `levels` helper and one renamed row in `BlockingLevelRecognizerTests` (T9); every `decide` call in `ClickFilterTests` gains the second argument, with `dragsBecomeMovesOnlyDuringAWithheldPressOfTheSameButton` keeping its assertions (T7); `TapContextTests`' argument list (T11); two rows in `DeviceSessionAnswerTests` (T10). Every other existing row stays as it is, and the suite staying green is the proof of each "as today" clause.

## Testing Decisions

### Strategy

Three existing seams, the highest first.

- **World through `InMemoryTrackpads`** (`LauncherKit/Tests/LauncherCoreTests/World.swift`, `InMemoryAdapters.swift`): the real `Launcher`, recognizer and `TapFilters` over the fake's two ports and scripted frames. It observes `offer(.pointer(.drag(button: 0))) -> Delivery`, which tells `.notSent` (port off) from `.decided(.pointer(.drop))`, `.passAsMove` and `.pass`; the switcher and window switcher; `pressVerdicts`; `broughtToFront`, the windows raised and the haptic pulses. This is the seam the reproduction runs at, and where R1 and R2 are proved as the user meets them. The rows resemble `TapGateTests.aPressStaysWithheldThroughTheThumbsLiftUntilTheButtonIsUp` (a press held across a state change, hand frames, drags offered between frames) and `WindowSwipeBlockingTests.withAccessibilityAPressDuringTheWindowSwipeIsDroppedAndTheSelectionFollowsOn` (a pressed slide's selection and `[.drop, .drop]`). It misses the real ports, the tap thread, and whether the window server moves the cursor for a drag the tap dropped: that is the hardware check in Further Notes, and by the Non-goals a different mechanism would be its own plan.
- **The recognizer over scripted frames** (`BlockingLevelRecognizerTests.swift`, its `levels(_:answering:)` helper, `TouchScript.swift`, `Fixtures.swift`): the level on every frame. The one case the World cannot model lives here: a window swipe start awaiting or refused its answer (`answering: nil` / `false`), since the fake answers synchronously. It misses the tap.
- **`ClickFilter` as a pure value** (`ClickFilterTests` in `ClickBlockingRecognizerTests.swift`): the whole verdict matrix over (event, both flags), including the one case no hand can produce at the World seam (a passed press's drags while the level blocks press drags: a passed press is a real click, which spoils any gesture before a slide can begin). It misses the wiring, which T1 to T6 and T8 prove.
- **The real `DeviceSession`** (`LauncherKit/Tests/LauncherPlatformTests/DeviceSessionAnswerTests.swift`): the session's stored level and `blocksClicks` over a confirmed, declined and unanswered window swipe; two rows are updated to the new level.

Faked: the window server's ports and notices (the fake's turns) and the clock (scripted frame times), exactly as every existing World row fakes them. Nothing new is faked; everything in `LauncherCore` runs for real.

Before and after: T1, T2, T5, T6 and T8 fail on today's code, reading `.passAsMove` where the row says `.drop`. T7's new matrix row, T9 (b) and (c), T10's first row and T11 name the new case, the new Bool or the new parameter, so they cannot compile on today's code; they are red on today's code plus a scaffold (the case declared, `blocksPressDrags` defined, the parameter added and ignored, as plan 014 staged its rows): T7's row reads `.passAsMove` under both flags, T9 (b) and (c) and T10's first row read `.clicks` where the row says `.pressDrags`, and T11 is green at once. T3, T4 and T10's second row pass today and pin R2's swap-series, declined-slide and unanswered-slide cases; T7's existing row and the existing rows named in the table pass today and stay green. The existing rows that already pin R2's blocking-window case are `TapGateRecoveryTests.theGatedPortDisabledAloneForgetsTheScrollAndKeepsThePress` (a withheld press's drag reads `.passAsMove` under a thumb and one finger) and `TapGateTests.aPressStaysWithheldThroughTheThumbsLiftUntilTheButtonIsUp` (the same once the thumb has lifted and nothing blocks); and `SwipeTravelAndExitTests.clicksStayBlockedForTheWholeSwipeWhateverTheThumbOrTheClockDid`, the rows of `WindowSwipeRecognizerTests.swift`, `ScrollSuppressionTests` and `ClickBlockingTests` are the proof that clicks and scrolling are blocked and the swipe decided exactly as today.

## Further Notes

- **The one open question, for the human, and the fastest way to settle it.** Whether the window server leaves the cursor still for a drag the session tap dropped is not proven by anything in this plan: the reproduction and every T row prove the verdict, and the Non-goals put the check on hardware and a different mechanism in its own follow-up. The probe in the plan folder settles it in about a minute, before or after the change: from a terminal that has the Accessibility permission, `swift plans/020-swipe-click-cursor-drift/.temp/probes/cursor-freeze.swift` (an optional argument is the seconds per phase, default 8); in each of its four spoken phases, press the trackpad with two fingers and slide while holding; it prints, per phase, the drags seen and how far the cursor got. Reading it: a small "farthest" in the `drop` phase, against a large one in the `move` phase, means dropping at the session tap holds the cursor and this plan's mechanism is right; a large "farthest" in `drop` and `hid-drop` with a small one only in `unlink` means the window server moves the cursor from the HID stream before any tap sees the event, dropping cannot stop it, and the follow-up is to unlink the cursor from the mouse for the slide (`CGAssociateMouseAndMouseCursorPosition`) and relink it at the slide's end, with `.pressDrags` as its trigger. The second outcome is plausible: the apps that lock the pointer on macOS use the association call rather than a tap. The plan's mechanism is the one its requirements name, and it is right in its own terms whatever the probe says: an app whose press was dropped today still receives the press's motion as pointer moves, and after the change receives nothing of the press at all.
- **Hardware check after the change, not an acceptance criterion** (it needs a hand): with the built app and Accessibility granted, several apps running: thumb in the anchor corner, two fingers down, press the trackpad and hold, slide right 15 mm, watch the pointer. Expected: the pointer may move a little while the fingers cover their first 3 mm, before the slide's direction is decided; from the moment the switcher appears it stays put while the selection moves. The same with two fingers sliding down while the frontmost app has two windows. A pointer that keeps travelling with the selection is the probe's second outcome.
- Plan 002's R6 ("pointer movement MUST NOT be blocked") now has exactly one exception, a withheld press's drags inside a confirmed swipe or window swipe; R2 keeps the rest of it, and the domain model's *Click blocking* entry states both.
- A mouse pressed during a trackpad swipe is withheld today because it cannot be told from a trackpad press (the domain model's *Click blocking*); its drags are now dropped too, so a mouse dragged with its button held while the other hand swipes stands still for the swipe. Accepted: the same indistinguishability that blocks the click.
- No wake is added for `.clicks` to `.pressDrags`: the gate does not change, and the tap reads the merged level on every event. Adding one would only cost a run-loop turn with nothing to do.
- Force Click pressure stages of a withheld press are dropped already and stay so; a Force Click during a swipe shows nothing, as today.
