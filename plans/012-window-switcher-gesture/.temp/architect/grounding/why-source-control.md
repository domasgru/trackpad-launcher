## Q1. Why `beginOrSpoil` spoils a non-sideways pair drag; origin of 3 mm, "more sideways", farthest-moved finger

### Direct evidence

1. **The 3 mm threshold predates the swipe; it is the tap's drag rule.**
   - `4552ceb` (2026-10-09, "S1: LauncherKit package, gesture recognizer and source policy gate", feature/001), first `GestureRules.swift`:
     `/// A finger farther than this from its landing point is dragging, not tapping.` / `static let dragThresholdMM = 3.0`
   - Same commit, the first recognizer spoils a tap when any finger passes it: `fingers.contains(where: { id, origin in down[id].map { surface.distanceMM(origin, $0.position) > GestureRules.dragThresholdMM } ?? false }) { state = .spoiled(anchor) }`.
   - Original glossary `ccd4654` docs/domain-model.md "Tap": "Fingers that touch down and lift again within a short window, without dragging."
   - Tests from #1 (blame `1cf9c4b`): `GestureRecognizerTests.fingerMovementPastThreeMillimetresIsADrag` (args `dragMM: 2.5 → fires`, `3.5 → no fire`) and `draggingFingersIsNotATapAndTheNextTapStillFires` (GestureRecognizerTests.swift:121-131).
   - Relevance: the swipe reused an existing "drag spoils the tap" boundary; no commit gives a reason for the value 3.0 itself. **Null: no commit message explains why 3 mm.**

2. **The swipe was carved out of "every drag spoils"; the non-sideways branch keeps the old behaviour.**
   - `f2c576f` (2026-10-11, "Five-finger tap and the swipe recognised on the frame thread (S1)", feature/006) replaced the tap's inline drag check with `if let move = Self.drag(...) { if let pair, move.isSideways { (state, event) = begin(pair, anchor, down) } else { state = .spoiled(anchor) } }`, and the same in the new `.resting` state. Introduced `Move.isSideways` ("More sideways than up or down.") and `drag(of:in:on:)` ("The farthest-moved finger's displacement, when it is more than `dragThresholdMM`. Deterministic: the maximum.").
   - `b15701e` (2026-10-11, "Fix implementation review findings", message: "...no swipes default on the recognizer, appearance-aware switcher icons, and test and duplication cleanups; logged under Implement.") folded the two copies into `beginOrSpoil` with the doc comment still in the code: `/// A sideways drag of a pair begins the swipe; any other drag spoils the gesture like every drag does.` (GestureRecognizer.swift:242). This was a de-duplication; behaviour unchanged.
   - Initializer doc (GestureRecognizer.swift:40): "`swipes`: false (the swipe setting is off): a pair's drag spoils like any drag."
   - Relevance: the code's own wording frames spoiling as the default for every drag, with the sideways pair as the single exception.

3. **Glossary text introduced with the design (`0451f0f`, 2026-10-11, "Design 5-finger gesture and the swipe switcher") and still current (docs/domain-model.md:24, :27):**
   - Tap: "Two fingers that slide sideways more than 3 mm under the anchored thumb are a swipe, not a tap; two fingers that rest longer than the window fire nothing but may still become a swipe."
   - Swipe: "Two fingers sliding left or right, started under an anchored thumb ...: the thumb anchored first, exactly two fingers landed, and one of them moved more than 3 mm, more sideways than up or down, with no time limit."
   - Relevance: "one of them moved more than 3 mm, more sideways than up or down" is the product-language form of `drag` (farthest finger) + `isSideways`. It states the rule, not the reason.

4. **Tests that pin "vertical first is never a swipe, even if it turns sideways later" (all blame `10b0e91`, squash of #5):**
   - `SwipeBeginTests.movingUpOrDownFirstIsNeverASwipeAndTheNextTapStillFires` (SwipeRecognizerTests.swift:38): `slide(byMM: (0, 10)).slide(byMM: (20, 0))` → `phases(frames).isEmpty`, and the next 2-finger tap fires.
   - `theMoveMustBeMoreSidewaysThanVerticalWhereItPassesThreeMillimetres` (:44): `(dx 3.0, dy 2.9) → begins`, `(dx 2.9, dy 3.0) → not`. Direction is judged once, at the frame the 3 mm boundary is crossed.
   - `aRestingPairThatIsDisturbedOrMovesVerticallyNeverSwipes` (:82-91): a resting pair that slides `(0, 10)` then `(20, 0)` never swipes and the next 1-finger tap fires.
   - `twoFingersBeginASwipeOnlyPastThreeMillimetres` (:8, args 2.9/3.1); `aMoveOnTheFrameThatCrossesFourHundredMillisecondsStillBegins` (:26). Only finger 1 moves (62→65.5 mm) while finger 2 is still, so one finger past 3 mm is enough.
   - Relevance: these tests encode exactly the behaviour the question asks about (spoil, not ignore, not later-swipe). Their names state the rule. **Null: no commit message, PR body or comment states why vertical-first should spoil instead of waiting.**

### Indirect / circumstantial

- **Why "spoil" means "never later": the `.spoiled` state predates the swipe.** In `4552ceb`, `case .spoiled(let anchor): if down.keys.allSatisfy({ $0 == anchor.id }) { state = .armed(anchor) }`. It is unchanged today (GestureRecognizer.swift:119-122). Inference: once any drag spoils, the remaining fingers do nothing until all non-thumb fingers lift. A "swipe later" would need a new non-spoiled waiting state. Alternative reading: none found; this is mechanics, not stated intent.
- **The swipe claims only movements the tap rules already throw away.** Tap drag >3 mm already fired nothing (`fingerMovementPastThreeMillimetresIsADrag`). The swipe begins at the same >3 mm boundary (`twoFingersBeginASwipeOnlyPastThreeMillimetres`), and `aDriftingTwoFingerTapBeginsAndEndsASwipeAndFiresNoTap` (SwipeRecognizerTests.swift:122) shows a 4 mm drifting 2-finger tap becoming a swipe that fires no tap. Inference: the swipe takes no tap that would otherwise fire. A vertical swipe at the same boundary would also take none. That is inference, not stated.
- **The cost already accepted for sideways drift:** `SwitcherOnScreenTests.aRollingThreeFingerTapWithDriftShowsTheSwitcherThenRemovesItWithoutOpeningAnything` (SwitcherOnScreenTests.swift:18) and `aTwoFingerTapThatDriftsShowsTheSwitcherBrieflyAndOpensNothing` (:39). When the first two fingers of a rolling 3-finger tap drift sideways >3 mm, the switcher shows briefly, then the third finger cancels it, and nothing fires. Inference: the vertical axis would bring the same flash for vertical drift. Today vertical drift silently spoils.
- **Farthest-moved finger = old "any finger" rule plus a direction.** The original tap rule was "any finger beyond 3 mm spoils" (`contains(where:)`). `max(by: hypot) > 3 mm` is equivalent for the trigger, and also yields the one `Move` whose direction decides. The comment says "Deterministic: the maximum." (GestureRecognizer.swift:338). The `Tap.fingers` comment says "Ordered by id, so everything derived from it is deterministic." (:373). Inference about motive: the code comments state determinism as the reason. No commit gives another reason.
- **Once swiping, vertical motion is ignored, not spoiling.** Since `f38cf95`, travel per frame is the mean of the per-finger `(dx, dy)` moves (GestureRecognizer.swift:150-165), passed to `SwipeTravel.travel(dxMM:dyMM:interval:)`. `.linear` returns `dxMM` only (PointerCurve.swift:144-145; test `PointerCurveTests.cursorTravelScalesPointsPerIconToTwelveStepMillimetres` asserts `SwipeTravel.linear.travel(dxMM: 3, dyMM: 9, interval: 1) == 3`). `.cursor` returns `PointerCurve.cursorPoints`, documented "Cursor points the pointer moves along x for one report" (PointerCurve.swift:91), where dy only feeds the velocity `hypot(dx, dy)`. So the axis is fixed at begin. The scalar `SwipePhase.moved(travelMM:)` carries one axis only (Gesture.swift:28-30).
- **The 3 mm constant is shared.** `dragThresholdMM` is also used for the thumb tap (`Self.drag(of: [thumb.id], ...)`, GestureRecognizer.swift:218, from `19cdbf9` "A thumb tap under two resting fingers swaps apps"). The glossary "Thumb tap" says "does not drag more than 3 mm" (domain-model.md:18). Changing the constant would move tap, swipe and thumb-tap boundaries together.
- **Fingers-first two-finger movement is a different path.** `f7a8a4f` (2026-10-14) "Swap: moving or scrolling fingers still swap": "A thumb tap under two fingers swaps whether they rested still, scrolled first, are scrolling during the tap, or moved between taps." It removed the drag-stop in `.rest`. The window swipe concerns the thumb-first path (`.tapping`/`.resting`), not `.rest`.
- **System swipes on the vertical axis.** `9044785` (2026-10-12, "Block the system's desktop swipes during gestures"): "macOS can count the anchored thumb as a finger, so thumb plus two sliding fingers can read as its three-finger swipe between desktops. The tap now also takes the system's gesture and Dock control events and drops Dock swipes while a thumb is anchored with another finger down, or a swipe is on." DockSwipeFilter.swift:3-4 covers "Mission Control and App Exposé". architecture.md:25 lists blocking "the system's desktop and Mission Control swipes". Inference: thumb + two fingers sliding up/down is the shape of macOS's 3-finger Mission Control / App Exposé swipe. Today's blocking already applies while a thumb is anchored and another finger is down (`blocksDockSwipes`, GestureRecognizer.swift:18-22, 180), but only with Accessibility granted (it lives in ClickTap).

### Contradictions (Q1)
- None between sources. One stale comment: `SwipePhase.began` "Two fingers under an anchored thumb moved sideways past 3 mm" (Gesture.swift:26) is accurate today but would become wrong with a vertical swipe.

1. **The app first promised no permission at all, and the AX/tap APIs were banned.**

   - `9044785` (2026-10-12) architecture.md: "...uses it only to block trackpad clicks, suppress trackpad scrolling and block the system's desktop and Mission Control swipes during gestures ... works fully without it except click blocking, scroll suppression and Dock swipe blocking."    - Relevance: there is precedent for widening what Accessibility is used for (twice, both in plan 006), each time by amending the architecture sentence. ## Q3. Why travel is in cursor points via the pointer curve (100 pt/icon), half-icon switching, and a rubber band that forgets past-end travel

### Direct evidence

1. **Original design: millimetres, 12 mm per icon, nearest icon, hard stops with a small rubber band.**
   - `0451f0f` glossary "Switcher": "Icons are 12 mm of finger travel apart and the selected icon is the nearest one, so the selection changes 6 mm in and every 12 mm after, in either direction; the ends are hard stops with a small rubber band."
   - `f2c576f` GestureRules: `/// Icons are this far apart in finger travel; the selection is the nearest icon.` `static let switcherStepMM = 12.0`; `/// The most the highlight's rubber band shows past an end, in icons.` `static let switcherMaxStretch = 0.2`.
   - Switcher.swift:30-31 (from `cb7b432`): "The nearest icon, never past an end: round(travel / 12 mm), half away from zero, clamped. Changes at ±6 mm, then every 12 mm, in either direction."
   - **Null: no commit explains why "nearest icon" (half-icon switching) was chosen over a full-step threshold.**

2. **First change, a speed-based acceleration, motivated by reach.** `5034e95` (2026-10-12, "Accelerate switcher swipes by finger speed"):
   > "Far icons were hard to reach within the trackpad's width. Swipe travel now grows with finger speed, like pointer acceleration: one to one when slow, up to three times when fast. The four numbers are knobs in the Debug animation tuner, applied live. Travel past the row's ends is capped at a quarter icon, so turning back moves the selection at once."
   - Added `switcherMaxOvershoot = 0.25` with the comment (still in GestureRules.swift:30-32): "Travel the switcher keeps past either end of its row, in icons; any more is dropped, so turning back moves the selection at once however far the fingers went past the end."
   - The deleted `SwipeAcceleration.swift` doc: "slow movement keeps its distance (precise, one icon at a time), fast movement covers more, so the far end of a long row fits on the trackpad."
   - Glossary updated in the same commit: "...like pointer acceleration, so the far end of a long row fits on the trackpad. ... travel past an end beyond a quarter icon is dropped, so turning back moves the selection at once."
   - Relevance: this is the stated problem (reach within the pad's width) and the stated reason for forgetting overshoot (immediate response on turning back).

3. **Replaced 24 minutes later by macOS's own pointer curve.** `f38cf95` (2026-10-12, "Swipe like the cursor and keep the switcher until both fingers lift"):
   > "Switcher travel now reproduces macOS's own trackpad pointer acceleration from IOHIDFamily, with the driver's curves and the user's tracking speed, at 100 cursor points per icon. The swipe lasts until both fingers lift, a finger that briefly loses contact rejoins, and a reconcile that changes nothing no longer restarts recognition and closes the switcher."
   - Deleted SwipeAcceleration.swift/SwipeAccelerationTests.swift, added PointerCurve.swift and CursorTravel.swift. PointerCurve.swift:3-6: "macOS's trackpad pointer acceleration, reproduced from the open-source HID event system (IOHIDFamily's `IOHIDParametricAcceleration` and `IOHIDPointerAccelerator`), so the switcher's highlight moves exactly as far as the cursor would for the same finger movement."
   - PointerCurve.swift:139-140: "The switcher's icon pitch, 80 pt icons with 20 pt between: the highlight moves exactly like the cursor over the row." `public static let shippedPointsPerIcon = 100.0`.
   - Glossary in the same commit: "The highlight moves exactly as far as the cursor would for the same finger movement, with macOS's own pointer acceleration at the user's tracking speed, so 100 points of cursor travel, the icon pitch, is one icon. The selected icon is the nearest one, so the selection changes halfway to the next icon."
   - LaunchTuning.swift:41: "Cursor points of travel per icon. The icon pitch makes the highlight move exactly like the cursor over the row." AnimationTuner hint: "cursor travel per icon; 100 = the icon pitch, lower reaches farther".
   - Relevance: 100 points = icon pitch (80 + 20) so the highlight tracks like the cursor would over the row. The motive is stated as matching the cursor exactly. **Null: the commit does not say why the hand-tuned acceleration from 5034e95 was abandoned.**

4. **100 pt/icon kept even when icons shrink.** Plan 008 glossary (`f378996`, current domain-model.md:30): "Icons are 80 pt, 20 pt apart; when that row would not fit the display, the icons and the gaps shrink by one factor so the whole row fits in one row. The highlight moves one icon for every 100 points the cursor would travel for the same finger movement, with macOS's own pointer acceleration at the user's tracking speed, whatever the icons' size on screen." Test `SwitcherOnScreenTests.thirtyRunningAppsStillTakeTwelveMillimetresPerIcon` (:130, blame `b67a5ca`).
   - Inference: after shrink, "exactly like the cursor over the row" no longer holds literally (pitch < 100 pt). The fixed per-icon travel was kept anyway.

5. **The rubber band "forgets" overshoot on turn-back (current form).** `08b2f05` (2026-10-13, feature/008) rewrote `Switcher.follow` from the 5034e95 "dropped" bookkeeping (cap at a quarter icon) to: "A frame that moves back towards the row from past an end restarts the travel at that end plus the frame's movement, so the selection changes the usual half icon after the turn however far the fingers went past the end." (Switcher.swift:39-42). Glossary at `f378996`: "The ends are hard stops with a small rubber band that adds no dead zone: the moment the fingers turn back, travel past the end is forgotten and the next icon is the usual half icon away." Test `pushingLeftPastTheFrontmostAppStretchesAndTheNextAppComesSevenMillimetresAfterTheTurn` (SwitcherOnScreenTests.swift:447, blame `b67a5ca`): after −30 mm past the left end, +5 mm keeps Safari with `stretch == 0`, and +2 mm more selects Mail.
   - The 08b2f05 commit message has no body ("The switcher shows the running apps in ⌘-Tab order"). The rewrite is folded into that commit without a stated reason, beyond the doc comment and glossary wording "adds no dead zone".

### Indirect / circumstantial

## Q6. Why the running-apps order is read at swipe begin and again at end; why the row is fixed at begin

### Direct evidence
- **Row fixed at begin since the first Switcher:** `cb7b432` Switcher.swift:3: "The switcher during one swipe. The row is fixed when the swipe begins; everything else follows the travel." (still Switcher.swift:3). `0451f0f` glossary "Frontmost app": "The active app at the moment a swipe begins..."

Lifting brings the selected app to the front with the gesture haptic; lifting on the frontmost app, or on an app that has quit, does nothing and is silent." (current domain-model.md:30, :33).
- **Tests (blame `b67a5ca`, under `// MARK: The row is fixed for the swipe`, SwitcherOnScreenTests.swift:467):**

### Indirect / circumstantial (Q6)
