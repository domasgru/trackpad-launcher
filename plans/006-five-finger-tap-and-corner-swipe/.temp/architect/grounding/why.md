# Why: the recognizer's spoil rules and `blocksClicks`, the `scrollWheel` ban, and the tap's mask

I have everything needed; no further lookups are required. All load-bearing citations from both investigators checked out against the files, commits, and PR metadata, with the exceptions noted below.

### The Question

Why does `GestureRecognizer` end a tap (`.spoiled`) on a delivered click, on the 400 ms timeout and on a 3 mm per-finger drag, and why does it own the per-trackpad `blocksClicks` flag computed from the thumb's `landedAt`? Why is `scrollWheel` banned by `SourcePolicyTests` under "R6 never scrolling", and why is the click tap's mask limited to mouse buttons, drags and pressure? The answer is a precursor to plan 006: a two-finger sideways swipe that begins after 3 mm with no time limit, survives the thumb lifting, keeps clicks blocked for its whole duration, and suppresses trackpad scrolling through the same tap.

### What We Found

**Why a tap spoils on click, timeout and drag**

- **[Direct]** The three spoil triggers implement 001's R11 exclusion list. Source: 001:167-174: "The following MUST NOT fire a gesture: a physical click …; … fingers that drag or scroll while the thumb is anchored; …". Scenario "Drag is not a tap" (001:200-202): "drags two fingers across the trackpad THEN nothing happens, and the cursor or scroll behaves as macOS normally would". The design pseudocode (001:737-738) spells out exactly today's condition: `buttonDown || frame.time - start > maxTapDuration || any remaining finger is > dragThresholdMM … → .spoiled(anchor)`.
- **[Direct]** `.spoiled` exists to void fingers already down when the thumb lands and to force a clean slate before the next tap. Source: 001-log:29: "candidate-2 ignored fingers already down when the thumb landed, so two resting fingers plus one tap would fire the 1-finger gesture (R11 'tap that began before the thumb') → candidate-1's voiding kept, encoded as the `spoiled` state." Pseudocode 001:729 ("wait for a clean slate") and 001:747 (`.spoiled(anchor): if (down minus anchor).isEmpty → .armed`). Plan 002 then promoted the recovery rule to a requirement: 002:45 "A tap longer than 400 ms MUST NOT fire, and no new tap begins until every non-thumb finger has lifted."
- **[Direct]** 3 mm and the original 300 ms were feel guesses, never tuned. Source: 001:445 non-goal "Fixed values, chosen to feel natural on common trackpads"; 001:1399 open question "Feel constants. 300 ms tap, 3 mm drag … Should these be tuned once … before they are frozen?"; 002 grounding `why.md:51` "300 ms has no recorded derivation and was never tuned". 002 explicitly kept the drag threshold out of scope: 002:236 "The anchor corner size and the drag threshold stay as they are."
- **[Direct]** 400 ms was a user choice to fix missed 3- and 4-finger taps. Source: 002:7 ("A tap counts only if the first finger landing and the last finger lifting fall within 300 ms. Fingers of a natural 3 or 4-finger tap land unevenly and often miss that window"); 002:237 "The user chose to try 400 ms first"; 002-brief:13 (user's answer: "tap limit 400 ms"); commit `3ce3f64` "S1: Forgiving taps — Count every distinct finger that touched during a tap and allow taps up to 400 ms."; PR #1 body "more forgiving taps (400 ms, every finger counts)".
- **[Direct]** A press spoils only when it was *delivered* to apps (`.click`), because the click filter's verdict is the single truth about a press. Source: 002 R8 (002:158): "While clicks are blocked (R4), a press during a tap MUST NOT cancel it … When clicks are not blocked, a press MUST cancel the tap, as today." Design decision 2 (002:376): "the recognizer's press rule shrinks to one line: `.click` cancels a tap, `.blocked` does not (R8)." Commit `0765cf4` replaced `frame.buttonDown` with `frame.press == .click` at both spoil sites.
- **[Supported]** Spoiling a tap does *not* end click blocking; blocking is deliberately a function of the anchor and the clock, not of tap success. Evidence: 002 R4 (002:66) defines blocking by thumb anchored + within 3 s + another finger down, with no reference to the tap; R8's scenario "Press and hold does nothing" (002:165-168) requires a 1 s hold (which spoils at 400 ms) to still be blocked; test `pressAndHoldOpensNothingAndBlocksThePress` (`/Users/dominykasgrubys/code/trackpad-launcher/.claude/worktrees/006-five-finger-tap-and-corner-swipe/LauncherKit/Tests/LauncherCoreTests/ClickBlockingTests.swift:33-38`) pins `[.drop, .drop]`; and `State.anchor` is defined for `.spoiled` (GestureRecognizer.swift:120-125) with the sketch at 002:559-560 computing `blocksClicks` from `state.anchor`. Investigator A offered "side effect of putting the flag on the anchor" as an alternative reading; the R8 scenario rules that out as the whole story.

**Why the recognizer owns `blocksClicks`, computed from `landedAt` on the frame clock**

- **[Direct]** Ownership follows the anchor. Source: 002 decision 1 (002:375): "*The recognizer owns the frame-side half of click blocking.* R4's predicate is a function of the anchor and the frame clock, and the recognizer is the only module that holds the anchor. … No timer: frames keep arriving while the thumb rests, so expiry is seen on the next frame (within about 10 ms), and when nothing touches, nothing is anchored and nothing is blocked."
- **[Direct]** A per-frame flag rather than a deadline, because the two clocks cannot be compared. Source: 002:247 ("stamped with a `CGEventTimestamp` that is not known to share a base with `FrameTime` (grounding Q1: never compare them)"); alternative rejected at 002:1146 ("A deadline instead of a per-frame flag … needs a clock both threads trust … Rejected"); 002 grounding `index.md:22` "decide 'blocking in effect' on the frame clock and let the tap read a decision, not a time".
- **[Direct]** Why 3 s. Source: 002:13: "Blocking has a time window so a thumb resting in the corner never disables clicking for long. 3 seconds is long enough for a few taps in a row and short enough to recover from."
- **[Direct]** Why the thumb lifting ends blocking at once. Source: 002 R4 (002:66): "Once the thumb lifts or leaves the anchor corner, clicks MUST work again at once. After the 3 seconds, clicks MUST work again even with the thumb still anchored." Test `liftingTheThumbEndsBlockingAtOnce` (`ClickBlockingRecognizerTests.swift:39-47`) pins `[false, true, false, false]`.
- **[Direct]** Why `landedAt` is optional and nil for an inherited thumb: under-blocking is the safe direction. Source: 002:375 "A reconcile cannot restart the window … The failure is under-blocking, the safe direction"; rejected alternative 002:1147; accepted tradeoff 002:1131. The rule was reversed once inside plan 002: `0765cf4` gave no window to *any* first-frame contact "whatever phase the driver reports" (002-log:24); `24993a0` ("Open the blocking window for a thumb landing on a recognizer's first frame") made `.landing` open one because "the first thumb landing after every launch, wake or grant opened no window. That broke R4, R7 and R8 on hardware" (002-log:43). The code and docs match the later rule.
- **[Direct]** Per-trackpad flag, merged at the read, reset by `run`. Source: 002:377 ("the per-trackpad flags live in each `DeviceSession`, which `run` replaces"), 002:401 ("Merging across trackpads happens at the read"); FrameStream.swift:5, 11, 60-62.
- **[Direct]** The brief asked for a "blocking in effect" *input* to the recognizer (002-brief:19); the design rejected it. Source: 002:1143: "R5's whole-press rule would live twice, in the filter and in the recognizer … Rejected: two modules owning one decision."
- **[Direct]** No timers is a standing architectural rule, not a 002 artefact. Source: 001:548 ("Time is data … no clock object or timer exists anywhere"), 001:1389 (an injected clock rejected because "a timer would be the app's only timer (R22)"), 001 R22 (001:419, 0% CPU idle), 002-log:20 ("002 revokes R20 only → timers and polling stay banned, the frame clock stays the only clock"), `docs/architecture.md:20`.

**Why `scrollWheel` is banned under "R6 never scrolling"**

- **[Direct]** The ban is the structural enforcement of 002 R6 and R9. Source: 002 R6 (002:126-127) "Pointer movement and scrolling MUST NOT be blocked"; 002 R9 (002:181) "It MUST NOT read, record or change keyboard input, scrolling, pointer movement or any other event: the only events it reads are presses and what belongs to a press"; decision 8 (002:382) "adds bans for what R6 and R9 forbid"; policy sketch 002:1085-1086 (`"R6 never scrolling": scrollWheel, ScrollWheel`); added in `b43619b` alongside the keyboard, synthesis and Input-Monitoring bans (diff verified; commit body is only the co-author line).
- **[Direct]** The *recorded* motivation for excluding scrolling is absence of a need plus a minimisation principle, not a scrolling-specific harm. Source: 002-log:11 "Mouse clicks were in scope without a stated problem → mouse clicks, pointer and scrolling are never blocked."; 002-log:12 "Accessibility use was unbounded → app uses it only to block clicks, never reads or changes other events."; 002-brief:13 (user's answer, verbatim) "block clicks only (not pointer or scroll)". The only reasoned non-goal is about the pointer: 002:233 "Freezing the pointer whenever the thumb rests in the corner would feel broken, and taps barely move it."
- **[Direct]** Scrolling during an anchored two-finger drag was an expected, checked behaviour in 001. Source: 001:1507 S5 hardware check: "during a two-finger drag with the thumb anchored the page scrolls as normal."
- **[Direct]** The pinning self-check does not pin `scrollWheel`. Source: SourcePolicyTests.swift:120-123 lists only IOHID, Input Monitoring, timer, display-link and polling tokens as "must stay banned"; 002:1099-1101 describes the same scope. The overlap half (111-119) forbids any token being both banned and confined.

**Why the mask is buttons, drags and pressure only**

- **[Direct]** The brief fixed the mask's scope and left room for pressure. Source: 002-brief:17: "The mask holds only mouse-button types (no keyboard, no scroll, no move); say how a `dragged` event during a blocked press is treated; leave room for the pressure type (34) if the Force Click manual check needs it."
- **[Direct]** Drags are read so a blocked press is withheld whole while the pointer keeps moving. Source: 002:376 ("turns that press's drags into plain moves so the pointer keeps moving (R6)"); tradeoff 002:1134 "We accept a mask that includes drags and pressure. While blocking is armed, the tap wakes for every drag and pressure change in the session … In exchange, a blocked press is withheld whole: apps never see a drag or a Force Click stage of a press they never got."; `ClickFilter.swift` `passAsMove` doc.
- **[Direct]** Pressure ships because Force Click is an R4 requirement. Source: 002 R4 (002:66) "Clicks are left-clicks, right-clicks and Force Clicks"; synthesis 002:1125 rejected "the pressure type left out of the mask until a manual check fails (the Force Click scenario is a requirement, so the mechanism ships and the check verifies it)"; `ClickFilter.swift` "A Force Click's stages belong to its press."
- **[Direct]** R9's current wording was fitted to the mask during design, and flagged for human confirmation. Source: 002-log:35: "R9's 'MUST NOT read … any other event' contradicted the tap's mask (drags, pressure) and the drag rewrite of a blocked press → R9 amended to name what belongs to a press; flagged for the human to confirm."; 002:1156 "Both amendments need the human's confirmation."
- **[Direct]** The design declares the closed vocabulary and its test discipline. Source: 002:437 "Nothing in the mask beyond buttons, drags and pressure: no keyboard, scroll or plain movement. No posting or synthesising of events."; ClickTap.swift:85-88 "a test parses one synthetic event of every listed type, so the tap is never sent a type the parser does not read … There are no keyboard, scroll or plain-move types."; `PointerEventTests` `maskCoversPressesTheirReleasesDragsAndPressureOnly` (bits pinned) and `ignoresEverythingElse` (`.scrollWheel` → nil).
- **[Direct]** Device attribution of clicks was deliberately not attempted. Source: 002:437 "No device attribution of clicks (`kCGMouseEventSubtype` is unproven for trackpad clicks …)"; 002:1156 leaves a `PressID` source field as a future option.

### Competing Hypotheses

On *why scrolling specifically* was excluded in 002, the record supports two stories and does not pick one:

- **Hypothesis A: privacy and trust minimisation.** Evidence for: 002-log:12 ("Accessibility use was unbounded → app uses it only to block clicks"), R9's "only to block clicks", `docs/architecture.md:25` "uses it only to block trackpad clicks during gestures", the 001 heritage of "No permissions" (001 R20, `4552ceb` banning `tapCreate` under "permission"). Evidence against or missing: nothing in 002 says scroll *reading* is a privacy concern in particular; the keyboard bans carry that weight explicitly ("never reads keyboard events").
- **Hypothesis B: no problem to solve.** Evidence for: 002-log:11 (scrolling was "in scope without a stated problem"), the brief's one-line answer "block clicks only (not pointer or scroll)" (002-brief:13), 001:1507 expecting normal scrolling during an anchored drag. Evidence against or missing: the non-goal (002:233) does give a *reason* for the pointer half, so the author was willing to reason about harm; the absence of a scrolling reason may be an omission rather than a judgement.

Both are compatible with 006 now adding scroll suppression: the need has appeared (006:17, 006-index F33: thumb plus two fingers produces ordinary scrolling and nothing else), and R9's minimisation can be re-scoped by amendment. Neither hypothesis implies that suppressing scrolling was ever judged harmful.

[Direct requirement; design open]
- **`State` must admit a swipe with no required `Anchor`** and anchor maintenance (lines 35-38) must not reset a swipe when the thumb lifts, leaves the corner or re-lands (006 R8, R9; F9 (iii)). - **`blocksClicks` (lines 78-83) gains a second source:** true for the whole swipe regardless of `landedAt`, the 3 s window and the thumb (006 R13; F9 (iv)). [Direct]

- **`PointerEventTests`:** `ignoresEverythingElse` drops `.scrollWheel`; `maskCoversPressesTheirReleasesDragsAndPressureOnly` adds bit 22; `everyMaskedTypeParses` covers the new case. [Direct]
- **Requirement text and docs that the change contradicts:** `docs/domain-model.md:69` "Pointer movement and scrolling are never blocked" and *Blocking window*; `docs/architecture.md:25` "uses it only to block trackpad clicks during gestures" (a cross-cutting rule; 006 task item 8); the ban label "R6 never scrolling"; the `tapTypes` comment "There are no keyboard, scroll or plain-move types"; `ClickFilter`'s "Everything the click tap is ever told about". 006:21 names the glossary entries but not the architecture sentence.
