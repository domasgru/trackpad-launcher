## Direct Evidence Found

### Q1. Why `beginOrSpoil` spoils a pair that drags more up/down than sideways; where 3 mm and "more sideways than up or down" come from; why the farthest-moved finger

**1a. The 3 mm number originates in plan 001 as a tap drag rule, an untuned feel constant.**
- `plans/001-trackpad-launcher-app/plan.md`, Design › "LauncherCore: gesture domain and recognizer" (line 642): `static let dragThresholdMM = 3.0                         // a finger farther than this from its landing point is dragging`. Recognizer pseudocode (line 738): "any remaining finger is > dragThresholdMM (surface.distanceMM) from its landing point → state = .spoiled(anchor)".
- Requirement it serves: 001 R11 (line 173), "The following MUST NOT fire a gesture: … fingers that drag or scroll while the thumb is anchored".
- 001 Non-goals (line ~446): "**Tunable anchor corner, tap timing or finger-count rules.** Fixed values, chosen to feel natural on common trackpads."
- 001 "Open questions and risks" (line 1399): "**Feel constants.** 300 ms tap, 3 mm drag, 2 mm anchor release margin, … Should these be tuned once on a 13" MacBook and a Magic Trackpad before they are frozen…?"
- 002 Non-goals: "**Tuning other gesture rules.** The anchor corner size and the drag threshold stay as they are."
- 006 Non-goals (line 476): "**Tunable step distance, icon size or thresholds.** 12 mm, 80 pt and 3 mm are fixed, like the other gesture rules."
- Relevance: the 3 mm is plan 001's tap-drag threshold reused by fiat; no plan records a measurement behind it.
- Prior synthesis (006 grounding `why.md`, "What We Don't Know"): "No derivation of 3 mm, 2 mm, 3 s, 300 ms or 400 ms beyond feel/user choice."

**1b. The swipe was placed exactly where the drag rule spoiled; "otherwise it is spoiled as today".**
- 006 Design › Problem (line ~491): the swipe "breaks three assumptions the recognizer was built on: … a movement past 3 mm spoils the tap (F9 ii)."
- 006 Design decision 1 "Begin" (line 634): "The drag check in `.tapping` becomes the swipe's entry. When the farthest-moved finger passes 3 mm, the tap becomes `.swiping` if swipes are on, the tap is a `Pair` (exactly two touched, both down, none joining on this frame) and that finger's displacement is more sideways than vertical (`|dx| > |dy|` in mm); otherwise it is spoiled as today. The drag check runs before the 400 ms check, so a slow pair that moves on the frame it crosses 400 ms still begins (R3 "no time limit")."
- Decision 1 "No time limit" (line 635): "`.resting` … becomes `.swiping` on a sideways first move past 3 mm and is spoiled by a vertical one."
- 006 Synthesis decision (line 1317): both candidates converged on "the swipe as two more states of the one recognizer … begun exactly where the drag rule spoiled".
- 006 dev log, Implement: "`.tapping`/`.resting` begin-or-spoil branch extracted" (the origin of the name `beginOrSpoil`; it was a review refactor, not a behaviour change).

**1c. The requirement itself mandates "spoil until all non-thumb fingers lift" on a vertical first move.**
- 006 R3 (line 59): "A swipe MUST begin when all of these hold: … one of the two fingers has moved more than 3 mm from where it landed, with that movement more sideways than up or down. There MUST be no time limit between the fingers landing and the movement. When the first movement past 3 mm is more up or down than sideways, no swipe begins and nothing further happens until every non-thumb finger has lifted. Two fingers that lift without moving past 3 mm remain a 2-finger tap."
- 006 R3 scenario "Up or down is not a swipe": "**WHEN** the user anchors the thumb and slides two fingers 10 mm downwards, then 20 mm to the right without lifting **THEN** no switcher appears and nothing opens". This scenario explicitly rules out "letting it become a swipe later".
- 006 Non-goals (line 470): "**Vertical swipes.** Up and down with two fingers is scrolling; the switcher is a sideways gesture only."
- 006 dev log Specify: "Missing non-goals → vertical swipes, quitting or hiding from the switcher, a cap on list length, window previews." (the vertical-swipe non-goal was added in review).
- 006 `.temp/architect/task.md`, the human's original brief (verbatim): "When user hold a thumb on left top corner, and start swiping with 2 fingers left or right we show the row of app icons …".
- 006 rubric (`.temp/architect/rubric.md` line 7): "a vertical first movement spoils until every non-thumb finger lifts".
- Tests that pin it: 006 T8 "(a) `thumb.land(fingers: 2).slide(byMM: (0, 10)).slide(byMM: (20, 0)).lift().tap(fingers: 2)` → phases == [], fired == [.two] (the trailing tap only). (b) Parametrised d ∈ {(3.0, 2.9), (2.9, 3.0)} … → phases.first == .began for the first, phases == [] for the second" with source "the boundary rows straddle abs(dx) > abs(dy) by 0.1 mm at the frame the drag passes 3 mm"; T11(b) the same for a resting pair; T6 "`GestureRules.dragThresholdMM` is strictly greater, rows 0.1 mm either side".
- Relevance: the "spoil, not ignore, not later-swipe" behaviour is a stated requirement plus a non-goal ("up and down … is scrolling").

**1d. Vertical two-finger movement under the thumb is ordinary scrolling, which the plans chose to keep.**
- 006 grounding `index.md` F33: "on the hardware, a thumb anchored plus two fingers sliding produces ordinary two-finger scrolling (horizontal and vertical) and nothing else; no Spaces or page-swipe gesture fires."
- 006 R12 scenario "Vertical movement inside the window does not scroll": "**WHEN** the user anchors the thumb and, within 3 seconds, slides two fingers up and down **THEN** the page does not scroll and no switcher appears". R12 scenario "Scrolling returns after 3 seconds": rest 4 s, scroll up → "the page scrolls".
- 006 Solution: "Vertical scrolling is suppressed inside that window too: one rule for clicks and scrolling, so the user learns a single 3-second window." (dev log Specify: "Vertical suppression inside the window had no rationale → one sentence in the Solution: one rule for clicks and scrolling.")
- 001 Verified without a test, R11 on hardware (line 1507): "during a two-finger drag with the thumb anchored the page scrolls as normal".

**1e. Why the farthest-moved finger: determinism, and the requirement names one finger's movement.**
- 006 Design sketch, `drag(of:in:on:)` doc (line 841): "The farthest-moved finger's displacement, when it is more than `dragThresholdMM`. Deterministic: the maximum."
- 006 Synthesis decision: candidate 1 was right on "a `Pair` constructed as exactly two with "none joining on this frame" and a deterministic farthest-finger mover (candidate 2 iterated a dictionary and missed a third finger landing on the begin frame)". Dev log Plan, cross-judge: "candidate 2's defects confirmed on the code: … a dictionary-ordered mover and a third finger missed on the begin frame".
- `Move.isSideways` doc in the sketch: "More sideways than up or down (R3)." with `abs(dxMM) > abs(dyMM)`.
- R3 text: "one of the two fingers has moved more than 3 mm … with that movement more sideways than up or down" — the requirement judges a single finger's displacement, not the midpoint.

**1f. Later plans changed the recognizer around the drag check without changing the rule.**
- 009 Implementation Decisions 3: "**One contact book replaces three scattered records (a prefactor).** … The anchor's landing time, the tap's landing points and the pair's origins read from it instead of carrying copies, and the drag check reads origins from it. Behaviour is unchanged" (origin of the `book` parameter in `drag(of:in:book:on:)`).
- 009 decision 2 reuses `dragThresholdMM` for the thumb tap: "A candidate is spoiled … on any frame where it is more than 3 mm from its landing point (`dragThresholdMM`)".
- 007 dev log Plan: "The R3 check said "grep for the number", which cannot work since `dragThresholdMM` is also 3.0" (the edge band is a separate 3 mm constant, `edgeBandMM`).

### Q2. Why the Accessibility APIs are confined to `SystemAccessibilityPermission.swift`; one permission used only for click blocking; earlier decisions on permissions, prompting and privacy

**2a. Plan 001: no permissions, no network, no logging, no own files, as product requirements.**
- 001 Problem statement (line 9): "A launcher only earns a place in the menu bar if it costs nothing to keep. One that asks for Accessibility, phones home, logs, or burns CPU while idle gets uninstalled."
- 001 R20 (line 401): "Trackpad Launcher MUST work without asking the user for any system permission (no Accessibility, Input Monitoring or other prompt)."
- 001 R21 (line 408): "Trackpad Launcher MUST make no network connection of any kind. It MUST write nothing to disk of its own except its settings (assignments, hand mode, whether it has launched before); files macOS writes on every app's behalf do not count. It MUST NOT write log messages of its own, to the system log or to any file."
- 001 R12: "If some part of this cannot be done without a permission prompt, R20 wins and that part is dropped." Dev log Specify: "R12 "restore its windows" was ambiguous, had no other-desktop case, and could conflict with R20 (no permissions) → R12 restated as "exactly what clicking the Dock icon does", R20 wins on conflict".
- 001 Non-goals: "**Working with *Tap to click* on.** The app requires it off instead of fighting the system's own clicks; this keeps the app free of permissions." and "**Gestures beyond the four, or actions beyond "bring to front"** (URLs, scripts, window layouts)."
- 001 Design decision 9 (line 556): "*Rules are enforced by structure.* `LauncherCore` cannot import AppKit, IOKit or the private framework. `SourcePolicyTests` scans every source file for logging, network, timer, own-file-write and permission APIs and fails on any hit." The 001 sketch bans `AXIsProcessTrusted`, `tapCreate`, `CGEventTapCreate`, `IOHIDManager`, `CGRequestListenEventAccess`, `CGPreflightListenEventAccess` under "R20".
- 001 "Deliberately not done" (line 605): "No key-event monitors; the only global monitor is mouse-down and exists only while the window is open."
- 001 dev log Specify: "Problem statement did not motivate privacy, permissions, idle cost → paragraph added." Prior synthesis (002 grounding `why.md`): "The no-permission rule's provenance is the plan's specification step, not a user-stated requirement." (second-hand claim; the 001 dev log line supports that the motivating paragraph was added in review.)

**2b. Plan 002 replaced R20 with exactly one optional permission, scoped to click blocking.**
- 002 Problem/Solution: "Trackpad Launcher asks for the Accessibility permission on first launch. With it, clicks are blocked for the first 3 seconds after the thumb lands in the anchor corner. … Without it, gestures work as before". "Its R20 "No permissions" is replaced by R9 here."
- 002 R9 (line 180): "**Accessibility is the only permission, and it is optional.** Trackpad Launcher MUST NOT ask for any permission other than Accessibility. Every feature except click blocking MUST work without it. With access granted, the app MUST use it only to block clicks as described here. It MUST NOT read, record or change keyboard input, scrolling, pointer movement or any other event: the only events it reads are presses and what belongs to a press (its release, its drags and its Force Click pressure stages), and the only events it changes are those of a blocked press."
- 002 dev log Specify: "Accessibility use was unbounded → app uses it only to block clicks, never reads or changes other events." and "Mouse clicks were in scope without a stated problem → mouse clicks, pointer and scrolling are never blocked."
- 002 `.temp/architect/task.md`, the human's follow-up answers: "Accessibility permission optional, asked on first launch, hint in the menu bar window otherwise; block clicks only (not pointer or scroll)".
- 002 dev log Plan, grounding: "an active mouse-button event tap returns nil from an untrusted process and a listen-only one is created disabled (probed) → Accessibility is the permission the tap needs; the design keeps the permission optional and the tap confined to one adapter."
- 002 dev log Plan, grounding (`why`): "the press rule, the button-state sampling and the permission and timer bans were one package for 001's R20 and R22; 002 revokes R20 only → timers and polling stay banned".

**2c. The R7/R9 in `SourcePolicyTests.confined` are plan 002's R7 and R9.**
- 002 SourcePolicyTests sketch (lines 1080–1101), verbatim:
  - "Removed: "AXIsProcessTrusted", "tapCreate", "CGEventTapCreate" (plan 002 R9 replaces 001 R20; now confined)."
  - "Kept and added … "R9 only Accessibility": IOHIDManager, CGRequestListenEventAccess, CGPreflightListenEventAccess, IOHIDRequestAccess, IOHIDCheckAccess, CGRequestPostEventAccess, CGPreflightPostEventAccess; "R9 no keyboard": keyDown, keyUp, KeyDown, KeyUp, flagsChanged, FlagsChanged; "R6 never scrolling": scrollWheel, ScrollWheel; "R9 never synthesise": .post(tap:, CGEventPost, postToPid"
  - "/// Each permission API lives in exactly one file, the adapter that owns it. Any other hit fails." followed by the four `Confined` rows identical to today's (`R9 one permission, one adapter`, `R7 the trust push`, `R9 the one event tap` ×2).
  - "/// Pins both halves of the plan 002 edit so neither drifts back: no confined token is also banned, and the kept IOHID, Input Monitoring, timer and polling tokens are still banned."
- 002 R7 is "**Blocking follows the permission live.** Click blocking MUST start within 5 seconds of access being granted and stop within 5 seconds of it being revoked, with no relaunch." (hence `notify_register` = "the trust push").
- 002 Design decision 8: "*Rules stay structure.* The scanner stops banning the two Accessibility APIs the design uses, confines each to the single file that owns it, adds bans for what R6 and R9 forbid, and pins the kept tokens with a self-check."
- 002 Synthesis decision: candidate-1 won also "on the stronger policy mechanism (confining each permission API to one file)".
- 002 `task.md` item 7: "Revise `SourcePolicyTests.banned`: remove only the permission tokens the design uses (`AXIsProcessTrusted`, `tapCreate`, `CGEventTapCreate`), keep `IOHIDManager` and the Input Monitoring tokens … `CGEvent`, `CFMachPort`, notify tokens and AX calls stay in `LauncherPlatform`".
- 002 Slices S2 checklist: "`SourcePolicyTests` fails on any Accessibility API outside `SystemAccessibilityPermission.swift`." T48: "`AXIsProcessTrusted` and `notify_register` only in `SystemAccessibilityPermission.swift`; `tapCreate` and `CGEventTapCreate` only in `ClickTap.swift`".

**2d. The architecture sentence ("one permission … only to block clicks") was written by plan 002.**
- 002 "Docs changed in Phase D" (line 1115): "the sentence "The app asks for no permission." becomes: "The app asks for one permission, Accessibility, and uses it only to block trackpad clicks during gestures. It never asks for Input Monitoring or any other permission, never reads keyboard events, and works fully without it except click blocking. The Accessibility APIs and the event tap are each confined to one adapter file by the source-scan test.""
- 006 amended it: Docs: "Privacy, Accessibility is used "only to block trackpad clicks and suppress trackpad scrolling during gestures", with the scroll-event API confined to the tap's file like the tap itself." 006 Solution amendments: "R9's "MUST NOT read, record or change … scrolling" is amended by R12 to R14 here (scroll events are read, and those of a suppressed scroll are changed, under the one Accessibility permission)". 006 Tradeoffs: "We accept that the tap wakes for every scroll event in the session while armed (F19), in exchange for one tap and no second permission."
- 006 Design decision 12: "*The gate moves the token; it does not weaken it (F17).* `scrollWheel` leaves `banned` and becomes `Confined(token: "scrollWheel", onlyIn: "ClickTap.swift")`".
- 008 decision 2 extended confinement to a private API: "The source-scan test confines the two private symbol names to the LaunchServices order's file." and the LaunchServices read was chosen to need "no link flag, no entitlement, no permission; the app is not sandboxed".

**2e. Prompting rules.**
- 002 R10: "On first launch Trackpad Launcher MUST show the system's Accessibility prompt once, unless access is already granted. … Later launches MUST NOT show the prompt." Non-goal: "**Prompting installs that already launched an earlier build.** The prompt appears only on first launch. Such installs see only the hint."
- 002 decision 6: prompt once from the derived first-launch fact; window held open under the prompt ("Without the hold, R10 fails as soon as the prompt appears"). Alternatives: "**A stored "prompted" flag.** A second boolean that must stay in sync with first launch"; "**Polling `AXIsProcessTrusted`** … Polling is banned".
- 002 R11 hint: "The menu bar icon MUST NOT change because of the permission." Shape: "`isAccessibilityGranted` … is not part of `GestureActivity`, so the icon cannot change because of it (R11)."
- 002 decision 5: "*Permission is one more push-only port; the adapter removes the noise.* `AccessibilityPermission` has `onChange` …, `isGranted()` (a fresh `AXIsProcessTrusted()`) and `prompt()`. The real adapter listens to `com.apple.tcc.access.changed`, re-reads trust on every broadcast and calls `onChange` only when the value changed, so another app's camera grant does not restart the devices".

**2f. "Works without the permission" was repeated for every later gesture.**
- 006 R12: "Without the Accessibility permission, nothing is suppressed and the swipe still works." Scenario "Without access, the page scrolls under the switcher".
- 009 dev log Specify: "No Accessibility permission was unspecified → R9: the swap still works, nothing is blocked." 009 decision 2: "R9 wants the swap without one, so unlike a tap gesture a thumb tap is never cancelled by a click."

**2g. Windows were explicitly out of scope twice; previews out of scope once.**
- 008 Non-goals (line 251): "**Windows instead of apps.** ⌘-Tab switches apps, not windows; so does the switcher. Picking a specific window is a different feature."
- 009 Non-goals (line 287): "**Swapping windows within one app (like ⌘-`).** The swap is app-level, like ⌘-Tab."
- 006 Non-goals (line 483): "**Running or hidden indicators, or window previews, in the switcher.** Just icons and the selected name."
- 001 R12 restores minimised windows through Dock-click semantics ("unhide it, restore its minimised windows, open a new window if it has none, switch to the desktop its window is on") with "If some part of this cannot be done without a permission prompt, R20 wins"; 008 R7 keeps that ("as clicking the app's Dock icon would").

### Q3. Why travel is cursor points through the pointer curve, 100 per icon; half-icon switching; rubber band that forgets overshoot

**3a. Originally millimetres, by requirement.**
- 006 R6: "During a swipe the icons are 12 mm of travel apart. Travel is the sideways movement of the midpoint of the two fingers, measured from where the swipe began. … Travel is measured in trackpad millimetres, the same on every trackpad, and the direction is the same in both hand modes: right moves the selection right."
- 006 decision 4: "*Travel crosses the port and the selection is decided on main (O2; F11).* The recognizer knows millimetres and nothing about rows. The row depends on the frontmost app and on the catalog, main-actor facts, so the selection belongs to the pure `Switcher` in core". Alternative rejected: "**The recognizer told the row length and emitting the selection (O2).** Exposes a main-to-frame channel mid-gesture … the 12 mm rule could only be tested through frames."

**3b. Changed to cursor points with macOS's pointer acceleration at the user's request, after hardware use (recorded only in the 006 dev log and an amended non-goal).**
- 006 dev log, Implement: "User request after hardware testing: far icons were hard to reach within the trackpad's width → swipe acceleration. The recognizer accumulates travel per frame as finger movement × gain(speed), with the midpoint's speed smoothed over 30 ms; gain eases from 1× below 40 mm/s to 3× above 200 mm/s (`SwipeAcceleration.shipped`). … Because acceleration made overshoot past the row's ends pile up, the switcher now keeps at most a quarter icon of travel past either end and drops the rest, so turning back moves the selection at once. … Amends the plan's non-goal on flick or momentum."
- 006 dev log, Implement (next bullet): "User request: swipe speed should match the cursor's, at the user's tracking speed if possible → the fixed 1×–3× curve was not that, so it is replaced by macOS's own pointer acceleration, reproduced from IOHIDFamily (`IOHIDParametricAcceleration`, `IOHIDPointerAccelerator`): the trackpad driver's `HIDAccelCurves`, 400 counts per inch, a 120 Hz report rate, the 67 Hz standardisation and 96/67 cursor scale, interpolated at the tracking speed (`com.apple.trackpad.scaling`, default 0.6875 when never set). Travel is cursor points, 100 per icon (the icon pitch). The tracking speed has no push (KVO on its dotted global key and the defaults-changed notification were both probed and never fire for an external write), so it is read at each swipe start: a read on a user event, not a poll."
- 006 Non-goals, amended (line 468): "**Momentum in the switcher.** The selection follows the fingers only and stops when they stop. (Amended after review, at the user's request: switcher travel now follows the cursor, with macOS's pointer acceleration at the user's tracking speed and 100 cursor points per icon, so R6's fixed 12 mm per icon no longer holds and a fast swipe travels farther than a slow one. R8 and R9 are amended too: a swipe ends when both fingers have lifted, a finger that briefly loses contact rejoins, and only a third finger landing while both are down cancels.)"
- 008 Non-goals: "**Reaching the far end of a very long row with a slow slide.** It may take a fast slide, since the switcher follows the cursor's pointer acceleration. There is no lift-and-reposition within one swipe, because lifting both fingers ends the swipe."
- 008 decision 10: "The core's travel (12 mm, or 100 cursor points, per icon) never sees the layout, so the finger travel per icon is unchanged by construction."
- Relevance: the "why" is two user requests (reach far icons; match the cursor). "100 per icon" is stated as "the icon pitch" (80 pt icon + 20 pt gap, 006 R10). The tracking-speed read at swipe start is justified by the no-polling rule.

**3c. Half-icon switching (nearest icon).**
- 006 R6: "The selected app MUST be the icon the highlight is nearest to, so the selection changes when the highlight crosses the midpoint between two icons: 6 mm after the swipe began, then every 12 mm. Reversing direction MUST move the selection back under the same rule."
- 006 dev log Specify: "R6 read as two selection rules (step every 12 mm vs nearest icon) → rewritten: icons are 12 mm apart, the nearest wins, so the selection changes 6 mm in and every 12 mm after".
- The human's brief (006 `task.md`): "we should indicate the currently selected icon during the swipe, but also kinda indicate the swipe progress, so you can feel how much you need to swipe to other icons, and in general feel that swiping responsive."
- 006 decision 4: "`selected = clamp(home + round(travel / 12 mm))`, rounding half away from zero, so it changes at ±6 mm and then every 12 mm, reversibly". Testing "Readings": "R6 "6 mm after the swipe began": at exactly 6.0 mm the selection has changed (half away from zero), at 5.9 it has not."
- 008 R3 scenario "Lift without moving an icon": "swipes right less than half an icon and lifts **THEN** nothing happens and no haptic plays".

**3d. Rubber band, and forgetting overshoot.**
- 006 R6: "The selection MUST NOT move past the first or last icon; travel beyond an end moves the highlight only slightly past it, and it springs back to the end icon." Non-goal: "**Wrapping from the last icon to the first.** The ends are hard stops with a small rubber band, so a long swipe always parks on the end icon."
- 006 Synthesis: "the rubber band's high-water mark and `switcherPushStepMM` are dropped: `stretch` is a pure saturating function of the raw excess, and the presenter re-adds a keyed additive spring on each change".
- 006 dev log Implement (acceleration bullet): "the switcher now keeps at most a quarter icon of travel past either end and drops the rest, so turning back moves the selection at once."
- 008 R4: "Turning back right MUST move the selection after the usual travel for one icon. The rubber band MUST NOT add a dead zone." 008 decision 3: "`follow` keeps at most a quarter icon (3 mm) of travel past either end for the rubber band, as today, and forgives it the moment the fingers turn back … so the next selection change comes the usual 6 mm from the turn, however far the fingers went past the end … That is R4's "no extra travel to undo the stretch", applied at both ends, and it replaces plan 006's rule, under which 3 mm of the way back undid the band first."
- 008 Further Notes "R4 is read literally, for the owner to confirm": "Today's rubber band keeps a quarter icon (3 mm) of travel past either end, and 3 mm of the way back undoes it before the selection can move: a dead zone, which R4 forbids … "the usual travel for one icon" is read as the 6 mm at which the selection changes from rest (half an icon), not the 12 mm pitch." 008 dev log Plan: "T10 quoted R4's "no extra travel to undo the stretch" while expecting 9 mm, 3 of which undid the band → R4 read literally: the band is forgiven on the turn at both ends, 6 mm from the turn".

### Q4. Why the switcher lives in `LauncherUI` while the launch overlay lives in `LauncherPlatform`; `NSGlassEffectView` in an `NSPanel` rather than SwiftUI `glassEffect`; icons drawn off-main before any swipe

**4a. Launch overlay: a stateless effect behind `SystemActions`, so platform.**
- 004 "The port: two methods on `SystemActions`" (line 213/224): "No new port. The animation is one more effect the core decides and macOS performs, which is what `SystemActions` already describes."
- 004 "The overlay (LauncherPlatform)": "`LaunchOverlay` is internal, created and owned by `WorkspaceActions`, which forwards both port methods to it."
- 004 dev log Plan: "Four public LauncherCore types plus two custom geometry values existed only for the overlay → the flight moved into LauncherPlatform as internal types using CG and Core Animation types directly, tested through `@testable import`."

**4b. Switcher: model state rendered by the shell, so UI.**

### Q5. "Every port has exactly two adapters"; async-delivering things behind a port, temp-dir/UserDefaults-pointable things concrete

**5a. Origin of the two-adapter rule.**

- 002 Design Problem (line 247), constraints carried over from plan 001: "every port has exactly two adapters; policy is pure and lives in `LauncherCore`, which imports Foundation and Observation only".

**5c. Why async deliverers go behind a port (003, building on 001).**

### Q6. Running-apps order read at begin and end; row fixed at begin; silence rules

**6a. Two reads, never stored.**
- 008 decision 1: "The read is a pull at the moment it is needed (a swipe's begin and end), never stored: the order changes with every activation and LaunchServices is its one source of truth."
- 008 decision 4 (line 276): "**The swipe in the model reads the running apps twice and never restarts recognition for a null row.** … On `ended` with a switcher up: index 0 is silent and leaves the window alone; otherwise a second `runningApps()` read decides whether the selected entry's location is still among the running apps (the frontmost or the list); if so, the gesture haptic plays, the app is brought to front … if not, nothing happens and nothing plays, so an app that quit mid-swipe is never relaunched. The row itself is a value fixed at `began`, so an app that launches mid-swipe never appears in it."
- 008 Further Notes: "**Cost on the swipe path after this plan:** one LaunchServices read (1.4 ms warm, under 4 ms cold, for 14 apps) and cache hits for icons; a second read at the lift."

**6b. Row fixed at begin (requirement).**
- 006 R4: "The row is fixed for the swipe from the moment it begins."
- 008 R5: "The row MUST be decided when the swipe begins and MUST NOT change during the swipe. An app that launches during the swipe MUST NOT appear in it. An app that quits during the swipe MUST stay in the row; lifting on it MUST do nothing, with no haptic, and MUST NOT launch it again."
- 010 R6: "The row MUST use the assignments in place when the swipe begins." 010 design: "Nothing is stored: there is no cached set and no new field on the model".
- No plan states a reason for fixing the row beyond these requirement sentences (see Gaps).

**6c. Silence rules.**
- Frontmost: 006 R8: "When the selected app is the frontmost app, nothing happens except that the switcher disappears: no haptic, no bring to front." ## Indirect / Circumstantial Evidence

- **A swipe state suppresses scrolling for its whole length, so keeping vertical out of the swipe keeps vertical scrolling usable.** 006 R13: clicks and scrolling are blocked "from the moment a swipe begins until it ends or is cancelled, however long after the thumb anchored". 006 decision 3 gives a parallel reason for the empty-list gate: "Without this gate, every user with an empty list (every upgrading user) would find an anchored thumb plus a sideways two-finger scroll suppressed for its whole length, past the 3 s window". Inference chain: if a vertical first move began a swipe-like state, vertical scrolling would likewise be suppressed past 3 s. Alternative reading: the plans never say this about vertical movement; the stated reason for the vertical rule is only the non-goal "Up and down with two fingers is scrolling" and the user's "left or right" brief.
- **`.spoiled` is the existing "clean slate" state, so reusing it gives "nothing until all fingers lift" for free.** 001 pseudocode: "fingers already down when the thumb lands … never fire: wait for a clean slate"; 001 dev log "candidate-1's voiding kept, encoded as the `spoiled` state". 002 R2 (via 002 S1): "lets no new tap begin while a spoiled tap's fingers are still down". Suggests vertical-spoil reuses a designed recovery path. Prior synthesis (006 grounding `why.md`, Avoid list) warned against "Reusing `.spoiled` to carry a swipe or its aftermath" — about the swipe's exits, not the vertical spoil.
- **Spoil keeps the blocking window on the 3 s rule.** 006 decision 2: "`blocksClicks` is `true` in `.swiping` … and follows the unchanged R4 expression in every other state." A spoiled pair therefore blocks/suppresses only under the 3 s rule. Inference: a vertical spoil returns the user to normal clicking/scrolling after 3 s.
- **"No main-to-frame channel" is a repeated design value.** 006 "Deliberately not done": "No main-to-frame channel: the recognizer is never told the row." 006 decision 3: R11 "holds with no main-to-frame channel". Suggests any new gesture whose eligibility depends on main-actor facts (e.g. how many windows) would be decided on main, not on the frame thread.
- **Permission-free mechanisms were preferred whenever one existed.** 001 `anyMouseButtonDown()` doc: "a state query, no tap, no TCC"; 003 Spotlight: "It needs no permission for a non-sandboxed app reading these folders"; 008 LaunchServices: "no link flag, no entitlement, no permission". Pattern, not a stated rule beyond 002 R9.
- **Readiness-before-gesture is a recurring requirement shape.** 004 (launch icon prepared), 006 decision 9, 008 decision 9 and S3 all move first-use costs off the gesture path, each backed by a probe (45–120 ms, 220.9 ms, 25 ms per app).
- **The model reads system state as a pull at a user event, never a poll.** 006 dev log on tracking speed "a read on a user event, not a poll"; 008 decision 1 "a pull at the moment it is needed"; 001/002/006 ban timers and polling (`SourcePolicyTests`). Suggests any per-swipe system read (e.g. a window list) follows the same pull-at-begin pattern. Inference only.
- **006 open question about where travel starts.** 006 Open questions: "R6 measures travel from where the swipe began, so with fingers moving together the first selection change comes about 9 mm after the fingers landed (3 mm to begin, then 6 mm). Should travel instead count from the landing midpoint?" No later plan answers it.
- **006 risk on uncapped blocking.** 006 Open questions: "*Risk:* a swipe's block has no cap. Its exits are a lift, a foreign landing, a delivered click, or a `run`." Prior synthesis (006 grounding `why.md`, Risk): "002 chose every ambiguity so that failure is *under*-blocking … A swipe-wide block with no cap inverts that".
