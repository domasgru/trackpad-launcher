# S1: Prefactor: the launch watch owns its clock; the bounce only draws

Work only in `/Users/dominykasgrubys/code/trackpad-launcher/.claude/worktrees/032-hide-background-apps`. Do not commit: the orchestrator commits.

## Goal

Nothing the user sees changes. A launch bounce plays, ends and times out exactly as today, but its cap, its 2 s limit and its 50 ms re-look waits become deadlines on the watch's own clock layer in a never-shown window, not on the bounce's slot. `BounceOverlay` only draws. A launch with no display to bounce on runs its watch to the end. Later slices need this so a launch can be watched with no bounce at all.

## Requirements

None directly (prefactor). It delivers in advance the clock of:
- R10: a launch is watched until the launched app shows a window.
- R11: "A launch that fails, or that shows no window within the time limit, MUST hide nothing. The limit is reached when the launched app shows no window within 10 seconds, or within 2 seconds of finishing launching, whichever comes first."

## Design

All in `LauncherKit/Sources/LauncherPlatform/` unless named. Today's shape: `BounceOverlay` holds, per bounce, a zero-size `clock` layer, `deadlines`, a `ranOut` closure, `after(_:in:_:)`, `limit(_:to:)`, `runOut`, `deadline(on:after:passed:)` and the nested `Deadline` class; `LaunchWatch` takes a `wait` closure that calls `BounceOverlay.after`.

### New: `LaunchClock.swift`, an internal platform type

- `@MainActor final class LaunchClock`, one per watched launch. It holds one zero-size `CALayer` in a shared host panel.
- `after(_ wait: Duration, _ passed: @escaping @MainActor () -> Void)`: adds a deadline. `stop()`: cancels every deadline (flag first), removes the layer, and closes the host when this was the last clock. Idempotent. After `stop()`, `after` does nothing.
- The host is one borderless, non-activating, one-point `NSPanel`, never ordered in, `canHide = false`, `isReleasedWhenClosed = false`, `hidesOnDeactivate = false`, `animationBehavior = .none`, content view `wantsLayer = true` set before reading its layer, made with the first clock and closed when the last one stops. It is shared main-actor static state. Do not reuse `LaunchOverlay.makePanel`: it is an on-screen panel's factory.
- Expose an internal read for T14 (for example `static var hostPanel: NSPanel?`) so a test can see the panel is never visible.
- A deadline is a 1→1 opacity `CABasicAnimation` on the clock's layer with `duration = wait.seconds`, whose delegate's `animationDidStop` is the push. Add it inside an explicit `CATransaction.begin/commit` with actions disabled, as `BounceOverlay.limit` does today: the process may have no `NSApplication`. Each key is unique (a counter).
- Move `BounceOverlay.Deadline` to `LaunchClock.Deadline` with its rule unchanged: a cancelled one reports nothing; a stop the system causes (`finished == false`, not cancelled) counts as passed, so no launch can outlive its cap. Move the `Duration.seconds` extension to this file too.
- Measured facts: a deadline on such a layer passes on time, about 90 ms late, with and without `NSApplication`, and while the app is hidden. A detached layer's animation stops at once with `finished == false`, so a window is needed. A removed sublayer's animation stops early with `finished == false`, so cancel before removing.
- A passed deadline can be dropped from the clock's list; keep nothing past `stop()`.

### `LaunchWatch.swift`

- `init(report:)`; the `wait` closure goes. The watch makes and owns one `LaunchClock`.
- `func start(cap: Duration)`: the cap deadline, reporting `.ranOut`. `func limit(to: Duration)`: from now on the watch runs out within that at the latest, never later than its cap or an earlier limit. No-op once stopped.
- Both deadlines report `.ranOut` at most once between them ("reports each fact at most once" stays true), and nothing after `stop()`.
- `relookLater` waits on the same clock.
- `stop()` stops the clock too. `isolated deinit { stop() }` stays. Starting the clock must not need a pid: a watch never given a pid still runs out.
- Rewrite the type's doc comment and the "R9's 50 ms" neighbours only where they say "the bounce's clock": it is the watch's own clock now.

### `BounceOverlay.swift`

- Loses `Bouncing.clock`, `Bouncing.deadlines`, `Bouncing.ranOut`, `deadlineCount`, `limit`, `after`, `runOut`, `deadline(on:…)`, `Deadline`.
- `start(_ app: AppEntry)` has no cap and no closure. When layout or the stage fails it simply returns with no bounce, and the watch carries on. `end` is a no-op for an app with no bounce (it is already).
- `#if DEBUG preview` starts with no deadline and runs until the tuner calls `stopPreview`, as the tuner already does on toggle and disappearance. The restart-in-place path stays, without the deadline cancel.
- Update the type's doc comment: time is not here.

### `WorkspaceActions.swift`

- `bringToFrontBouncing` keeps today's port signature (S4 renames it): guard one per app, create `LaunchWatch(report:)`, store it, `open(app, then: watch.opened)` first, then `watch.start(cap: cap)`, then `bounces.start(app)`.
- `limitLaunchBounce(of:to:)` calls `launches[app]?.watch.limit(to: limit)`.
- `endLaunchBounce` still removes the launch, stops the watch and ends the bounce.
- Every report still passes `LaunchReportGate` through `report(_:of:)`, deadlines included.

### `LauncherCore` text only

## Rules that apply

- `docs/architecture.md`, Concurrency: "No timers, no polling, no background loops… Time passes only on Core Animation's clock… a deadline is an animation on a layer of the effect it belongs to, its completion is the push, and it cannot outlive that effect. A launch watch's deadlines (its cap, its wait after the app finished launching, the interval between its window-list re-reads) are on a zero-size layer of the watch's own, in a one-point window that is never ordered in, made with the first watch and closed with the last; the layer goes when the watch ends."
- Same section: "Animation is Core Animation keyframes… Whatever an animation leaves behind is cleaned up from its completion callback. No code of ours runs per display frame, and display links are banned along with timers."
- Same section: "`LauncherCore` and `LauncherPlatform` are nonisolated by default; the model, the ports and the public surface of every real adapter are `@MainActor`."
- `docs/architecture.md`, Shape: "`LauncherCore` … imports Foundation and Observation only"; "Framework and wire types never cross a port."
- `docs/domain-model.md`, *Launch bounce*: "No bounce lasts longer than 10 seconds, and a window that shows after a bounce ended plays nothing."
- `docs/domain-model.md`, *Launch watch*: "Its time passes on the render server's clock, like the bounce's, so no watch outlasts its 10 seconds by more than a fraction of a second."
- The source scan (`LauncherCoreTests/SourcePolicyTests.swift`) bans `nonisolated(unsafe)` and timers, and confines `AXUIElement` and `CGWindowList` to `FrontmostAppWindows.swift` and `LaunchWatch.swift`. Keep those in `LaunchWatch.swift`.
- The repo logs nothing: no `print`, `NSLog`, `os_log`, `Logger`.
