# Rubric: 5-finger gesture and the swipe switcher

What success looks like for this task: a design a maintainer can extend without breaking the frame path's invariants, in which the swipe, the fifth gesture, scroll suppression, the switcher and the window edits each land in the module that already owns that knowledge, every R1–R24 scenario is traceable through the usage and the sketch to one synchronous test seam, and the handful of platform facts the design rests on are named with their probe or source rather than assumed. The candidates see only the task; this rubric is the picker's tool. Score each criterion 0–3 (0 absent or wrong, 1 present with a defect that would surface in implementation, 2 sound, 3 sound and simpler than the alternative).

## C1. One frame-path state machine carries taps and the swipe

The recognizer (or a sibling on the same frame thread) encodes in its state type that a tap needs the anchor and a swipe does not; the swipe begins exactly where the drag rule used to spoil, a two-finger rest has no time limit while a tap keeps 400 ms, a vertical first movement spoils until every non-thumb finger lifts, a third finger or a delivered click cancels and a blocked press does not, the thumb may lift and re-land (and a re-landed thumb is a correct anchor afterwards), and the frame-side blocking flag is true for the whole swipe. No timer or second clock. Each R1, R3, R8, R9, R13 scenario can be read off `step`'s pseudocode. Defect examples: an optional anchor beside a phase flag; a swipe that dies with the anchor; the 400 ms spoil still ending a two-finger rest; a cancel that forgets "nothing further until every finger lifts".

## C2. Clicks and scrolling share one rule, through the existing tap, with the gate updated structurally

Scroll suppression reads the same per-frame flag as click blocking (no deadline, no clock comparison), lives in the one event tap, exempts wheel mice by the probed field, suppresses the momentum of a suppressed scroll and leaves earlier momentum alone with a stated memory rule that works although the fingers have lifted when momentum begins, and does nothing without the permission. The decision drop-vs-zeroed-deltas is made and justified. `SourcePolicyTests` moves tokens from banned to confined (with the overlap self-check in mind) and `PointerEventTests` is amended; `CGEvent` and its fields stay in `LauncherPlatform`. Defect examples: a second tap; comparing `CGEventTimestamp` with `FrameTime`; a swipe-wide block signalled through a new channel instead of the existing flag; weakening the gate.

## C3. The model owns the decisions; the seams are synchronous and complete

`Launcher` (or a value it owns) builds the row, starts the selection on the frontmost app, maps travel to selection and highlight through a pure function (12 mm, 6 mm, hard stops with overshoot), decides the tick, the end effects (front, haptic, no animation, window close, silent no-op on the frontmost) and the cancel. The frontmost app and the switcher's presentation sit behind a port or observable state that an in-memory adapter or a test can drive and read synchronously; the hardware fake still delivers inline. Every R4–R11 scenario and R23 (including an old record) is nameable at the `World` seam with a concrete handle; the swipe script primitives `TouchScript` lacks are named. Defect examples: selection math in the view; the frontmost read inside the recognizer or on the frame thread; a test that must wait on a hop.

## C4. Interface depth and reader load

The public surface grows by few members (count them: port methods, model properties and intents, event cases) and hides the row-building, selection math, icon preparation, panel configuration and menu shape behind them. No pass-through methods; no framework types across ports; the path from a frame to a highlight on screen is traceable in at most three modules; the switcher's transient state has one writer. Prefer the candidate whose model, port and view responsibilities match the launch overlay's precedent or improve on it with a reason. Defect examples: the view assembling the row from `installedApps`; a `SystemActions` method per switcher visual change; a second copy of the "missing app" rule.

## C5. The window, the menu and persistence reuse what exists and stay correct

The fifth row flows from `Gesture.allCases`; the switcher section's rows, remove (hover and context menu), drag reorder and empty state are specified against the existing SwiftUI layout; the "Add app…" menu is a second pure shape of `InstalledApps` with a disabled state, built lazily like the picker and reusing the icon cache; the window's height is capped by the visible frame with the list scrolling inside and a refit when the list changes (R22); `Settings` gains the fifth assignment and the ordered list with a decoder that loads today's record; the hint copies are pure values. Usage and type sketch agree. Defect examples: a menu built on the click path from disk; the window still sized by the full fitting height; synthesized `Codable` on the new shape; hint copy only in the view.

## C6. Platform honesty and risk handling

Every platform claim cites its probe or source (ordering of hops, icon timing, scroll event shape, `frontmostApplication`, the tolerant decoder, the panel configuration), the design picks the documented or structurally safer mechanism where the cost is nil, and the open questions name what only a human or hardware can settle (frontmost nil or Trackpad Launcher itself, drag reorder in a non-activating key panel, the feel of the tick and the spring, Reduce motion) instead of papering over them. Defect examples: "SwiftUI handles it"; an icon drawn on the main actor at swipe begin without the probe's numbers; an unstated assumption that momentum events carry the blocking condition.
