# S1: A press held through a swipe no longer moves the pointer

Repo root (worktree): `/Users/dominykasgrubys/code/trackpad-launcher/.claude/worktrees/020-swipe-click-cursor-drift`. Swift package: `LauncherKit/`. Gate: `Scripts/test.sh` (runs `swift test` in `LauncherKit` with Xcode's developer dir). Do not commit; the orchestrator commits your work. Stay on the current branch.

## Goal

Pressing the trackpad by accident while starting a swipe or a window swipe leaves the pointer where it is: the click tap drops the held press's drags for as long as the swipe lasts, the press stays blocked whole, and the swipe runs exactly as without the press. Everywhere else a withheld press's drags still move the pointer. The level, the filter, its wiring, the comments say so, and the temporary reproduction test is gone.

### The bug, for context

A confirmed swipe or window swipe blocks clicks (`Blocking.clicks`), so the click tap withholds the press. While a press is withheld, its finger movement reaches the tap as drag events, and `ClickFilter.decide` (`LauncherKit/Sources/LauncherCore/ClickFilter.swift`) turns every drag of a withheld press into a plain pointer move (`PointerVerdict.passAsMove`). That rewrite was chosen (an earlier plan's requirement "pointer movement MUST NOT be blocked") for presses during the tap's blocking window, before swipes existed. So the drags of a press held during a swipe move the pointer.

Reproduction (temporary, untracked, delete it in this slice): `LauncherKit/Tests/LauncherCoreTests/ReproPressedSwipeTests.swift`. It plays the thumb at (10, 10), two fingers landing, a press 20 ms later, then a 3.5 mm and a 12 mm slide; the switcher (or window switcher) shows and `hardware.offer(.pointer(.drag(button: 0)))` reads `.decided(.pointer(.passAsMove))`. Read it for the World / TouchScript idiom, then delete it; T1 and T2 replace it.

## Requirements

### R1. A press's drags do not move the pointer during a swipe or window swipe
While a confirmed swipe or window swipe is under way on any running trackpad, the click tap MUST drop the drags of a withheld press, so the pointer does not move with the fingers. The press itself MUST stay blocked whole, as today (its down, release and Force Click stages are dropped), and the swipe MUST behave as it does without the press: same selection, same travel, same lift.

- Scenario (swipe): GIVEN Accessibility is granted, the swipe setting is on and several apps are running; WHEN the thumb rests in the anchor corner, two fingers land, the trackpad is pressed 20 ms later and held, and the fingers slide 3.5 mm and then 12 mm sideways; THEN the switcher shows with its selection moved as without the press, and a drag of the held press is dropped instead of passed as a pointer move.
- Scenario (window swipe): GIVEN Accessibility is granted and the frontmost app has two visible windows; WHEN the same hand slides down 3.5 mm and then 12 mm with the press held; THEN the window switcher shows, and a drag of the held press is dropped.

### R2. Outside a swipe, a withheld press's drags still move the pointer
Whenever no confirmed swipe or window swipe is under way on any trackpad (the tap's 3 s blocking window, a slide awaiting the model's window-swipe answer, a swap series, or after the swipe ended while the press is still held), the drags of a withheld press MUST still pass as plain pointer moves. Drags of a passed press, and drags with nothing held, MUST still pass untouched.

### R3. The tap's gating is unchanged
The gated port MUST open and close exactly as today; this change adds no event type to either port's mask and posts no events.

### Non-goals (do not build)
- Moving the pointer back or decoupling it from the mouse (`CGWarpMouseCursorPosition`, `CGAssociateMouseAndMouseCursorPosition`).
- Freezing the pointer for a press in the blocking window or a swap series, including the first 3 mm of the slide before its direction is decided.
- Unblocking the accidental press.

## Design

### `Blocking` (`LauncherKit/Sources/LauncherCore/GestureRecognizer.swift`)

One more rung above `.clicks`, named `.pressDrags` (not `.drags`: in the recognizer a "drag" is a finger's displacement past 3 mm, `drag(of:)`, `dragThresholdMM`; this rung is about the pointer drag events of a press). A confirmed swipe or window swipe reads `.pressDrags`; the blocking window and a swap series keep reading `.clicks`. `.pressDrags` blocks everything `.clicks` blocks, and in addition the drags of a withheld press are dropped instead of passed as plain pointer moves.

- `blocksClicks` becomes `self >= .clicks` (today `self == .clicks`), so every reader of `blocksClicks` (the scroll filter's flag, `Recognition.blocksClicks`, the session's `blocksClicks`) treats `.pressDrags` as `.clicks`.
- New `public var blocksPressDrags: Bool { self == .pressDrags }`.
- `wantsTap` and `blocksDockSwipes` untouched.
- The ordering chain is by construction: `blocksPressDrags ⇒ blocksClicks ⇒ blocksDockSwipes ⇒ wantsTap`. If there is an ordering comment on `Blocking` or nearby, it gains that first link and reads exactly `blocksPressDrags ⇒ blocksClicks ⇒ blocksDockSwipes ⇒ wantsTap`.

Why a level and not a second flag: `TapContext.blocking` is the merged maximum of every running trackpad's level, merged by one `max()` in the real adapter and the in-memory one; the tap thread reads that one value afresh on every event. A level needs no new merge, and because `.pressDrags` includes `.clicks` the merge stays right across trackpads (window open on one, swipe on the other, merge to `.pressDrags`).

Why the verdict reads the level current at each drag rather than latching at press time: in the reported hand the press lands 20 ms after the fingers, inside the blocking window, before the slide crosses 3 mm, so the press is withheld by the window and the swipe confirms later; R1 wants its drags dropped from then on, and R2 wants them moving the pointer again once the swipe has ended with the press still held. Only a per-drag read gives both. Do not add a latch.

### The recognizer

Sets `.pressDrags` exactly where it sets `.clicks` for a confirmed slide today, in the state-level computation at the end of `step`: a swiping state that is confirmed reads `.pressDrags`. The swap-series branch stays `.clicks`, the thumb rules stay `.clicks` / `.dockSwipes` / `.possible`, the thumb-touch floor and the final `max` are unchanged, and the state machine and its events are untouched. Consequences, all following from today's shape:
- a swipe (sideways, confirmed as it begins) reads `.pressDrags` from its `.began` frame;
- a window swipe reads by the thumb rules on its `.began` frame and `.pressDrags` from the frame after the model's confirmation until the frame both fingers have lifted, whose level is that of the state the slide falls into (the thumb alone, `.possible`; a thumb with a third finger inside the window, `.clicks`);
- a declined or unanswered window swipe never reads `.pressDrags`.

### `ClickFilter.decide` (`LauncherKit/Sources/LauncherCore/ClickFilter.swift`)

Signature becomes `decide(_ event: PointerEvent, trackpadBlocking: Bool, trackpadBlocksDrags: Bool) -> PointerVerdict`. The parameter is required, not defaulted, so every call site states what it reads.
- A press is withheld iff `trackpadBlocking`, as today.
- A release follows its press, both flags ignored, as today.
- A drag of a withheld press is `.drop` when `trackpadBlocksDrags` is true, `.passAsMove` otherwise.
- A drag of a passed press, or with nothing held on that button, `.pass`, as today.
- Pressure and `Holding` unchanged (so the frame thread's press sampling `TouchFrame.Press.blocked` is unchanged; a blocked press cancels neither the swipe nor the tap).

Why two Bools and not the level: each filter (`ScrollFilter`, `DockSwipeFilter`, `ClickFilter`) takes exactly the Bools derived from the merged level that it needs and knows nothing of the ordering; `TapFilters` is the one place that derives them. Keep it so.

### `TapFilters.decide` (`LauncherKit/Sources/LauncherCore/TapFilters.swift`)

Passes `context.blocking.blocksPressDrags` as the click filter's `trackpadBlocksDrags` for pointer events; nothing else in it changes. The gate predicate, `follow`, `settle` and `portsWereDisabled` are untouched. A dropped drag changes no gate state (its decision carries `gate: nil`, like any verdict on an event already in flight); port masks untouched; nothing posted.

### No adapter changes

The session (`LauncherPlatform`, `DeviceSession`) stores and merges `Blocking` as it does; the frame thread's wake condition stays "`wantsTap` flipped" (`.clicks` ↔ `.pressDrags` flips nothing). `TapContext.estimated(from:)` (a scroll may be under way from `.dockSwipes` up) holds for `.pressDrags` with no change. The in-memory trackpads merge by `max()`, unchanged. Do not touch `TapEvent.swift` (`ridesGatedPort`) or `LauncherPlatform/ClickTap.swift` (`pressTypes`, `gatedTypes`). If the compiler flags an exhaustive `switch` over `Blocking` anywhere, handle `.pressDrags` the way `.clicks` is handled there.
