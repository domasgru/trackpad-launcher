## Goal

The *Hide background apps* switch sits below the thumb corner row, off by default and kept across relaunches and login starts. With it on, a tap on a running app, a swipe lift, a swap and a window swipe lift that changes the front window hide every other running app with a Dock icon, Finder included, on every display and Space, the app the user came from once the target has come to the front. Every silent action hides nothing; flipping the switch hides and shows nothing; a gesture whose app has no process brings it to front and hides nothing. Trackpad Launcher's own settings window is not hidden yet (S3), and a launched app's window hides nothing yet (S4).

## Requirements

- R1: "The settings window MUST show a *Hide background apps* switch in its third group, directly below the thumb corner row."
- R2: "The setting MUST be off on a fresh install and for a user upgrading from a version without it."
- R3: "The app MUST keep the user's choice across a relaunch and a login start."
- R4: "Flipping the switch MUST NOT hide or show any app. It only affects later actions."
- R5: "While the setting is on, a tap that brings its target app to the front MUST hide everything else. This holds when the target app is already in front." A tap with no assignment, or whose app is no longer installed, hides nothing.
- R6: "While the setting is on, a swipe lift that brings an app to the front MUST hide everything else."
- R7: "While the setting is on, each swap MUST hide everything else, including the app it replaced." In a swap series: after the first swap Mail is visible and Safari hidden; after the second Safari is visible and Mail hidden.
- R8: "While the setting is on, a window swipe lift that puts a different window in front MUST hide every app except the frontmost app. The frontmost app's own windows stay as the lift left them." Windows coming forward as the fingers pass them hide nothing until the lift.
- R9: "While the setting is on, an action MUST NOT hide anything when it brings nothing to the front and changes no window. This holds for every silent swipe, window swipe and thumb tap."
- R13: "Hiding MUST cover the same apps as ⌘⌥H: every other running app with a Dock icon, on every display and Space, Finder included. A full-screen app follows what ⌘⌥H does to it. Menu bar apps stay as they are. The Desktop's icons stay."
- R18: "Hidden apps MUST stay in the switcher with their look unchanged. They MUST keep their relative order."
- R19: "A swap MUST treat a hidden app like any other app."
- R20: "Bringing a hidden app to the front MUST show it."
- R21: "Turning the setting on or hiding apps MUST NOT trigger a permission prompt."

## Design

### Settings record (`LauncherCore/Settings.swift`)

`public var isHideBackgroundAppsOn = false`, the key being the field's name permanently, read in `init(from:)` with `decodeIfPresent … ?? isHideBackgroundAppsOn` like its siblings. Absent reads as off, so every record written so far keeps today's behaviour; an undecodable value fails the whole record, as for every field.

### Model (`LauncherCore/Launcher.swift`)

- `public var isHideBackgroundAppsOn: Bool`; `public func setHideBackgroundApps(on: Bool)`: guard unchanged, set, `store.save(settings)`, and re-run nothing (no `reconcile`): no recognizer reads it, and flipping it hides and shows nothing.
- One private helper that hides when the setting is on: `system.hideOtherApps(than: app.bundleID)`. The model never reads hidden-ness.
- Hide calls, each after the guard that silences its action, and after its bring-to-front:
  - `fire`: after the bring-to-front and, when it plays, after `playLaunchAnimation`, so the hide never delays either. Only for an app that has a process.
  - The swipe's `.ended`: after `system.bringToFront(open.selectedApp)`, keeping the selected app.
  - `swap`: after `system.bringToFront(target)`, keeping the target.
  - The window swipe's `.ended`: only when `lift.playsHaptic`, after `system.raiseWindows(lift.raises)`, keeping the window switcher's app (`WindowSwitcher.Content.app`; surface it from `WindowSwitcher` as you need). The lift keeps using `raiseWindows`, never `bringToFront`. `playsHaptic` is defined as "a different window in front, or a minimised one restored", which is R8's condition. Never hide in `.moved` or `.cancelled`.
- `fire` and `hasProcess`: today `visual(for:)` reads `hasProcess` only with the animation on. Now read it when the launch animation setting or the hide setting is on; with both off it is not read, as today. A gesture whose app has no process brings it to front with no immediate hide (animation on: the bounce as today; animation off: plain `bringToFront`). A gesture for an app whose bounce still plays keeps the one bounce and hides nothing. Keep "the silent cases decided only here" in `fire`'s guard. Reshape `Visual` as you need; it stays private.

### Port (`LauncherCore/Ports.swift`, `SystemActions`)

One new member, `func hideOtherApps(than app: BundleID)`, with this contract as its doc text: hides every running app with a Dock icon whose bundle identifier differs from `app` (every copy of `app` stays), on every display and Space, full-screen apps included; menu bar apps stay. Idempotent; needs no permission; returns at once. The app frontmost at the call, when it is not `app`, is hidden only once `app` has come to the front; the others go out on the next main-actor turn.

### Adapter (`LauncherPlatform/WorkspaceActions.swift`)

The `.hide(` call lives in this one file. Keep this as a small private part of the file (deep behind the one port member), not a new public surface.

- The set: `NSWorkspace.shared.runningApplications` where `activationPolicy == .regular` (exactly the set ⌘⌥H covers: Finder in, menu bar apps out), not terminated, `bundleIdentifier != app.rawValue`, and not this process (Trackpad Launcher itself comes in S3). An app with a nil bundle identifier differs, so it hides.
- Do not read `isHidden` first: it costs as much as the call. About 0.75 ms per visible app and 0.25 ms per hidden one; the enumeration is 0.02 ms.
- **Every call first drops any pending deferred hide** (see below).
- **Frontmost deferral.** Read `NSWorkspace.shared.frontmostApplication`. When it is in the set, it is not hidden now: it becomes the pending hide, hidden when `app` activates. Hiding the frontmost app at once makes macOS activate the next visible app, and in the common race that app lands second in the ⌘-Tab order above the app the user came from, which would send the next swap to a third app (measured: a same-turn hide put a third app second in three series steps of four; hidden after the target's activation, the previous app stayed second in every step).
- The pending hide waits on `NSWorkspace.shared.notificationCenter`'s `didActivateApplicationNotification`, read through `NSWorkspace.applicationUserInfoKey`. On `app`'s activation: hide the pending app, forget it. On any other app's activation: forget it, hiding nothing. It is forgotten also by the next `hideOtherApps` call and once fired. It has no deadline. **Its observer exists only while a hide is pending**, so nothing runs otherwise; remove it in all three cases. Mind Swift 6 sending rules: extract the bundle identifier from the notification and hop on the main queue, as the file's other callbacks do; hold the running app only on the main actor.
- When `app` is already frontmost (a tap on the app in front, a window swipe lift) nothing is deferred.
- **The immediate hides go out on the next main-actor turn** (`Task { @MainActor … }`), after the gesture's own turn has committed the bring-to-front and the launch animation's first frame. The gesture's own turn pays only the enumeration and the frontmost read. A rapid second swap before the first's activation lands drops the first's pending hide, and the kept app's own late open unhides it, so the app the series started from can end visible behind the front app; the series still alternates. That is accepted.

### Settings view (`LauncherUI/SettingsView.swift`)

The third section holds `ThumbCornerRow` and then a `Toggle("Hide background apps", isOn: Binding(get: { launcher.isHideBackgroundAppsOn }, set: { launcher.setHideBackgroundApps(on: $0) })).toggleStyle(.switch)`, as *Animate app launch* is written. Update `SettingsView`'s doc comment. The window's height rule is untouched: the switch always shows, and the window's size comes from `contentFittingSize`.

### Fake (`Tests/LauncherCoreTests/InMemoryAdapters.swift`, `World.swift`)

`RecordingSystemActions` gains:
- `hidesOthers: [BundleID]`: every hide, by the app it kept, in order.
- `hiddenApps: Set<BundleID>`: which apps are hidden now, computed in `hideOtherApps` as every ⌘-Tab app and the frontmost app but the kept one, added at once (the adapter's deferral and its next-turn hop are its own mechanics), and cleared for an app by any bring-to-front (`bringToFront` and `bringToFrontBouncing`).
- `onHideOtherApps: (() -> Void)?`: run inside the hide, like `onPlayLaunchAnimation`, so a row can see what was already recorded.

`World` exposes them as other rows reach the fake (`world.system.hidesOthers`). Fold `hidesOthers.isEmpty` into both `nothingHappened` helpers (`SwapTests.swift`, `SwapSettingTests.swift`).

- `docs/domain-model.md`, *Hide background apps setting* (the code must match it): "One global setting, off by default, shown as the *Hide background apps* switch in the settings window's thumb corner group, directly below the thumb corner row. While it is on, every action that brings an app or a window to the front, a tap on a running app (the app already in front included), a swipe lift, a swap, and a window swipe lift that leaves a different window in front, also hides every other running app with a Dock icon, as Hide Others (⌘⌥H) does: on every display and Space, full-screen apps and Finder included (the Desktop's icons stay) …; menu bar apps stay as they are, and the frontmost app's own windows stay as the action left them. The app the user came from is hidden once the app brought forward is in front, so the ⌘-Tab order keeps it next and a swap series alternates as before. A silent action hides nothing. … Hiding needs no permission. Hidden apps stay in the switcher and the swap in their place, and bringing one to the front shows it. Flipping the switch hides and shows nothing: it applies to the next action and is saved at once, so it survives a quit, a force-quit or a Mac restart. - `docs/domain-model.md`, *Bring to front*: "A hidden app is brought to front like any other: it shows again."
- `docs/domain-model.md`, *Feedback*: haptics are unchanged by this slice.
- Hidden apps need no handling in the model: LaunchServices' ⌘-Tab order lists hidden apps in their place; `bringToFront` (an `openApplication` with activation) unhides.
