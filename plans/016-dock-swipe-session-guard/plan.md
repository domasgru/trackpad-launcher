# Plan: The system's swipes stay blocked for the whole touch under a thumb gesture

## Problem statement

The human reports that, rarely, while switching apps (thumb in the corner, two fingers sliding sideways) or windows (two fingers sliding up or down), macOS's own three-finger swipe goes through: the desktop switches, or App Exposé opens. macOS counts every contact on the pad, so thumb plus two sliding fingers reads as its three-finger swipe; the app is meant to drop that Dock swipe for as long as the gesture lasts.

Root cause, from reading origin/main `bc56781` (not reproduced live: the human judged it too timing-dependent to reproduce on purpose, so the scenarios below reproduce it at the recognizer and filter seams instead).

1. Dock swipe blocking follows the recognizer's idea of an anchored thumb, not the contacts macOS counts. In `GestureRecognizer.step`, the level is `.dockSwipes` or more only for a confirmed slide, a swap series, or `state.thumb` plus another contact down. The thumb stops counting while it is still on the pad in several ways:
   - It rolls more than `anchorReleaseMarginMM` (2 mm) past the corner zone, which a hand dragging two fingers easily does. It then becomes a rest finger and the level drops to `.none`. `SwapRecognizerTests.aThumbThatRollsOutOfTheCornerIsARestFingerAndTheNextLandingIsJudgedAnew` asserts exactly this, so the gap is codified, not merely untested.
   - During an unconfirmed window swipe (awaiting the main actor's answer) only the thumb rule blocks, so a rolled thumb leaves the level at `.possible`: a vertical three-contact slide, which is App Exposé's swipe, passes.
   - A confirmed slide whose thumb was lost ends into `.rest` of the contacts still down, level `.none`. Lifting and sliding again to keep switching is a three-contact slide with nothing blocking it.
   - A thumb that blips (loses contact for a frame) and comes back inside the 2 mm margin but outside the strict corner zone lands as a finger, not a thumb.
2. `DockSwipeFilter` judges a Dock swipe on its first event and keeps the verdict to its end. A `mayBegin` that arrives while not blocking latches `.pass`, so the `began` after it passes even if blocking has turned on in between. Nothing visible has happened at `mayBegin`, so dropping from `began` would still keep the swipe whole in the Dock's view (the existing `TODO(D2)` in `DockSwipeFilter.resume`).

Once any of these lets a Dock swipe's opening through, the filter keeps passing that whole swipe by design, which is why one bad frame is enough.

## Requirements

### R1. Dock swipes stay blocked until the pad empties after a thumb gesture
Once a thumb has been in a trackpad's anchor corner (anchored, or landed under resting fingers), or a swipe, window swipe or swap series has started there, that trackpad's blocking level MUST be at least `.dockSwipes` on every frame with two or more contacts down, and at least `.possible` on every frame with one contact down, until a frame where no contact is down. This MUST hold whatever happens to the thumb meanwhile: rolling out of the corner, lifting, or landing again anywhere. Click blocking (`.clicks`) MUST NOT change: it is still decided exactly as today. Contacts the recognizer already ignores (edge contacts) do not count as contacts down.

#### Scenario: The thumb rolls out of the corner during an app switch and the fingers slide again
- GIVEN a thumb anchored in the corner and two fingers that started a swipe
- WHEN the thumb rolls 30 mm out of the corner, the two fingers lift, land again and slide sideways while the thumb stays on the pad
- THEN every frame with two or more contacts down blocks Dock swipes, and every frame with one wants the tap, from the thumb's anchoring until the frame the pad empties

#### Scenario: The thumb rolls out of the corner while a window swipe awaits its answer
- GIVEN a thumb anchored in the corner and two fingers that just began a window swipe, with no answer from the main actor yet
- WHEN the thumb rolls out of the corner before the answer arrives
- THEN those frames block Dock swipes

#### Scenario: A thumb blip lands again in the release margin
- GIVEN a thumb anchored in the corner with two fingers down
- WHEN the thumb loses contact for one frame and comes back 1 mm outside the corner zone, inside the release margin
- THEN every frame blocks Dock swipes until the pad empties

#### Scenario: Ordinary use is unaffected
- GIVEN no thumb has anchored since the pad last emptied
- WHEN three fingers land outside the corner and swipe
- THEN no frame blocks Dock swipes and the level is `.none`, as today

### R2. A Dock swipe is judged on its `began`, not on a passed `mayBegin`
`DockSwipeFilter` MUST judge a Dock swipe again on `began` when its `mayBegin` passed: if blocking is on at `began`, that event and the rest of the swipe MUST be dropped. A swipe whose `mayBegin` was dropped MUST stay dropped whole, a swipe that passed its `began` MUST still pass whole, and a swipe first seen mid-way MUST still pass, as today.

#### Scenario: Blocking turns on between `mayBegin` and `began`
- GIVEN a Dock swipe whose `mayBegin` arrived while not blocking
- WHEN its `began` arrives while blocking
- THEN `mayBegin` passed, and `began`, every `changed` and the `ended` are dropped

### R3. The tap's gate keeps its invariant
The gated port MUST stay open whenever R1 or R2 can drop a Dock swipe, and MUST close once the pad is empty and nothing is withheld, so ordinary trackpad use still routes no high-volume events through the app (plan 014 R1).

### R4. Docs match the code
`docs/architecture.md` and the doc comments on `Blocking`, `Recognition.blocksDockSwipes` and `DockSwipeFilter` MUST describe the new rules. The tests that assert the old rule (a rolled-out thumb with fingers down does not block Dock swipes) MUST be updated to the new one, keeping their click-blocking assertions unchanged.

## Non-goals

- A live end-to-end reproduction: the human asked not to attempt one, because the leak depends on millimetres of thumb drift and frame timing.
- The event tap's gate-opening latency when a thumb and fingers land on the same frame, and Dock swipes passing while the system has the tap disabled for a timeout: both judged unlikely causes, left as they are.
- Widening the corner or the 2 mm release margin: that would change what anchors and fires gestures, not only what is blocked.
- Blocking clicks or scrolling for longer: only Dock swipes need the longer guard, because only they count the thumb as a finger.

## Implementation Decisions

- **One domain term carries R1: the *thumb touch*.** The stretch of frames from one with a thumb in the anchor corner (anchored, or landed under resting fingers) until the first frame with no contact on the trackpad, edge contacts aside. Every gesture, swipe, window swipe and swap series lies inside one, and what the thumb does inside it changes nothing about its length. Dock swipe blocking becomes a rule over the thumb touch: blocked on every frame of it with two or more contacts down. `docs/domain-model.md` gains *Thumb touch* and *Dock swipe blocking* entries, and *Click blocking* and *Swipe setting* point to them.

- **Cause 1 is fixed inside `GestureRecognizer`, behind its existing interface.** `step` keeps returning one `Recognition` with one `Blocking` level; nothing outside the recognizer (the `DeviceSession`, `mergedBlocking`, `TapContext.estimated(from:)`, the in-memory trackpads) learns anything. The recognizer gains one private piece of memory, whether a thumb touch is on, kept beside the state machine rather than inside it. After today's rules have produced a frame's level, the thumb touch is on iff some contact is down (the frame is already edge-filtered, so edge contacts never count) and either it was on before the frame or today's level is at least `.possible`. That condition is exactly R1's trigger: today's rules give `.possible` or more precisely when a thumb is anchored (every state carrying an anchor, or a thumb under a rest), a slide is on (confirmed or not) or a swap series is on, and each of those begins with a thumb admitted in the corner. The frame's level is today's level raised to `.dockSwipes` while the thumb touch is on with two or more contacts down, and to `.possible` with one. Raising never produces `.clicks`, so `blocksClicks` is unchanged by construction, which is R1's "click blocking MUST NOT change". The thumb touch ends on the first frame with no contact down, which today's rules already make `.none`. The state machine itself is untouched: a rolled thumb is still a rest finger whose next landing is judged anew, swaps, taps and slides are decided as today.

- **Why the level is raised beside the state machine and not by keeping the thumb.** Keeping a rolled or lifted thumb as "the thumb" would change what fires: the domain model says a thumb that rolled out is a finger until it lifts and lands again, and the swap and tap tests pin that. Counting contacts in the tap thread is impossible: the tap sees events, not contacts; the recognizer is the only place that counts contacts, and the level is already the one value the tap reads. A new `Blocking` case was considered and rejected: `.possible` and `.dockSwipes` already mean "the gated port must be receiving" and "the system's swipes are blocked", which is all the thumb touch adds.

- **`Blocking`'s doc comments gain the thumb touch.** `.possible`: a thumb anchored alone, a slide awaiting its confirmation, or one contact down in a thumb touch. `.dockSwipes`: a thumb with another contact down (its 3 s over or its landing unknown), or two or more contacts down in a thumb touch. `Recognition.blocksDockSwipes`'s comment is restated the same way. The ordering, `wantsTap`, `blocksDockSwipes` and `blocksClicks` are unchanged.

- **Cause 2 is fixed inside `DockSwipeFilter`: each opening event is judged on its own, and only a drop is remembered.** The filter's state becomes one Bool, a drop under way (`isDropping` is that stored value). `decide`: while dropping, every phase is `.drop`, and `.ended` or `.cancelled` end the drop; otherwise `.mayBegin` and `.began` are each judged on `trackpadBlocking` as they arrive (`.drop` starts a drop), and `.changed`, `.ended` and `.cancelled` pass. So a passed `mayBegin` commits nothing and a `began` under blocking drops the rest of the swipe (R2's scenario); a swipe whose `mayBegin` was dropped stays dropped whole; a swipe that passed its `began` passes whole because nothing is dropping; a swipe first seen mid-way passes, as today. `forgetSwipe()` clears the drop. A `.pass` memory no longer exists because nothing reads it under the new rule, so `resume()` and its `TODO(D2)` are deleted: the question D2 deferred (whether a `began` after a no-warning open should pass to keep the Dock whole) is answered by R2 and the problem statement: nothing visible has happened at `mayBegin`, so a `began` is judged on the level whether or not its `mayBegin` was seen. `TapFilters.settle` stops resuming Dock filters when the gate opens; its predicate, routing and notice handling are unchanged. This decides the policy half of plan 014's open question 1 (D2); its empirical half, whether real Dock swipes send a `mayBegin` before their `began`, is still unobserved (plan 014's M2 (8) was not run). If they do, R2's case leaves the Dock a passed `mayBegin` with no `ended`, the lone warning plan 014 worried about. That is accepted: the same lone warning already exists in the no-warning-open race, and the problem statement judges that nothing visible has happened at `mayBegin`; the optional hardware check in Further Notes is where it would show.

- **The gate (R3) needs no code change; its invariant is kept by the two fixes.** `TapFilters`'s predicate stays `wantsTap || a press withheld || a scroll suppressed || a Dock swipe dropping`. R1 keeps `wantsTap` true on every frame of a thumb touch with a contact down, so the gated port is receiving on every frame where R1 can drop; R2 starts a drop from `began`, which holds the port open until that swipe ends. One observable shift follows from R1 and is accepted: when the thumb lifts while a finger stays down, the port now stays open until the pad empties, where today it closed once nothing was withheld. The cost is the gated events of a touch a thumb already took part in, bounded by that touch; an ordinary touch never opens the port, and the reopen estimate (`TapContext.estimated(from:)`) is unaffected: a thumb touch cannot start without an opening today's rules already make, and the only openings that disappear are today's mid-touch reopenings (the thumb lifting or rolling out with fingers still down, then the level rising again), which is harmless because the scroll filter then sees the gesture from its start instead of resuming to `.unseen`, and reaches the same verdict.

- **Existing tests that assert the old rule are updated, click-blocking assertions kept (R4).** Five test files change, and no `@Test` body outside the rows named here is edited. Edited bodies: `SwapRecognizerTests.aThumbThatRollsOutOfTheCornerIsARestFingerAndTheNextLandingIsJudgedAnew` (T5: the rolled-thumb stretch now blocks Dock swipes while two or more contacts are down and wants the tap on every frame until the thumb lifts; its click-blocking and firing assertions stay); the three `BlockingLevelRecognizerTests` rows `aThumbRolledOutOfTheCornerIsNone`, `anUnconfirmedWindowSwipeWhoseThumbLiftedIsPossible` and `aDeclinedWindowSwipeWithTheThumbGoneIsNone` (T11, renamed to the levels they now read: `.possible`, `.dockSwipes`, `.dockSwipes`); `TapGateTests.aPressStaysWithheldThroughTheThumbsLiftUntilTheButtonIsUp` (T10: the gate now closes on the empty frame, not on the release with a finger still down). Moved or removed with `DockSwipeFilter.resume()`: `FilterResumeTests.aPassingDockSwipeIsForgottenOnResume` becomes a resume-free row of `DockSwipeFilterTests` (T6 (d): a passed swipe whose end was never seen does not pass the next one under the flag, the property plan 014's T16 (a) pinned, now held by construction), `aDroppedDockSwipeSurvivesResume` is deleted (there is nothing to resume), and `aDockSwipeIsDroppingOnlyFromADroppedOpeningToItsEnd` moves to `DockSwipeFilterTests` unchanged. New rows: T1 to T4 in `BlockingLevelRecognizerTests`, T6 in `DockSwipeFilterTests`, T7 to T9 in `TapGateTests`. `DockSwipeBlockingRecognizerTests` is untouched. Every other row in the suite stays as it is: those rows are the proof that clicks, scrolling, swaps, taps and slides are decided exactly as today. One of them, `TapGateRecoveryTests.twoTrackpadsMergeToTheHighestLevelAndTheGateFollowsTheOneThatStillWantsIt`, keeps passing with its last assertion now satisfied by both trackpads wanting the tap rather than one; its earlier assertions still prove the merge, so it is left alone.

- **Docs.** `docs/architecture.md`, Concurrency: the sentence on when the gated port is on now says "while some trackpad is in a thumb touch, from a thumb in the anchor corner until its pad is empty", and the sentence on an unanswered window swipe says it blocks clicks and scrolling by the thumb rules and Dock swipes as any thumb touch does. `docs/domain-model.md` as in the first decision. In the code: `Blocking.none`'s comment ("no thumb in the corner, no slide, no swap series") gains "and no thumb touch"; `Blocking.possible`, `Blocking.dockSwipes` and `Recognition.blocksDockSwipes` as above; `Swipe.isConfirmed`'s comment ("blocks only by the thumb rules") says clicks and scrolling by the thumb rules, Dock swipes as any thumb touch does; `DockSwipeFilter`'s doc comment states the new rule and the `TODO(D2)` is gone.

## Testing Decisions

### Strategy

Three seams, all existing, from the one the rows prefer down.

- **The recognizer over scripted frames** (`LauncherKit/Tests/LauncherCoreTests/BlockingLevelRecognizerTests.swift`, whose `levels(_:answering:)` helper reads the level per frame and checks the two derived Bools against it, and `SwapRecognizerTests.swift`, with `TouchScript.swift` and the hand frames and `step(_:answering:)` of `Fixtures.swift`): the `Blocking` level on every frame, the events fired, and `answering: nil` for an unanswered window swipe. The new recognizer rows (T1 to T4) join `BlockingLevelRecognizerTests.swift` beside the three rows T11 updates; `DockSwipeBlockingRecognizerTests.swift`'s rows resemble them and stay as they are. This is where R1's four scenarios are reproduced, frame by frame in millimetres, which is the reproduction the human asked for in place of a live one. It observes the level and the events; it misses the tap and the gate.
- **`DockSwipeFilter` as a pure value** (`DockSwipeFilterTests.swift`, `FilterResumeTests.swift`): scripted (phase, blocking) sequences and the verdict each returns, plus `isDropping`. R2's reproduction lives here. It misses the gate around the filter.
- **World through `InMemoryTrackpads`** (`TapGateTests.swift`, `World.swift`): the real `Launcher`, recognizer and `TapFilters` with the fake's two ports; observes `isGatedPortOn` after every frame and `offer(_:) -> Delivery`, which tells "never delivered" from "passed". R3 is proved here, and R1 and R2 once more as the user would meet them: a Dock swipe that today reads `.notSent` or `.pass` reads `.decided(.dockSwipe(.drop))`. It misses the real ports, the tap thread and the real Dock's event sequence, which plan 014's hardware checks covered and this change does not touch.

Faked: the window server's ports and notices (the fake's turns) and the clock (scripted `FrameTime`), exactly as every existing World row fakes them. Nothing new is faked; everything in `LauncherCore` runs for real.

Before and after: T1, T2, T3, T5, T6, T7, T8, T9, T10 and T11 fail on today's code with the "today reads" value each row states, so each is a failing test first; T4 passes today and guards the "ordinary use is unaffected" scenario, and is the one row that would fail if the thumb touch were never ended. The rows that update an existing test keep that test's click-blocking and firing assertions word for word, so the diff shows only the Dock-swipe and gate lines moving. The rest of the suite staying green is the proof of every "unchanged" and "as today" clause in R1 and R2: clicks and scrolling (`ClickBlockingTests`, `ScrollSuppressionTests`, `ClickBlockingRecognizerTests`), swaps and taps after a rolled thumb (`SwapRecognizerTests`, `GestureRecognizerTests.thumbMayRollTwoMillimetresPastTheCornerEdge`), slides whatever the thumb did (`SwipeTravelAndExitTests`, `WindowSwipeRecognizerTests`), the Dock filter's whole-swipe rules (`DockSwipeFilterTests`), and the gate's other paths (`TapFiltersTests`, `TapGateRecoveryTests`).

## Further Notes

- The thumb touch is kept as one Bool beside the state machine on purpose: the state machine answers "what fires", the thumb touch answers "what macOS counts", and the two disagree exactly where the bug lives (a rolled thumb is a rest finger to the first and a contact to the second). Folding one into the other would couple the swap and tap rules to Dock swipe blocking.
- Accepted cost: after a thumb lifts while a finger stays down, the gated port stays on until the pad empties, so a finger that keeps pointing after a gesture routes its drags and scrolls through the app for the rest of that touch, at plan 014's measured per-frame cost. The one near-ordinary touch this reaches is a pointing stroke that starts inside the corner zone (about 25 × 19 mm on the 14" pad): today it opens the port on landing and closes it once the finger is 2 mm past the zone; now the port stays open for the whole stroke, and a three-finger system swipe added during that same touch is blocked (T11 (a) pins the level). A stroke that starts anywhere else never opens the port, so `docs/performance.md`'s numbers for ordinary pointing and scrolling stand.
- A `run` mid-touch (a setting or hand-mode change, a wake) replaces the recognizer, as today. The new recognizer starts a thumb touch only if a thumb is in the corner on its first frame; fingers alone resting on that frame, with the thumb already rolled out, are an ordinary touch to it. This is the same class of case as plan 002's "a thumb already resting when recognition restarted opens no window", and is left as is.
- Dropping a Dock swipe from a `changed`, once blocking turns on mid-way, was considered and rejected: a swipe the Dock has already started moving would be left half way, which plan 006 and `DockSwipeFilter`'s comment ("could leave the Dock stranded between two desktops") rule out; R2 keeps "first seen mid-way passes".
- Plan 014's open question 1 (D2) had two halves. Its policy half (pass a `began` after a no-warning open to keep the Dock whole?) is decided by R2 the other way: a `began` is judged on the level whether or not its `mayBegin` was seen, because the problem statement judges nothing visible to have happened at `mayBegin`. Its empirical half (do real Dock swipes send a `mayBegin`?) is still unobserved; if they do, a passed `mayBegin` followed by a dropped `began` leaves the Dock a lone warning, which is accepted as a risk already present in the no-warning-open race and is what the optional check below would reveal.
- Optional check for the human after the change ships, not an acceptance criterion since a live reproduction was ruled out: Safari over a long page, Mission Control on three-finger swipe, thumb in the corner, two fingers slide right until the switcher shows, then drag the thumb out of the corner and, without lifting it, lift and re-land the two fingers and slide again: the desktop must not switch and App Exposé must not open; the same with two fingers sliding down under the thumb while the frontmost app has one window. While there, plan 014's D2 observation: thumb in the corner alone, then three fingers landing and sliding, ten times, watching whether the Dock ever shows a half-started switch or a stuck Mission Control transition (a lone `mayBegin`'s visible trace, if it has one).
