# S4: A launched app hides the others once its window shows

Work only in `/Users/dominykasgrubys/code/trackpad-launcher/.claude/worktrees/032-hide-background-apps`. Do not commit: the orchestrator commits.

**Safety: never run anything that hides, activates, launches or quits a real app. No test, script or probe of yours may call the real hide or open an app on this Mac.**

## Goal

With the switch on, a gesture that launches an app leaves the other apps visible until the app shows its first window, then hides them, with the launch animation on (the bounce plays as today) or off (nothing shows at the pointer, the launch is watched unseen). A launch that fails or shows no window within the limits hides nothing, and so does one whose app is not the frontmost app when its window shows, because the user went elsewhere. A repeated gesture keeps the one launch and takes the switch's current value; the switch alone changes nothing about a launch already under way.

## Requirements

- R10: "While the setting is on, a gesture that launches an app that is not running MUST leave other apps visible until the launched app shows a window. It MUST then hide everything else." This holds with the launch animation setting off.
- R11: "A launch that fails, or that shows no window within the time limit, MUST hide nothing. The limit is reached when the launched app shows no window within 10 seconds, or within 2 seconds of finishing launching, whichever comes first."
- R12, as amended and final: a launch hides only if the launched app is the frontmost app at the moment its window shows. A user who brought a different app to the front and is still there hides nothing; a user who went away and came back before the window gets the hide. The frontmost read at window time decides. **There is no activation observer.**
- R4 for a launch under way: flipping the switch with no gesture changes nothing about it.

## Design

### Model: `WatchedLaunch` replaces the bounce's record (`LauncherCore`)

Today `LaunchBounce.swift` holds `LaunchProgress`, `LaunchBounceEnding` and the two-case enum `LaunchBounce` with `cap`, `grace`, `Step` and `next(after:)`; `Launcher.bounces: [BundleID: LaunchBounce]`.

- Rename the file `WatchedLaunch.swift`. `LaunchProgress` and `LaunchBounceEnding` keep their shapes and ordering rules.
- `struct WatchedLaunch: Equatable, Sendable`: the stage (`Stage`, the two cases `startingUp`, `startedUp`, carrying today's `Step` and today's six-transition `next(after:)` table unchanged) and `var hidesOthersOnWindow: Bool`. `WatchedLaunch.cap` (10 s) and `.grace` (2 s). Nothing in the model reads whether the bounce shows, so the record does not carry it: the table and the limit are the same either way, and the adapter ignores an ending when no bounce shows.
- `Launcher.launches: [BundleID: WatchedLaunch]` replaces `bounces`, one record per launched bundle, written only by `fire` and `progress(_:of:)`.

### `fire`

Today `visual(for:)` returns `(visual, hides)`. Reshape it so these rules hold; keep `fire` the one place silent cases are decided.

- App already in `launches`: keeps the one watch exactly as it is, bouncing or unseen; `launches[id]?.hidesOthersOnWindow = settings.isHideBackgroundAppsOn` (the latest gesture's setting counts); `system.bringToFront(app)`; no animation, no immediate hide. The launch animation setting applies to the next launch.
- With the animation and hide settings both off: `system.bringToFront(app)`, and `hasProcess` is not read, as today. No record.
- Otherwise read `hasProcess`:
  - With a process: as S2 built it: bring to front, the animation when that setting is on, the hide when that setting is on.
  - With no process: record `WatchedLaunch(stage: .startingUp, hidesOthersOnWindow: settings.isHideBackgroundAppsOn)` and `system.bringToFrontWatching(app, cap: WatchedLaunch.cap, bouncing: settings.isLaunchAnimationOn)`. So the animation off and the hide on gives an unseen watch.

### `progress(_:of:)`

The handler keeps its table.

- `.startUp(limit)`: stage becomes `.startedUp`; `system.limitLaunch(of: app, to: limit)`.
- `.end(ending)`: remove the record, `system.endLaunch(of: app, with: ending)`. Then, **after the end so the bounce's launch animation starts first**: when `progress == .windowShown`, the record says `hidesOthersOnWindow`, and `system.runningApps().frontmost?.bundleID == app`, call `system.hideOtherApps(than: app)` directly. Do not use the helper that checks the live setting: the record decides, not the switch now (T11).
- `.wentNowhere` and `.ranOut` never hide. A launch that ended leaves no record, so a late window hides nothing.
- The read is `runningApps().frontmost`, a few milliseconds on a launch push, never on the gesture path. Trackpad Launcher active without its Dock icon reads as the app before it, as everywhere else. A splash screen cannot defeat this: the app's activation precedes any window. Measured: LaunchServices activates a launched app about 90 ms after the open, long before its first window (0.5–1.6 s later), and the launched app does not take focus back when its window shows.
- Update the class doc comment at the top of `Launcher.swift` and the record's comment.

### Port (`LauncherCore/Ports.swift`)

- `bringToFrontBouncing(_:cap:)` becomes `func bringToFrontWatching(_ app: AppEntry, cap: Duration, bouncing: Bool)`: opens the app first exactly as `bringToFront` does, then watches its launch and pushes `onLaunchProgress` for it until the end, and, when `bouncing`, shows the launch bounce; one watch per app; a second call while one runs only brings the app to front.
- `limitLaunchBounce(of:to:)` becomes `limitLaunch(of:to:)`: no-op for an app whose launch is not watched.
- `endLaunchBounce(of:with:)` becomes `endLaunch(of:with:)`: stops the watch and its clock, and ends the bounce with the given ending when one shows, otherwise ignores the ending. Idempotent.
- `onLaunchProgress`, `LaunchProgress` and `LaunchBounceEnding` keep their shapes and ordering rules, but their texts say "launch" where they say "bounce" today: "only for an app whose bounce plays" becomes "whose launch is watched"; `LaunchProgress`' header, `.wentNowhere`'s text and the comment block above the members follow. Rewrite the block's call table to the new names and add the hide. `prepareLaunchAnimations`' text names the new member.

### Adapter (`LauncherPlatform/WorkspaceActions.swift`)

`bringToFrontWatching`: as `bringToFrontBouncing` is now (S1's order: `open(app, then: watch.opened)`, `watch.start(cap: cap)`), then `if bouncing { bounces.start(app) }`. `limitLaunch` and `endLaunch` as the old members; `bounces.end` is already a no-op with no bounce. Texts: `launches`' comment, `LaunchReportGate`'s and `report(_:of:)`'s say launch. Nothing else in `BounceOverlay` or `LaunchWatch` changes.

### Fake (`Tests/LauncherCoreTests/InMemoryAdapters.swift`)

- `launches: [Launch]` with `struct Launch: Equatable { let app: AppEntry; let cap: Duration; let bouncing: Bool; var limits: [Duration] = [] }`: the launches being watched, in start order; every `bringToFrontWatching` call appends; `endLaunch` removes, as `bounces` does today.
- `bounces` becomes derived: the bouncing `launches`, mapped to today's `Bounce(app:cap:limits:)`, so existing rows keep their literals.
- `bringToFrontWatching` records the bring to front first, clears the app from `hiddenApps`, then appends. `onBringToFrontBouncing` becomes `onBringToFrontWatching`.
- `bounceEndings` becomes `launchEndings`, since it now records unseen watches' ends; `BounceEnd` becomes `LaunchEnd`. This is a mechanical rename across the test files: every expected value stays exactly as it is.
- `LaunchBounceTests`' table literals keep their values under the new names (`WatchedLaunch.Stage.startingUp.next(after:)`, `WatchedLaunch.cap`).

## Rules that apply

- `docs/domain-model.md`, *Launch watch*: "Trackpad Launcher following an app a gesture launched, from the gesture until the first of: the app's first window showing (a splash screen counts), 2 seconds after the app finished starting up with no window, 10 seconds after the gesture, or the launch failing or the app quitting. It runs while the launch bounce shows and, with the launch animation setting off, unseen, while the hide background apps setting waits for the app's window; with both settings off nothing is watched. The bounce's endings and the hide after a launch are what it decides. A gesture for an app whose launch is watched keeps that one watch as it is, bouncing or unseen."
- `docs/domain-model.md`, *Launch animation setting*: "While it is off, a gesture shows nothing at the pointer…; the feedback and bring to front are unchanged, and with the hide background apps setting on a launch is still watched, unseen, so the other apps hide once the app's window shows. A change applies to the next gesture, so a bounce already playing runs its course."
- `docs/domain-model.md`, *Hide background apps setting*: "A gesture that launches its app hides the others only when the launch watch sees the app's first window, and only if the app is the frontmost app at that moment; a launch that fails, shows no window within the watch's limits, or whose user went to another app meanwhile hides nothing, and a repeated gesture for a launching app takes the switch's value then."
- `docs/domain-model.md`, *Launch bounce*: "No bounce lasts longer than 10 seconds, and a window that shows after a bounce ended plays nothing… A gesture for an app whose bounce still plays keeps that one bounce: the haptic plays and the app is brought to front, and nothing else shows."
- `docs/architecture.md`, Concurrency: "only while a launch is watched (its launch bounce shows, or the hide background apps setting waits for its first window), its launch's progress (`openApplication`'s answer, KVO on the launched process, an Accessibility window observer), and, only while a launch bounce shows, pointer moves." So no pointer monitor for an unseen watch: `BounceOverlay.start` alone makes them.
- Same section: "The one repeating read is bounded the same way: a watched launch whose app finished launching but whose next window it cannot be told of… re-reads the window list every 50 ms on that watch's clock, for at most the 2 s it then waits."
- `docs/architecture.md`, Privacy: Accessibility is used "to notice the first window of an app a gesture is launching, while its launch is watched (its launch bounce shows, or the hide background apps setting waits for that window)."
- The watch must never read as a bounce in names or texts you touch; `LaunchBounceEnding` keeps its name.
