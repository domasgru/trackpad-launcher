# Why: the click tap's lifetime, its thread, the filters' in-flight state, and the accepted races

Synthesised from two investigators (source control; plans) with every load-bearing citation spot-checked against the worktree at `9557bc2` (origin/main, PR #17). Paths below are relative to the repository root. "002:437" means `plans/002-gesture-click-blocking/plan.md` line 437; "002-log:26" that plan's `development-logs.md`; "002/.temp/candidate-2.md:667" its committed architect candidate. Code lines are from the current files.

---

### The Question

Why is the click tap installed and enabled for the whole time click blocking is armed (gestures active and Accessibility granted) rather than only while the recognizer may need to block; why did plan 002 rule out enabling or disabling it from the frame thread; why does it run on its own thread; why do the three filters hold their in-flight state as they do; and which timing races did earlier plans accept? The answer feeds a change that will gate the tap so high-volume events reach the process only while blocking may be needed or a filter still has something in flight, so it ends with what must be preserved, what may change, what to avoid, and the risks.

### The Code in Question

- `LauncherKit/Sources/LauncherPlatform/ClickTap.swift` (251 lines): `final class ClickTap: Sendable` (line 12); `install()` creates an active `.cgSessionEventTap` at `.headInsertEventTap` over `TapEvent.tapMask` (28–31) and starts a dedicated `userInteractive` thread whose run loop holds only the tap's source (34–44); `remove()` disables and invalidates the port on main (52–61); the re-enable on `tapDisabledByTimeout` / `tapDisabledByUserInput` forgets all three filters' memory and re-enables (65–72). The mask is 13 types: presses and releases, drags, pressure (34), scroll (22) and the system gesture / Dock control events (29, 30) (115–120, pinned by `TapEventTests`).
- `LauncherKit/Sources/LauncherCore/GestureRecognizer.swift`: `step` computes `blocksClicks` / `blocksDockSwipes` (182–200) from `State.thumb` (437–445), a confirmed swipe, or a swap series.
- `LauncherKit/Sources/LauncherCore/ClickFilter.swift`, `ScrollFilter.swift`, `DockSwipeFilter.swift`: three pure state machines, one each per tap, fed every event in order on the tap thread.
- `LauncherKit/Sources/LauncherPlatform/FrameStream.swift` (`DeviceSession.holding`, `anyTrackpadBlocksClicks()`), `MultitouchTrackpads.swift` (`run`, which removes and reinstalls the tap), and `LauncherCore/Touch.swift` (`TouchFrame.Press.init(holding:buttonDown:)`, the frame-side precedence rule).

### What We Found

#### 1. The tap's lifetime: why "installed and enabled for the whole armed run"

- **[Direct]** Plan 002 defined the tap's lifecycle inputs as trust and gesture activity, not recognizer state. Source: `002/.temp/architect/task.md:22` ("**Lifecycle.** When the tap is created and torn down relative to trust and to gesture activity; what happens on revoke ..., on grant without relaunch, on `run([])`, on wake. Idempotent under repeated reconciles.") and `002:379` (decision 5: `reconcile()` arms blocking with `blockClicks: activity.isActive && isAccessibilityGranted`, "R4 'No blocking while gestures are inactive' and R9 'Declined' in one expression"). Today's `Launcher.swift:221–224` carries the same expression.
- **[Direct]** The tap belongs to the hardware port and lives from one `run` to the next because the plan wanted one convergent lifecycle. Source: `002:377` (decision 3: "`run` already means 'forget everything, start exactly this', and that now includes 'remove the tap; install a fresh one if asked and allowed'. Every reset is structural") and `002:1132` (tradeoff: "We accept tearing down and recreating the tap on every reconcile ... In exchange, there is one convergent `run` and no second lifecycle path for the tap."). Code: `ClickTap.swift:8` ("It exists from the `run` that armed click blocking until the next `run`."), `Ports.swift:35–38`, `MultitouchTrackpads.swift:66–87`.
- **[Direct]** Plan 002's grounding explicitly raised, and left open, whether the tap's enabled state should follow the frame thread. Source: `002/.temp/architect/grounding/why.md:135` (risk 6, "Idle cost of a tap": "The record does not address whether that is a 'blip' or how the tap's enabled state would follow the frame thread.").
- **[Direct]** The only recorded reasons for not gating the tap by recognizer state are in the non-base candidate. Source: `002/.temp/architect/candidate-2.md:667` (alternative rejected: "**Hop the window decision to main and enable/disable the tap as it opens and closes.** Zero wakeups for mouse clicks while idle, but a main-actor hop per window transition, and a disabled tap misses the passed presses the filter must know about. Rejected on cost and correctness; a possible later refinement if the idle blips ever matter.") and `candidate-2.md:654` (tradeoff: "We accept that the tap, while installed, wakes the click thread for every mouse-button event session-wide ... in exchange for never enabling or disabling the tap from the frame thread"). Two reasons, then: a main-actor hop per transition (cost) and a disabled tap missing passed presses (correctness). Note the cost weighed was mouse-button events only; the mask then held no scroll or gesture types.
- **[Direct]** The final plan states the rule without any reason. Source: `002:437` (Deliberately not done: "... No enabling or disabling of the tap from the frame thread.") and `002:1319` (S4 checklist: "`grep -rn tapEnable LauncherKit/Sources` hits `ClickTap.swift` only (nothing on the frame thread enables or disables the tap)."). Candidate-1, the chosen base (`002:1121`), does not contain the sentence (`002/.temp/architect/candidate-1.md:177`), and neither the Synthesis decision (`002:1117–1125`) nor `002-log` records grafting it or its reasons.
- **[Direct]** The cost model every plan accepted counts only the callback's own work, and R22's zero-cost bound covers no-touch idle only. Source: `002:1134` ("the tap wakes for every drag and pressure change in the session (a dictionary lookup each)"), `002:1133` ("It sleeps in the kernel between events and costs no idle CPU"), `006:672` and `006:1343` ("scroll events add ~100–250 Hz of wake-ups while anyone scrolls with the tap armed (F19, accepted)"; "We accept that the tap wakes for every scroll event in the session while armed (F19), in exchange for one tap and no second permission."), `006/.temp/architect/grounding/index.md:41` (F19: "each a few lock reads"), `001:419–420` (R22: "While no finger is touching any trackpad, Trackpad Launcher MUST show 0% CPU"). The one hardware check that would have observed scroll wake-ups (`006:1511`) was never run: `006-log:38` ("every hardware and on-screen check under Verified without a test is still unticked (no hardware available to the implementers)").
- **[Direct]** Scroll (type 22) and the gesture/Dock control types (29, 30) were added to the existing tap without revisiting its lifetime. Source: commit `9d2f330` ("Scroll suppression in the one tap (S4)", 2026-10-11) and commit `9044785` ("Block the system's desktop swipes during gestures", 2026-10-12; both squashed into [PR #5](https://github.com/domasgru/trackpad-launcher/pull/5), `10b0e91`); `006-log:45` for the Dock swipe fix (a user report after merge review, "Not reproduced (intermittent); diagnosed from the code and settings"). No plan estimates the volume or cost of types 29 and 30; they appear only in that log entry. `docs/architecture.md:20` gained its parenthetical event list in `10b0e91` while keeping "sleeps until an event arrives".
- **[Direct]** No commit after `10b0e91` (2026-10-12) touches `ClickTap.swift` or any of the three filters. Source: `git log --all -- ClickTap.swift ClickFilter.swift ScrollFilter.swift DockSwipeFilter.swift` returns `0765cf4`, `6ed0d9c`, `24993a0`, `1cf9c4b`, `9d2f330`, `9044785`, `10b0e91` only. `tapEnable` is called from `ClickTap.swift:55` and `:70` and nowhere else.

#### 2. Why the tap runs on its own thread

- **[Direct]** To keep every click in the session off the app's main thread and avoid a WindowServer stall that ends in a timeout disable. Source: `002:378` (decision 4: "Every click in the login session waits for the tap's callback. On the main run loop that wait would include the app's UI work (the picker's enumeration, LaunchServices lookups, SwiftUI layout) and risk a stall with main blocked in a WindowServer call while the WindowServer waits on our tap, until the tap is disabled by timeout. A dedicated `userInteractive` thread holds only the tap's run loop source, sleeps in the kernel between events and ends when `run` removes the tap. The thread retains its `ClickTap`, so no callback can outlive the object behind its `userInfo` pointer."), `002:1145` (alternative "The tap on the main run loop ... Rejected for robustness."), `002:1133` (tradeoff: "click latency that never depends on the app's main thread and no WindowServer stall risk"), `002/.temp/architect/candidate-2.md:111` ("The WindowServer disables a slow tap and clicks then pass silently; the main thread does this app's only slow work"). Code: `ClickTap.swift:9–11`, `34–44`.
- **[Direct]** The SDK header is the source of the disable/re-enable contract. Source: `002/.temp/architect/grounding/index.md:18` (quoting `CGEvent.h`: "If a tap becomes unresponsive or a user requests taps be disabled, an appropriate `kCGEventTapDisabled...` event is passed to the registered CGEventTapCallBack function. An event tap may be re-enabled by calling this function"; conclusion: "a slow callback (the WindowServer's timeout is undocumented) gets the tap disabled and events pass through").

#### 3. Who may touch the tap's port

- **[Direct]** One writer per shared field, with main the port's writer and the tap thread only re-enabling. Source: `002:401` ("*Writers:* `sessions` by main (`run`); each `blocking` flag by its device's frame callback; the filter by the tap thread; the tap's port by main (install and remove), with the tap thread only re-enabling through it. No two threads write the same field."), `ClickTap.swift:19` ("Writer: main (install, remove). The tap thread only re-enables."), `ClickTap.swift:13` (filter: "Writer: the tap thread. Reader: frame threads."), `FrameStream.swift:19–22` (blocking flags: "Writer: this device's frame callback. Reader: the click tap's thread."), `docs/architecture.md:18` ("State shared with that thread lives behind a `Mutex` with one writer."), `SourcePolicyTests.swift:53` (`nonisolated(unsafe)` banned under rule "one writer per field"). The frame thread is not listed as a writer of the port anywhere.
- **[Direct]** The frame thread never waits on main; the one main-to-frame channel is the window swipe answer. Source: `docs/architecture.md:18` ("Until the answer arrives the slide blocks only by the thumb rules, and the frame thread never waits for it."), `012:690` ("No main-to-frame channel beyond the one answer per window swipe start."), `012:1471–1472`, `012/.temp/architect/grounding/why.md:168` ("Never restart devices from a gesture.").

#### 4. The defensive re-enable, and what it forgets

- **[Direct]** The re-enable was designed in from the SDK contract, not from an incident, and the forgetting half was hardened at cross-judge. Source: `002:378` (quoted above), `002/.temp/architect/task.md:17` (the brief asks to "handle `tapDisabledByTimeout` / `tapDisabledByUserInput` by re-enabling"), `002-log:26` ("candidate-2 never cleared a passed press whose release the tap missed while disabled, cancelling every later tap until the next reconcile → candidate-1's forget-on-disable kept and extended to passed presses"), `006:649` (decision 11: "on `tapDisabledBy…` the filter forgets the gesture in progress, as `forgetHeldPresses()` does"), `006:1479` (T67: "under-blocking is the safe direction, plan 002"). Code: `ClickTap.swift:65–72`, `ClickFilter.swift:73`, `ScrollFilter.swift:64`, `DockSwipeFilter.swift:29`. `git blame` places lines 65, 67, 70, 71 in `1cf9c4b` (slice `6ed0d9c`) and lines 66, 68, 69 in `10b0e91`.
- **[Direct]** The plan accepted the consequence of a system disable as a "microsecond window". Source: `002:1130` ("We accept that a press the tap passed unseen while disabled by the system (a microsecond window) reads as nothing to the recognizer, so a tap could fire beside such a click. In exchange, no frame can ever read a press as a click before the tap has passed it, which would cost the user both the click and the gesture.").
- **[Direct]** The re-enable path has no automated test and `run` needs real devices. Source: [PR #7](https://github.com/domasgru/trackpad-launcher/pull/7) "Not covered": "`ClickTap.handle` re-enabling a disabled tap: it needs Accessibility trust." "`MultitouchTrackpads.run`: it needs real devices." The hardware recovery checks are written (`002:1258`, `006:1511`, pause in the debugger while clicking or scrolling, resume) but `006-log:38` records 006's as unticked.
- **[Direct]** Nothing in code or history covers an intentional disable by the app itself. The forgetting at `ClickTap.swift:66–69` is tied to the two system-initiated event types only. That would be a new case.

#### 5. ClickFilter: a press blocked or passed whole

- **[Direct]** R5 is a requirement born from an undefined case. Source: `002:113–114` ("The press-down and the release of one press MUST both be blocked or both be passed."), scenarios "Thumb lands during a drag" and "Press across the 3-second mark" (`002:116–124`), `002-log:6` ("Press straddling the window or a drag in progress was undefined → new requirement: a press is blocked or passed as a whole."). Code: `ClickFilter.swift:48` ("The press is blocked or passed whole: the flag is ignored."); `docs/domain-model.md:120`.
- **[Direct]** The filter's own decision is the single truth about a press; the frame thread reads `holding` and never decides for itself. Source: `002:376` (decision 2: "No ordering of events can produce 'a click and a gesture' or 'neither': a press the tap passed reads `.click` only after the tap passed it, and a press the tap dropped reads `.blocked` whatever the button state table says."), `002:1143` (the alternative of a "blocking in effect" input to the recognizer rejected: "R5's whole-press rule would live twice ... When they disagree, the user gets the click and the gesture.").
- **[Direct]** The frame-side precedence rule, and why `.nothing` reads as no press even with the button down. Source: `002:367` ("`.nothing` reads nil even if the button state is down (the tap has not decided that press yet, or passed it unseen); with no filter (no tap, no permission), the button state alone says `.click`"), `002-log:23` (cross-judge: candidate-1 read button state, "so a frame could read a press as a click before the tap dropped it, costing the user both the click and the gesture → the frame's press is derived from `ClickFilter.holding` ..., with the button still down required for `.click` so a release the tap missed heals on the next frame"). Code: `Touch.swift:67–80`, `FrameStream.swift:71–72` ("the click filter's summary when a tap is live, nil otherwise"), test `holdingAndButtonStateDecideWhatAFrameReads` (`ClickBlockingRecognizerTests.swift:220`) pins `(.nothing, true) -> nil` and `(nil, true) -> .click`.
- **[Direct]** Drags and pressure are in the mask so a withheld press is withheld whole. Source: `002:1134` ("apps never see a drag or a Force Click stage of a press they never got"); `ClickFilter.swift:28, 55`.

#### 6. ScrollFilter: suppressed at any point, never relaxes

- **[Direct]** The rule, its reasons and the race it closes. Source: `006:649` (decision 11: "a gesture first seen at an opening event ... while blocking is `dropped`, so apps never see any of it; a gesture that was passing and meets blocking mid-way becomes `stilled`, its remaining events passed with every delta zeroed ..., so the app's began-to-ended bracket closes and nothing moves; a `passed` gesture's momentum is never touched; the treatment never relaxes inside a gesture, the scroll analogue of plan 002 R5's whole press. This is what survives the race the single flag cannot order: a `changed` or `ended` arriving after the lift frame has cleared the flag is still part of a gesture already decided"), `006:1329` (tradeoff; "We read R12's 'work again at once' as 'the next scroll works at once'"), `006:1352` (dropping only leaves apps an open gesture with no `ended`; zeroing only makes overlay scrollers flash and "per-event decisions relax at the lift and leak"), `006-log:28` (candidate 2's "per-event scroll rule that leaks a `changed` racing the lift frame" rejected). Code: `ScrollFilter.swift:40, 43, 53`; `docs/domain-model.md:120`.
- **[Direct]** Memory is needed because momentum arrives after the flag is already false. Source: `006/.temp/architect/grounding/index.md:40` (F18, probed on hardware: a flick is `began → changed… → ended`, then ~7 ms later `momentum begin → continuous… → end`, ~0.9–1.0 s long; "momentum suppression (R14) needs one bit of memory per gesture because momentum events arrive after the fingers lifted (when `blocksClicks` is already false)"). Also from F18: `mayBegin` arrives ~17 ms before `began`; events are 4–12 ms apart in a gesture, 8–17 ms in momentum; an always-on `.defaultTap` over `scrollWheel` saw "0 disabled notices".
- **[Direct]** Later plans treat "never relaxes" as a constraint they cannot undo. Source: `012-log:65` ("Rejected: ... starting blocked then releasing (the scroll filter never relaxes)"), `012:1465–1466` (a window swipe's blocking starts one frame late "because a scroll the filter has once stilled stays stilled until the fingers lift"), `010-log:18`.

#### 7. DockSwipeFilter: judged once at its opening event, kept whole

- **[Direct]** Source: `006-log:45` ("a pure `DockSwipeFilter` that judges each Dock swipe once on its opening event and keeps it whole, since dropping one mid-way could strand the Dock between desktops. Its flag, `Recognition.blocksDockSwipes`, has no time limit: a thumb anchored with another contact down, or a swipe on. The field numbers are undocumented and taken from reverse-engineered Dock swipe tools; a hardware check must confirm the tap sees and drops real Dock swipes."), commit `9044785`, `DockSwipeFilter.swift:11–12` ("So a swipe first seen mid-way always passes."), `ClickTap.swift:17` (one filter per gesture event type, "since a Dock swipe may arrive as both"), `ClickTap.swift:152` ("A Dock swipe with an unknown phase reads as `.changed`, which the filter never starts a drop on."), `docs/domain-model.md:120` ("A system swipe is blocked or passed whole, decided when it begins."). Plan 006's `plan.md` was never amended for this; R9's "MUST NOT read ... any other event" (`002:181`) was amended for scrolling (`006:21`) but not for types 29 and 30.

### What We Can Reasonably Infer

- **[Inferred]** The final plan 002 most likely carried candidate-2's rule over on its two stated reasons (a main-actor hop per transition; a disabled tap missing passed presses) without re-deriving them. Reasoning: the sentence appears verbatim in neither candidate-1 nor the Synthesis's recorded grafts, yet it is in the final "Deliberately not done" (`002:437`) and the S4 checklist (`002:1319`); candidate-2 is the only text that argues it (`candidate-2.md:146, 654, 667`); and the grounding had asked exactly this question (`grounding/why.md:135`). The alternative reading, that the final author had a different unrecorded reason, cannot be excluded.
- **[Inferred]** Of candidate-2's two reasons, the cost reason no longer holds and the correctness reason still does. Reasoning: the cost objection was "a main-actor hop per window transition" at a time the tap woke only for mouse-button events (`candidate-2.md:654`); transitions happen once per thumb landing or lift, whereas plan 014's profile puts the recurring cost at every scroll and gesture event through SkyLight's decode and re-post (`014:14`, 84% of the app's CPU while touching). The correctness objection, "a disabled tap misses the passed presses the filter must know about", is still exactly what `Touch.swift:67–80` and `002:367` encode: with a live `ClickTap`, a press the filter never saw reads as no press, so it cannot cancel a tap gesture. Plan 002 accepted that outcome only for "a microsecond window" (`002:1130`).
- **[Inferred]** The "not from the frame thread" rule is consistent with, and likely partly motivated by, the one-writer discipline and the "frame thread never waits" rule, though no text links them. Reasoning: `002:401` names main as the port's only writer with the tap thread re-enabling; `ClickTap.swift:19` repeats it; `docs/architecture.md:18` says state shared with the frame thread has one writer and the frame thread never waits. Enabling from the frame thread would add a third writer of the port's enabled state and a synchronous WindowServer call on the frame callback. An alternative reading: the one-writer rule is about Swift fields behind a `Mutex`, and `CGEvent.tapEnable` is a system call, so the rule may not have been meant to cover it.
- **[Inferred]** The same-frame landing opens blocking from that frame, so a gate keyed on recognizer state would need to be open from the first frame that shows a thumb with another contact down. Reasoning (code mechanics, not intent): from `.idle`, a corner contact landing with another contact lands the recognizer in `.spoiled(anchor)` (`GestureRecognizer.swift:94`); `State.thumb` returns the anchor for `.spoiled` (`:443`); `blocksClicks` is then true when the thumb's landing time is known (it is, since both landed with `.landing`) and another contact is down (`:191–198`). No test pins this, so it is a reading of the code only.
- **[Inferred]** For the scroll filter, only the dropped and stilled treatments carry load-bearing memory; a `passed` gesture's memory changes no verdict. Reasoning from the filter's table (`ScrollFilter.swift:38–60`, pinned by T64–T67): a `passed` gesture that meets blocking becomes stilled whether the filter remembers it (`touching(.passed) && blocking → stilled`) or first sees it mid-way (`nil` stage, mid phase, blocking → stilled); momentum with no known gesture passes, as `coasting(.passed)` does. A dropped or stilled gesture, by contrast, needs its memory to keep dropping or stilling after the lift clears the flag (`006:649`, T71). This bears on how long a gate must stay open after blocking ends: through the end of any dropped or stilled gesture's momentum, which F18 measured at about a second after the lift.
- **[Inferred]** The Dock swipe filter's safe direction (a swipe first seen mid-way passes) means a gate that opens late lets a Dock swipe through whole, which is the exact symptom the 29/30 fix was for. Reasoning: `006-log:45` adds the types because "thumb plus two sliding fingers reads as that three-finger swipe"; `DockSwipeFilter.swift:11–12` passes anything first seen after its opening event; so if the swipe's `mayBegin`/`began` arrives before the gate opens on the thumb's landing frame, the desktop switches. Whether that ordering happens on hardware was never measured (the fix itself was "Not reproduced (intermittent)").

### Competing Hypotheses

On why the final plan 002 forbade enabling or disabling the tap from the frame thread:

- **Hypothesis A: cost and correctness, carried from candidate-2.** Evidence for: `candidate-2.md:667` and `:654` state both reasons; the rule's wording in `002:437` matches candidate-2's; the grounding had raised the question (`grounding/why.md:135`). ## Constraint set for gating the tap

Each item names its evidence tier. "Direct" items are stated requirements, decisions or tradeoffs in the record; "Inferred" items follow from them plus the code's mechanics.

### Preserve

1. **A press is blocked or passed whole, and the filter must see both edges of any press it decided.** `002:113–114` (R5), `002:376`, `ClickFilter.swift:44–50`. [Direct] A gate must not stop event delivery while `ClickFilter.holding != .nothing`; the withheld press's drags (`.passAsMove`) and pressure (`.drop`) belong to it too (`002:1134`). [Inferred from R5 plus the mask decision]
2. **A scroll gesture suppressed at any point stays suppressed through its momentum, and a gesture first seen mid-way is stilled, never dropped.** `006:649`, `006:1329`, `006-log:28`, `012-log:65`, `ScrollFilter.swift:40–48`, T64–T67, T71. [Direct] The momentum of a dropped or stilled gesture arrives after the flag is false and lasts about a second (F18, `006/.temp/.../index.md:40`), so a gate must stay open until the filter's gesture memory is clear (`stage == nil`), not merely until blocking ends. [Inferred] A `passed` gesture's memory is not load-bearing. [Inferred, see above]
3. **A Dock swipe is judged once at its opening event and kept whole; one first seen mid-way passes.** `006-log:45`, `DockSwipeFilter.swift:11–12`, `docs/domain-model.md:120`. [Direct] A gate must stay open while any `DockSwipeFilter.current == .drop`, for both event types (`ClickTap.swift:17–18`). [Inferred]
4. **The frame-side press rule: with a live filter, only the filter's `holding` decides; a press the tap passed must read `.click` while the button is down so it cancels a tap gesture; a press the tap dropped must read `.blocked`.** `002:367`, `002:376`, `002-log:23`, `Touch.swift:67–80`, test `holdingAndButtonStateDecideWhatAFrameReads`. [Direct] Plan 002's one accepted exception, a press passed unseen while the system had disabled the tap, was accepted as "a microsecond window" (`002:1130`). [Direct] A gated-off tap that leaves `holding` at `.nothing` while a tap gesture could be in progress widens that window to the whole gated period and reintroduces "the click and the gesture", which `002:376` says no ordering may produce. [Inferred; this is candidate-2's correctness objection, `candidate-2.md:667`]
5. **The tap's own `userInteractive` thread, installed and removed on main, with its run loop holding only the tap's source.** `002:378`, `002:1133`, `002:1145`, `ClickTap.swift:9–11, 34–44`. [Direct]
6. **One writer per shared field; main owns the port's install and remove; the tap thread only re-enables.** `002:401`, `ClickTap.swift:19`, `docs/architecture.md:18`, `SourcePolicyTests.swift:53`. [Direct] Whatever toggles the gate must have a single, named writer.
7. **Recovery on `tapDisabledByTimeout` / `tapDisabledByUserInput`: forget every filter's memory (under-blocking is the safe direction) and re-enable.** `002:378`, `002-log:26`, `006:649`, `006:1479`, `ClickTap.swift:65–72`. [Direct] Plan 014 R4 adds that the re-enable must not turn on a tap the gate wants off (`014:39`); the existing code has no such case. [Direct]
8. **`run` removes the tap itself and never relies on macOS to stop delivery; a tap exists iff asked, allowed and a trackpad runs; the port's lifecycle has no second path.** `002:377`, `002:379`, `002:1132`, `Ports.swift:35–38`, `ClickTap.swift:51`, `MultitouchTrackpads.swift:66–87`. [Direct]
9. **No timers, polling or background loops; the frame thread never waits on main.** `docs/architecture.md:18, 20`, `SourcePolicyTests.swift:30–35`, `001:548`. [Direct] A gate cannot close "after a delay"; it must be driven by frames and events.
10. **The first-frame premise and the accepted landing race.** `blocksClicks` can be true on the first frame after arming, and a press right after a grant must be caught (`24993a0`, `ClickBlockingTests.swift:66–72`); a press reaching the tap before its finger's landing frame passes and cancels the tap (`002:1129`, T33, `009:382`). [Direct] A gate must be open no later than the shipped tap would have decided these presses, or the test expectations change.
11. **The thumb rules open blocking on a frame where other fingers may already be dragging or scrolling (thumb under a rest or mid-drag), and `blocksDockSwipes` has no time limit.** `006:1331`, `009:302`, `002:116–119`, `SwapRecognizerTests.swift:29–42`, `011/testing.md:27`. [Direct] The gate must open on that frame and stay open while a thumb is in the corner with another contact down, past the 3 s window.
12. **The source-policy confinements: `tapCreate`/`CGEventTapCreate` and `scrollWheel` only in `ClickTap.swift`; nothing posts or synthesises events.** `SourcePolicyTests.swift:49–51, 79–81`, `002:437`. [Direct]

### Change

1. **The tap's enabled state within its run-to-run lifetime.** Nothing in the record requires the tap to receive events outside the states plan 014 R1 names; the brief that fixed the lifetime named only trust and activity (`task.md:22`), the grounding left the enabled-state question open (`grounding/why.md:135`), and candidate-2 called gating "a possible later refinement if the idle blips ever matter" (`candidate-2.md:667`). [Direct] The cost model behind the always-on choice counted callback work only (`002:1134`, F19) and never the per-event transport cost plan 014 measured (`014:14`). [Supported]
2. **The rule "no enabling or disabling of the tap from the frame thread" (`002:437`), provided its recorded reasons are addressed**: the correctness reason (Preserve 4) by design, and the writer discipline (Preserve 6) by naming one owner of the gate. The cost reason (a main-actor hop per transition) is small next to the measured per-event cost, since transitions are per thumb landing and lift rather than per event. [Inferred]
3. **`docs/architecture.md:20`**, which must stop claiming the tap "sleeps until an event arrives" and say when it receives events (`014:42`; the sentence's history: `1cf9c4b`, parenthetical in `10b0e91`). [Direct]
4. **The in-memory fake (`InMemoryAdapters.swift:12–16, 103–123`)**, which today models an always-on tap and so cannot catch gate-timing regressions (`014:33` already requires the gating to be modelled). [Direct]
5. **The re-enable branch (`ClickTap.swift:65–72`)**, which must become gate-aware (`014:39`). [Direct]
6. **The tap's shape (one tap over the whole mask).** No plan forbids a second tap or a narrower mask for the gated period: 006 chose "one tap" to avoid a second permission (`006:1343`), which a second tap in the same process would not need; the policy rule "R9 the one event tap" confines creation to `ClickTap.swift` (`SourcePolicyTests.swift:79–80`) and would need rewording. [Inferred]

### Avoid

1. **Closing the gate while anything is withheld, stilled or dropped in flight** (a press held, a scroll gesture dropped or stilled including its momentum, a Dock swipe dropped). This is Preserve 1–3 and plan 014 R3 (`014:36`).
2. **Resetting filter memory on a gate transition.** The plans record what a fresh or forgetting filter costs mid-gesture: a headless resumption without the `began` apps never saw, a stuck elastic edge or an overlay scroller that never appears (`006:641`, `006-log:35`, `006:1505`). That was accepted only for rare events (a system disable, a reconcile), and plan 008 removed the one such restart a user gesture could trigger (`008:276`). [Direct]
3. **Leaving `holding == .nothing` to mean "no press" while the tap is gated off during any state in which a tap gesture or swap could complete.** `Touch.swift:67–80` falls back to button state only when `holding` is nil (no filter). Either keep the gate open whenever a thumb is anchored (the only state a tap gesture can fire from, `001:548`, `006:636`), or make the frame path read a gated-off tap as "no filter live". Both are design choices for 014; the record only says what the current two states mean. [Inferred]
4. **Relying on macOS to stop delivery** after a revoke or a `run` (`002:379`, `ClickTap.swift:51`). [Direct]
5. **A timer or delay to close the gate** (`SourcePolicyTests.swift:30–35`, `docs/architecture.md:20`). [Direct]
6. **Making the frame thread a second writer of the port's enabled state, or having it wait on a system call whose cost is unmeasured** (`002:401`, `ClickTap.swift:19`, `docs/architecture.md:18`). If the frame thread is to drive the gate, the record offers no measurement of `tapEnable` to say it is cheap. [Inferred]
7. **Re-enabling on `tapDisabledBy…` when the gate wants the tap off** (`014:39`). [Direct]
8. **Treating a reconcile as a gating mechanism.** The reconcile path replaces recognizers and ends any swipe or window swipe in progress (`Launcher.swift:228`, `Ports.swift:39`), and `006-log:48` moved reconcile away from needless re-runs because they closed the switcher mid-swipe. [Direct]

### Risk

1. **Latency between the frame that sets a blocking flag and the tap actually receiving events.** The shipped build has no such gap: the tap is always enabled, and the only race is the press edge against the frame callback (`002/.temp/.../index.md:27`, "not probed"). A gate adds the enable's own latency on top. In that gap a press passes unseen (then must still read `.click`, see Preserve 4); a scroll `mayBegin`/`began` passes and the gesture is stilled rather than dropped, so an overlay scroller flashes (`006:1374` describes this symptom for the analogous race); a Dock swipe's opening event passes and the whole desktop switch goes through, the very symptom the 29/30 fix addressed (`006-log:45`, intermittent and never reproduced). Nothing in the record bounds the enable latency. [Inferred]
2. **`CGEvent.tapEnable` as a primitive is unmeasured**: cost, blocking behaviour, thread-safety, whether events already queued to the port arrive after a disable, and what the first events after a re-enable look like. The only recorded probes are of an always-on tap. [Unknown]
3. **The real tap's enable/disable path cannot be tested without Accessibility trust and real devices** ([PR #7](https://github.com/domasgru/trackpad-launcher/pull/7) "Not covered"); 006's hardware checks for the tap, including the Dock-swipe check, were never ticked (`006-log:38`). Any gating change needs a hand check on hardware, including the debugger-pause recovery checks (`002:1258`, `006:1511`). [Direct]
4. **The behavioural suite cannot see a gate.** With the fake unchanged, every World-level blocking test passes whatever the gate does (`InMemoryAdapters.swift:12–16`). [Direct]
5. **The tap thread cannot wake itself to re-enable** once it receives no events, and the re-enable branch today assumes the tap should always be on. A gate needs an owner that is awake when blocking starts: the frame thread (contradicts `002:437` as written) or main (a hop whose lateness the window-swipe answer already shows can exceed a frame, `012:1267`). [Inferred]
6. **The same-frame landing and the thumb-under-rest cases open blocking on their first qualifying frame** (`GestureRecognizer.swift:94, 191–198`; `SwapRecognizerTests.swift:29–42`), and no test pins `blocksClicks` on the same-frame landing. A gate keyed on "thumb anchored" rather than on the flags would need to cover `.spoiled`, `.rest` with a thumb, and an unconfirmed `.swiping` (`State.thumb`, `:437–445`). [Inferred]
7. **The stuck-flag assumption.** Blocking clears on the empty frame after the last lift (`002:1161`, `006:1375`). A gate that stays open on a stale flag would keep the full event volume routed through the process until the next touch or `run`; a gate that closes on a stale filter memory would violate Preserve 2–3. [Inferred]
8. **`docs/architecture.md:18`'s "frame thread never waits"** and the one-writer rule constrain where the toggle may live; the plans' only precedent for a main-to-frame channel is the window swipe answer, added with a "no further channels" note (`012:690`). [Direct]
