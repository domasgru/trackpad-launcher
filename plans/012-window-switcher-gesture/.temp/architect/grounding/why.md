# Why the swipe, the switcher, the ports and the permission rules are shaped as they are

Synthesised from two investigators (source control; plans 001 to 011) with spot-checks of every load-bearing citation against the worktree, the commit log and `gh`. Tiers: **[Direct]** an author wrote the reason; **[Supported]** several sources converge; **[Inferred]** our reading, hedged; **[Speculative]** a guess, labelled; gaps are listed, not filled.

---

### The Question

The architect is adding a *window swipe*: two fingers sliding up or down under the anchored thumb (today a vertical first drag spoils the pair), showing the frontmost app's windows in a vertical list top-left, raising each window as the selection passes it through the Accessibility API (`AXUIElement`, `AXRaise`, `kAXMinimized`), with window previews that need Screen Recording. Why is the existing code shaped the way it is, across seven sub-questions, so the change can preserve those reasons or knowingly change them?

### What We Found

#### Q1. Why `beginOrSpoil` spoils a non-sideways first drag; where 3 mm, "more sideways than up or down" and the farthest-moved finger came from

- **[Direct]** The 3 mm value is plan 001's *tap* drag rule, a feel constant that was never derived or tuned. Source: `plans/001-trackpad-launcher-app/plan.md` sketch :642 (`dragThresholdMM = 3.0 // a finger farther than this from its landing point is dragging`), R11 :173 ("fingers that drag or scroll while the thumb is anchored" MUST NOT fire), Non-goals ("Fixed values, chosen to feel natural on common trackpads"), Open questions :1399 ("**Feel constants.** 300 ms tap, 3 mm drag … Should these be tuned once on a 13" MacBook and a Magic Trackpad before they are frozen…?"). Commit `4552ceb` shipped it with the comment still at `GestureRules.swift:20-21` ("A finger farther than this from its landing point is dragging, not tapping."). Later plans froze it by fiat: 002 Non-goals ("the drag threshold stay[s] as [it is]"), 006 Non-goals :476 ("12 mm, 80 pt and 3 mm are fixed, like the other gesture rules"). No plan or commit records a measurement behind 3.0.
- **[Direct]** The constant is shared by three gestures. The thumb tap reuses `dragThresholdMM` (`GestureRecognizer.swift:218`, from `19cdbf9`; `docs/domain-model.md` "Thumb tap": "does not drag more than 3 mm"), and the swipe begins at the same boundary (`SwipeRecognizerTests.swift:8` `twoFingersBeginASwipeOnlyPastThreeMillimetres`, args 2.9/3.1). Changing it moves tap, swipe and thumb-tap boundaries together.
- **[Direct]** The vertical-first spoil is a stated requirement, with a stated rationale in a non-goal. Source: `plans/006-five-finger-tap-and-corner-swipe/plan.md` R3 :59: "one of the two fingers has moved more than 3 mm from where it landed, with that movement more sideways than up or down … When the first movement past 3 mm is more up or down than sideways, no swipe begins and nothing further happens until every non-thumb finger has lifted." Its scenario "Up or down is not a swipe" (10 mm down, then 20 mm right, without lifting → no switcher, nothing opens) rules out "becomes a swipe later". Non-goals :470: "**Vertical swipes.** Up and down with two fingers is scrolling; the switcher is a sideways gesture only." The human's brief asked only for "swiping with 2 fingers left or right" (006 `.temp/architect/task.md`); the non-goal itself was added by the Specify review (006 `development-logs.md:19`: "Missing non-goals → vertical swipes, quitting or hiding from the switcher, a cap on list length, window previews.").
- **[Direct]** The swipe was deliberately carved out at the exact point where the tap's drag rule already spoiled, as the minimal change, and everything else kept spoiling. Source: 006 design decision 1 "Begin" (:634): "The drag check in `.tapping` becomes the swipe's entry. When the farthest-moved finger passes 3 mm, the tap becomes `.swiping` if swipes are on, the tap is a `Pair` … and that finger's displacement is more sideways than vertical (`|dx| > |dy|` in mm); otherwise it is spoiled as today." Decision 1 "No time limit" (:635): `.resting` "becomes `.swiping` on a sideways first move past 3 mm and is spoiled by a vertical one." Synthesis :1317: both candidates converged on the swipe "begun exactly where the drag rule spoiled". The code comment preserves that framing: `GestureRecognizer.swift:242` "A sideways drag of a pair begins the swipe; any other drag spoils the gesture like every drag does." Commit `f2c576f` introduced the branch inline in `.tapping` and `.resting`; `b15701e` extracted `beginOrSpoil` as a review de-duplication with no behaviour change (006 `development-logs.md:43`: "`.tapping`/`.resting` begin-or-spoil branch extracted").
- **[Direct]** "Spoil" means "nothing until every non-thumb finger lifts" because `.spoiled` is plan 001's pre-existing clean-slate state, reused unchanged. Source: `4552ceb` (`case .spoiled(let anchor): if down.keys.allSatisfy({ $0 == anchor.id }) { state = .armed(anchor) }`, still `GestureRecognizer.swift:119-122`); 006 decision 1 "Exits": "Every exit goes to `thumb.map(.spoiled) ?? .idle` … Both are 'nothing further until every non-thumb finger has lifted'."
- **[Direct]** Vertical two-finger movement under the anchored thumb is, by requirement, *scrolling*, suppressed only inside the 3-second blocking window and expected to work again after it. Source: 006 R12 :286 (suppressed "under exactly the conditions that block clicks … Scrolling MUST work again at once when that condition no longer holds"); scenario :289 "Vertical movement inside the window does not scroll" (and no switcher appears); scenario :294 "**Scrolling returns after 3 seconds**: rests the thumb in the anchor corner for 4 seconds, then scrolls up with two fingers → the page scrolls"; Solution :17 "Vertical scrolling is suppressed inside that window too: one rule for clicks and scrolling, so the user learns a single 3-second window." Test T68(b) pins it (`thumb.wait(4 s).land(fingers: 2)` → scroll passes). Plan 001's hardware check recorded the baseline: "during a two-finger drag with the thumb anchored the page scrolls as normal" (001 plan.md :1507).
- **[Direct]** The farthest-moved finger was chosen for determinism and because the rival design missed a third finger landing on the begin frame. Source: 006 Synthesis :1317: candidate 1 was right on "a `Pair` constructed as exactly two with 'none joining on this frame' and a deterministic farthest-finger mover (candidate 2 iterated a dictionary and missed a third finger landing on the begin frame)"; 006 dev log Plan, cross-judge: "a dictionary-ordered mover and a third finger missed on the begin frame". Code: `GestureRecognizer.swift:337` "The farthest-moved finger's displacement, when it is more than `dragThresholdMM`. Deterministic: the maximum."; `:373` "Ordered by id, so everything derived from it is deterministic." R3's wording judges one finger ("one of the two fingers has moved more than 3 mm … with that movement more sideways"), not the midpoint.
- **[Direct]** Direction is judged once, on the frame the 3 mm boundary is crossed, by a strict 45° split, and tests pin both. Source: 006 T8 ("the boundary rows straddle abs(dx) > abs(dy) by 0.1 mm at the frame the drag passes 3 mm"); `SwipeRecognizerTests.swift:38` `movingUpOrDownFirstIsNeverASwipeAndTheNextTapStillFires` (`(0, 10)` then `(20, 0)` → no phases, next tap fires), `:44` `theMoveMustBeMoreSidewaysThanVerticalWhereItPassesThreeMillimetres` (`(3.0, 2.9)` begins, `(2.9, 3.0)` does not), `:82-91` `aRestingPairThatIsDisturbedOrMovesVerticallyNeverSwipes` (all blame `10b0e91`, PR #5). `Move.isSideways` is `abs(dxMM) > abs(dyMM)` (`GestureRecognizer.swift:335`).
- **[Direct]** Once a swipe is on, the vertical component feeds only the speed, never the travel; the axis is fixed at begin. Source: `f38cf95`; `PointerCurve.swift:91-100` (`cursorPoints` is "Cursor points the pointer moves along x", `dy` enters only `hypot(dx, dy)`), `:144-145` (`.linear` returns `dxMM`); `SwipePhase.moved(travelMM:)` carries one scalar (`Gesture.swift:28-30`); `PointerCurveTests.swift:47` asserts `.linear.travel(dxMM: 3, dyMM: 9, interval: 1) == 3`.
- **[Direct]** The record contradicts itself over time on what macOS makes of thumb-plus-two-fingers, and the later finding won. 006 grounding (`.temp/architect/grounding/index.md:64`, F33): "on the hardware, a thumb anchored plus two fingers sliding produces ordinary two-finger scrolling (horizontal and vertical) and nothing else; no Spaces or page-swipe gesture fires." After merge: 006 `development-logs.md:45`: "User report after merge review: swiping in the switcher sometimes also moved to another desktop. Not reproduced (intermittent) … macOS can count a thumb that landed recently or shifts as a finger, so thumb plus two sliding fingers reads as that three-finger swipe." Fix `9044785` ("Block the system's desktop swipes during gestures") added `DockSwipeFilter` covering "the system's own trackpad swipe between desktops or full-screen apps, or into Mission Control and App Exposé" (`DockSwipeFilter.swift:3-4`), blocked "while a thumb is anchored with another finger down, or a swipe is on" (`GestureRecognizer.swift:18-22`), only through the tap, so only with Accessibility granted. The dev log adds: "The field numbers are undocumented and taken from reverse-engineered Dock swipe tools; a hardware check must confirm the tap sees and drops real Dock swipes." No later record of that check was found.
- **[Direct]** Three comments are stale relative to the shipped behaviour and would mislead a vertical-axis change: `Gesture.swift:26` (`.began`: "moved sideways past 3 mm"), `:28-29` (`.moved`: "Sideways movement of the two fingers' midpoint … in trackpad millimetres": it is the accelerated mean per-finger move in step millimetres since `f38cf95`), `:31` (`.ended`: "The first of the two fingers lifted": since `f38cf95` the swipe ends when both have lifted, `GestureRecognizer.swift:143-146`); likewise `Switcher.swift:13`.

#### Q2. Why the Accessibility APIs are confined to one adapter file; why "one permission, used only for click blocking"; what earlier decisions say about a second permission and a second use of Accessibility

- **[Direct]** The no-permission rule began as a product requirement with a stated motive. Source: `plans/001-trackpad-launcher-app/plan.md:9`: "A launcher only earns a place in the menu bar if it costs nothing to keep. One that asks for Accessibility, phones home, logs, or burns CPU while idle gets uninstalled."; R20 :401 "MUST work without asking the user for any system permission (no Accessibility, Input Monitoring or other prompt)"; design decision 9 :556 "*Rules are enforced by structure.* … `SourcePolicyTests` scans every source file for … permission APIs and fails on any hit." Commit `4552ceb` banned `AXIsProcessTrusted`, `tapCreate`, `CGEventTapCreate`, `IOHIDManager`, `CGRequestListenEventAccess`, `CGPreflightListenEventAccess` under rule "permission"; `ccd4654` wrote "The app asks for no permission." into `docs/architecture.md`. The 001 dev log records the motivating paragraph was added in review ("Problem statement did not motivate privacy, permissions, idle cost → paragraph added").
- **[Direct]** Window manipulation through Accessibility was considered for "bring to front" in plan 001 and explicitly dropped in favour of Dock-click semantics, with the permission rule winning. Source: 001 R12 :230: "the result MUST be exactly what clicking the app's Dock icon does: unhide it, restore its minimised windows, open a new window if it has none, switch to the desktop its window is on … If some part of this cannot be done without a permission prompt, R20 wins and that part is dropped."; 001 `development-logs.md:7`: "R12 'restore its windows' was ambiguous, had no other-desktop case, and could conflict with R20 (no permissions) → R12 restated as 'exactly what clicking the Dock icon does', R20 wins on conflict." 008 R7 keeps those semantics; `WorkspaceActions.swift:13` "One call reproduces a Dock click".
- **[Direct]** Windows and previews were scoped out three times. Source: 006 Non-goals :483 "**Running or hidden indicators, or window previews, in the switcher.** Just icons and the selected name." (added in review, dev log :19); 008 Non-goals :251 "**Windows instead of apps.** ⌘-Tab switches apps, not windows; so does the switcher. Picking a specific window is a different feature."; 009 Non-goals :287 "**Swapping windows within one app (like ⌘-`).** The swap is app-level, like ⌘-Tab."
- **[Direct]** Plan 002 replaced R20 with exactly one optional permission whose use is bounded to click blocking, at the human's instruction. Source: 002 R9 :180-181: "**Accessibility is the only permission, and it is optional.** Trackpad Launcher MUST NOT ask for any permission other than Accessibility. Every feature except click blocking MUST work without it. With access granted, the app MUST use it only to block clicks as described here. It MUST NOT read, record or change keyboard input, scrolling, pointer movement or any other event…"; 002 `development-logs.md:12` "Accessibility use was unbounded → app uses it only to block clicks, never reads or changes other events."; the human's answer in 002 `task.md`: "Accessibility permission optional, asked on first launch, hint in the menu bar window otherwise; block clicks only (not pointer or scroll)". Why that permission at all: dev log :18 "an active mouse-button event tap returns nil from an untrusted process and a listen-only one is created disabled (probed) → Accessibility is the permission the tap needs".
- **[Direct]** The `Confined` table is plan 002's mechanism, and its R7/R9 tags are 002's requirement IDs. Source: 002 design decision 8 "*Rules stay structure.* The scanner stops banning the two Accessibility APIs the design uses, confines each to the single file that owns it, adds bans for what R6 and R9 forbid"; 002 Synthesis: candidate 1 won partly "on the stronger policy mechanism (confining each permission API to one file)"; the 002 sketch (:1080-1101) is textually the table `b43619b` shipped: "/// Each permission API lives in exactly one file, the adapter that owns it. Any other hit fails." with `AXIsProcessTrusted` → `SystemAccessibilityPermission.swift` ("R9 one permission, one adapter"), `notify_register` ("R7 the trust push", R7 = "Blocking follows the permission live"), `tapCreate`/`CGEventTapCreate` → `ClickTap.swift` ("R9 the one event tap"). The architecture sentence was written by 002 Phase D (plan.md ~:1115) and landed in `d207504`.
- **[Direct]** Every later widening of what Accessibility does amended that one sentence and stayed inside the one event tap; the second widening was done without a second permission on purpose. Source: 006 Solution amends 002 R9 ("scroll events are read, and those of a suppressed scroll are changed, under the one Accessibility permission"); 006 decision 12 "*The gate moves the token; it does not weaken it.* `scrollWheel` leaves `banned` and becomes `Confined(… onlyIn: "ClickTap.swift")`" (shipped in `9d2f330`); 006 Tradeoffs :1343: "We accept that the tap wakes for every scroll event in the session while armed (F19), in exchange for one tap and no second permission."; `9044785` extended the sentence again for Dock swipes. Current `docs/architecture.md:25`: "uses it only to block trackpad clicks, suppress trackpad scrolling and block the system's desktop and Mission Control swipes during gestures. It never asks for Input Monitoring or any other permission … works fully without it except click blocking, scroll suppression and Dock swipe blocking." `ClickTap.swift:6-7`: "The app's one event tap".
- **[Direct]** "Works without the permission" was restated for every gesture since. Source: 006 R12 "Without the Accessibility permission, nothing is suppressed and the swipe still works."; 009 dev log Specify "No Accessibility permission was unspecified → R9: the swap still works, nothing is blocked."; `Ports.swift` `TrackpadHardware.run` doc: "If the system refuses it, nothing is filtered … and taps behave exactly as without access."
- **[Direct]** Permission-free private APIs were preferred and given the same one-file confinement. Source: 008 decision 2 and `LaunchServicesOrder.swift:4-7` ("no link flags, no entitlements, no prompt; the app is not sandboxed … a missing one fails the whole load, so a macOS that drops them reads as 'no order' instead of crashing"); `08b2f05` added two `Confined` rows ("the private ⌘-Tab order has one reader"); `docs/architecture.md:26`.
- **[Direct]** The scan does not enforce what the architecture sentence claims for window APIs, and nothing anywhere mentions Screen Recording. `SourcePolicyTests.swift:64-76` confines only `AXIsProcessTrusted`, `notify_register`, `tapCreate`, `CGEventTapCreate`, `scrollWheel` and the two LaunchServices symbols; `banned` (:13-55) has no `AXUIElement*`, `CGWindowList*`, `ScreenCaptureKit`, `CGRequestScreenCaptureAccess` or `CGPreflightScreenCaptureAccess` tokens. `git grep` for `screen recording|ScreenCapture|AXUIElement|AXRaise|kAXMinimized|CGWindowList` over `LauncherKit`, `TrackpadLauncher`, `docs`: no hits; `git log --all -S'AXUIElement'` and `-S'Screen Recording'` over the same paths: no commits; the plans grep found only 002's grounding citing `AXUIElement.h` for `AXIsProcessTrustedWithOptions` and a 004 manual script counting the app's own windows with `CGWindowListCopyWindowInfo`. The list self-check that pinned "both halves of the plan 002 edit" was removed in `12c14b7` (PR #7: "`noSourceUses` already catches an overlap that matters").
- **[Direct]** Surrounding rules a second permission or a new AX use would meet: `docs/architecture.md:20` "No timers, no polling, no background loops: every input is a push" (`Timer`, `asyncAfter`, `Task.sleep`, `makeTimerSource`, `displayLink` banned); `:13` "Framework and wire types never cross a port … External data is parsed into domain types at the adapter."; `SystemAccessibilityPermission.swift:5-7`: tccd's broadcast "names no app and no service, so trust is re-read on every broadcast and `onChange` runs only when the value changed. No polling." The user-facing promise is scoped to blocking: `Copy.swift:12-13` "Clicks and scrolling aren't blocked during gestures. Allow Accessibility access to block them."

#### Q3. Why travel is cursor points through the pointer curve (100 per icon), half-icon switching and a rubber band that forgets travel past the end

- **[Direct]** The original rule was millimetres, 12 mm per icon, nearest icon, hard stops with a small rubber band, and the recognizer was kept ignorant of the row. Source: 006 R6 :135 ("icons are 12 mm of travel apart … The selected app MUST be the icon the highlight is nearest to, so the selection changes … 6 mm after the swipe began, then every 12 mm … Travel is measured in trackpad millimetres, the same on every trackpad"); 006 decision 4 :642 "*Travel crosses the port and the selection is decided on main.* The recognizer knows millimetres and nothing about rows. The row depends on the frontmost app and on the catalog, main-actor facts, so the selection belongs to the pure `Switcher` in core"; Alternatives :1348 rejected "The recognizer told the row length and emitting the selection" because it "Exposes a main-to-frame channel mid-gesture". Nearest-icon came from the Specify review resolving two readings of R6 ("R6 read as two selection rules (step every 12 mm vs nearest icon) → rewritten: icons are 12 mm apart, the nearest wins"), on a brief that asked to "indicate the swipe progress, so you can feel how much you need to swipe to other icons, and in general feel that swiping responsive" (006 `task.md`). Shipped in `f2c576f`/`cb7b432` (`Switcher.swift:30-31`: "round(travel / 12 mm), half away from zero, clamped. Changes at ±6 mm, then every 12 mm").
- **[Direct]** The first change (speed acceleration) answered a reach problem found on hardware, and the overshoot cap was added because acceleration made overshoot pile up. Source: `5034e95` "Far icons were hard to reach within the trackpad's width. Swipe travel now grows with finger speed, like pointer acceleration … Travel past the row's ends is capped at a quarter icon, so turning back moves the selection at once."; 006 `development-logs.md:46` "User request after hardware testing: far icons were hard to reach within the trackpad's width → swipe acceleration … Because acceleration made overshoot past the row's ends pile up, the switcher now keeps at most a quarter icon of travel past either end and drops the rest". `GestureRules.swift:30-32` still carries that commit's wording for `switcherMaxOvershoot`.
- **[Direct]** The second change, the same day, replaced the hand-tuned curve with macOS's own because the user asked for the cursor's speed and the fixed curve "was not that". Source: 006 `development-logs.md:47`: "User request: swipe speed should match the cursor's, at the user's tracking speed if possible → the fixed 1×–3× curve was not that, so it is replaced by macOS's own pointer acceleration, reproduced from IOHIDFamily … Travel is cursor points, 100 per icon (the icon pitch). The tracking speed has no push (KVO … and the defaults-changed notification were both probed and never fire for an external write), so it is read at each swipe start: a read on a user event, not a poll."; `f38cf95`; `PointerCurve.swift:3-6` ("so the switcher's highlight moves exactly as far as the cursor would for the same finger movement"), `:139-140` ("The switcher's icon pitch, 80 pt icons with 20 pt between: the highlight moves exactly like the cursor over the row." `shippedPointsPerIcon = 100.0`); `CursorTravel.swift:5-6` ("Read here so no IOKit or preferences type reaches the core"), `:11-13`; `FrameStream.swift:35-38` (curve installed on the `began` frame; "Travel is 0 on the frame a swipe begins"). 006 Non-goals :468 was amended to record it ("R6's fixed 12 mm per icon no longer holds and a fast swipe travels farther than a slow one").
- **[Direct]** The core still works in "step millimetres" (12 per icon); `.cursor` converts points to that unit, and every recognizer test runs `.linear`. Source: `PointerCurve.swift:132` ("Travel is in step millimetres: 12 per icon"), `:146-148`; `GestureRecognizer.swift:324`; `MultitouchTrackpads.swift:90` (recognizers are built `.linear` and switched at begin); 008 decision 10 "The core's travel (12 mm, or 100 cursor points, per icon) never sees the layout, so the finger travel per icon is unchanged by construction." This is why 100 points per icon survived the row shrink (`docs/domain-model.md` "Switcher": "whatever the icons' size on screen"; `SwitcherOnScreenTests.swift:130` `thirtyRunningAppsStillTakeTwelveMillimetresPerIcon`), and 008 Non-goals accept the consequence ("Reaching the far end of a very long row with a slow slide. It may take a fast slide").
- **[Direct]** The rubber band's "forget the overshoot on the turn" is plan 008's literal reading of its own R4, replacing 006's rule, and flagged for the owner to confirm. Source: 008 R4 :99-100 "Turning back right MUST move the selection after the usual travel for one icon. The rubber band MUST NOT add a dead zone."; decision 3 :274 "`follow` keeps at most a quarter icon (3 mm) of travel past either end for the rubber band, as today, and forgives it the moment the fingers turn back … so the next selection change comes the usual 6 mm from the turn, however far the fingers went past the end … it replaces plan 006's rule, under which 3 mm of the way back undid the band first."; Further Notes :343 "**R4 is read literally, for the owner to confirm.** … if the owner meant plan 006's rule after all, they revert to 9 mm and the band is kept on reversal." Shipped in `08b2f05` (no commit body); `Switcher.swift:37-42`; `SwitcherOnScreenTests.swift:447` `pushingLeftPastTheFrontmostAppStretchesAndTheNextAppComesSevenMillimetresAfterTheTurn`.
- **[Direct]** Plan text and code disagree on units and on the swipe's end, because the requirements were amended by annotation rather than rewritten: 006 R6 still says trackpad millimetres and 12 mm; 006 R8 :180 still says "A swipe ends when the first of the two fingers lifts" while the amended non-goal, `f38cf95`, `GestureRecognizer.swift:143-146` and the glossary say both fingers; 008 mixes "12 mm" and "100 cursor points". An open question from 006 (:1365) was never answered: "R6 measures travel from where the swipe began, so … the first selection change comes about 9 mm after the fingers landed (3 mm to begin, then 6 mm). Should travel instead count from the landing midpoint?"

#### Q4. Why the switcher panel lives in `LauncherUI` while the launch overlay lives in `LauncherPlatform`; why `NSGlassEffectView` in an `NSPanel`; why icons are drawn off-main before any swipe

- **[Direct]** The launch overlay is an *effect the core requests through a port*, so it lives with the adapter that plays it. Source: `plans/004-launch-icon-animation/plan.md:224` "No new port. The animation is one more effect the core decides and macOS performs, which is what `SystemActions` already describes."; 004 dev log Plan: "Four public LauncherCore types plus two custom geometry values existed only for the overlay → the flight moved into LauncherPlatform as internal types"; `fb5edcc` ("LaunchOverlay, a click-through, non-activating panel owned by WorkspaceActions and closed from Core Animation's completion"); `Ports.swift:48` "Effects the core decides and macOS performs."
- **[Direct]** The switcher is *model state rendered by the shell*, and the effect-through-a-port shape was considered and rejected as shallow. Source: 006 decision 8 :646 "*The switcher is model state rendered by `LauncherUI`, with AppKit and Core Animation.* The model owns which apps are shown, which one is selected, when the switcher shows, and every effect; a test reads `launcher.switcher` with no fake. `SwitcherPresenter`, owned by `MenuBarShell`, iterates `Observations({ launcher.switcher })`…"; Alternatives :1349 "**The switcher as an effect behind `SystemActions` (`show`, `update`, `hide`) played by `LauncherPlatform`.** Three methods mirroring model state, callers coordinating several calls per swipe, state split between the model and the adapter, tests reading a recording fake instead of the model. Hides nothing the model-state shape does not. Rejected as shallow; the overlay precedent is for stateless effects."; `docs/architecture.md:11` "the AppKit/SwiftUI shell renders the model's state and forwards user intents"; `Launcher.swift:11-12`; `SwitcherPresenter.swift:5` "Renders `launcher.switcher` and holds nothing the model holds."
- **[Direct]** The panel configuration is copied from the overlay rather than shared because the module graph forbids the import. Source: 006 Tradeoffs :1337 "We accept that the switcher panel repeats the launch overlay's ten-line panel configuration in `LauncherUI`, because `LauncherUI` does not depend on `LauncherPlatform`. The recipe is cited, not shared."; 008 dev log Implement: a fix pass that made `LauncherUI` import `LauncherPlatform` was undone ("a new module dependency, so the spelling moved to the core"); `SwitcherPanel.swift:5-7`.
- **[Direct]** AppKit layers plus Core Animation (not SwiftUI) because of the architecture's animation rule. Source: 006 Alternatives :1350 "**SwiftUI for the switcher's content.** Declarative, with `glassEffect` and environment Reduce motion, and less code. But its springs and scale animations are not Core Animation keyframes, the architecture's animation rule, and the overlay precedent is Core Animation. Kept for the window, not used for the switcher."; Tradeoffs :1338; `docs/architecture.md:21` "Animation is Core Animation keyframes, which the render server plays … No code of ours runs per frame, and display links are banned" (rule and `displayLink` ban from plan 004, `fb5edcc`); `b15701e` replaced a `panel.animator()` fade with a Core Animation fade after review.
- **[Direct]** Glass is a product requirement; `NSGlassEffectView` follows the launcher window's precedent, and whether it renders in this kind of panel was left to a manual check. Source: `docs/product-overview.md` ("latest liquid glass human interface design"); 001 Design chose `NSGlassEffectView` for `LauncherPanel` after `task.md` item 8 offered "`NSGlassEffectView` or `.glassEffect`" with no reason recorded; 006 Open questions :1367 "Does `NSGlassEffectView` render inside a `.screenSaver`-level, non-key, click-through panel, and does a glass highlight read well over a glass panel in light and dark? Manual R10 check; the fallback is a translucent material capsule."; Verified without a test R10 :1500 "Why not a test: window-server output." The 006 dev log :38 records every hardware and on-screen check "still unticked (no hardware available to the implementers)". The rounded-clip fix is shared in `GlassClip.swift` (`85ea6f2`, moved by `b15701e`).
- **[Direct]** Icons are drawn before any swipe because probes measured the first draw as too slow for a gesture, and a requirement forbids the delay. ### What We Can Reasonably Infer

- **[Inferred]** The module placement follows one rule that the plans state only by example: an *effect the core requests through a port* lives with its adapter in `LauncherPlatform` (overlay); *observable model state the shell renders* lives in `LauncherUI` (switcher). Reasoning: 004 :224 and 006 :1349 give those two reasons for the two cases, and 006 says "the overlay precedent is for stateless effects". The general rule is not written in `docs/architecture.md`, so it is our reading of two consistent decisions.
- **[Inferred]** A vertical window swipe with R3's "no time limit" would directly collide with 006 R12's scenario "Scrolling returns after 3 seconds". Reasoning: that scenario (rest the thumb 4 s, scroll up with two fingers → the page scrolls; T68(b)) is exactly the finger pattern that would begin a vertical swipe, and a swipe suppresses scrolling for its whole length (006 R13; `9d2f330`). This is the same harm 006 decision 3 built the `swipes` gate to prevent on the sideways axis for users "with an empty list"; with the swipe setting on by default (008), every user would meet it. The plans never discuss a vertical swipe as an option, so this is an inference from combining requirements, not a recorded decision.
- **[Inferred]** Thumb plus two fingers sliding up or down is likely to be read by macOS as its three-finger *vertical* swipe (Mission Control / App Exposé), which the Dock-swipe filter already drops but only with Accessibility granted. Reasoning: `9044785` and dev log :45 establish that macOS can count the anchored thumb as a finger for the horizontal three-finger swipe; `DockSwipeFilter.swift:3-4` already lists Mission Control and App Exposé among the swipes it drops; `blocksDockSwipes` is true "while any thumb is anchored and another finger is down" and for the whole of a swipe; all of it runs in `ClickTap`, which exists only with trust. Without the permission, a vertical window swipe would plausibly open Mission Control. The dev log's "a hardware check must confirm the tap sees and drops real Dock swipes" has no recorded follow-up.
- **[Inferred]** Vertical drift of a two- or three-finger tap past 3 mm would start flashing the window list, where today it spoils silently. Reasoning: the sideways equivalent is an accepted, tested cost (`SwitcherOnScreenTests.swift:18` `aRollingThreeFingerTapWithDriftShowsTheSwitcherThenRemovesItWithoutOpeningAnything`, `:39` `aTwoFingerTapThatDriftsShowsTheSwitcherBrieflyAndOpensNothing`; 006 T13); a vertical begin at the same 3 mm boundary would reproduce it on the other axis.
- **[Inferred]** The "100 points per icon = the icon pitch, so the highlight moves exactly like the cursor over the row" rationale is already not literal and is tied to the horizontal row's geometry. Reasoning: 008 decision 10 kept 100 points per icon when icons shrink "by construction" because the core never sees the layout; a vertical list with a different pitch would need its own points-per-item or would accept the same non-literal match. Nothing in the record says which.
- **[Inferred]** A vertical travel axis is mechanically a small change in `PointerCurve` but a new contract at the port. Reasoning: `cursorPoints` computes the multiplier from the velocity magnitude and scales only `dx` (`PointerCurve.swift:93-100`, "Reproduces `IOHIDPointerAccelerator::accelerate`"); scaling `dy` by the same multiplier appears to be the symmetric form, but that should be checked against IOHIDFamily rather than assumed. `SwipePhase.moved(travelMM:)` carries one scalar with no axis, and `FrameStream` installs the curve on the `began` frame, so the axis would have to be fixed at begin and carried in or beside `began`.
- **[Inferred]** A second TCC permission would arrive on the same tccd broadcast that `SystemAccessibilityPermission` already listens to, and the `notify_register` confinement means a Screen Recording listener would have to live in that file or the `Confined` row would have to change. Reasoning: `SystemAccessibilityPermission.swift:5-7` ("The notification names no app and no service, so trust is re-read on every broadcast"); `SourcePolicyTests.swift:66` confines `notify_register` to that file. Mechanics, not a stated design.
- **[Inferred]** Raising windows *as the selection passes* would be the first mid-swipe effect on the system; today the only mid-swipe effect is the light tick, and the begin/end double read was designed around a single effect at the lift. Reasoning: `Launcher.swift:204` "The one place a swipe becomes effects"; `.moved` only plays `.lightTick`; 008 decision 4's second read compares the lift target against a fresh read; 009 :292 shows the system's view lags an activation by tens to hundreds of milliseconds. Mid-swipe `AXRaise` calls would change the state the end read compares against and would race the next `.moved`. That is an inference about interaction, not a recorded concern.
- **[Inferred]** Any live-refreshing preview would have to be push-driven and would sit in tension with "no code of ours runs per frame". Reasoning: `docs/architecture.md:20-21` and the `Timer`/`displayLink` bans; the first icon pass is waited for before the run loop starts (`:19`), so previews of every window at start would be a startup cost the record never measured.

### Competing Hypotheses

**Why the vertical first drag spoils rather than waiting or becoming a swipe later.** The record supports two readings that are compatible but not the same decision.

- **Hypothesis A: a deliberate product rule to keep vertical two-finger scrolling usable under an anchored thumb.**
  - Evidence for: 006 Non-goals :470 names scrolling as the reason; 006 R12's scenarios require vertical scrolling to return after 3 s; the Solution :17 explains vertical suppression inside the window as "one rule for clicks and scrolling"; 001's hardware check recorded scrolling under the thumb as the baseline.
  - Evidence against or missing: the non-goal was added in review as a "missing non-goal" (dev log :19) rather than coming from the brief; no plan weighs a vertical swipe against scrolling and rejects it.
- **Hypothesis B: the inherited default, described after the fact.** Every drag already spoiled; the sideways pair was carved out as the minimum change and the vertical case simply stayed "spoiled as today".
  - Evidence for: 006 decision 1 "otherwise it is spoiled as today"; Synthesis "begun exactly where the drag rule spoiled"; the code comment "any other drag spoils the gesture like every drag does"; the human asked for "left or right" and nothing else.
  - Evidence against or missing: R3's explicit vertical sentence and its scenario show the behaviour was consciously specified, not left implicit.

Either way, the change must treat vertical scrolling under the thumb (R12) as the thing being traded away, not as an accident.

**Why the row is fixed at begin.**

- **Hypothesis A: stability under the fingers** (icons must not shift under the highlight mid-swipe). Evidence for: none stated anywhere; only the requirement sentences (006 R4, 008 R5). [Speculative]
- **Hypothesis B: a consequence of "the model keeps none of it".** Evidence for: 008 decision 1 "never stored"; 008 Further Notes "a pull from the source of truth, no state"; the model has no push source for running-app changes (the presenter, not the model, watches `NSWorkspace`, 008 decision 9), so a changing row would need state or a poll, both avoided. ## Constraint set for the window swipe (derived from the findings)

### Preserve

1. **One recognizer, one finger session; the vertical swipe is another branch of the same entry.** Begin it in `beginOrSpoil` at the same 3 mm crossing, from the same farthest-finger `drag`, with direction judged once on the crossing frame by a strict split; keep the `Pair` guard and the swipes-off spoil spoiling (006 decision 1, Synthesis :1317; `GestureRecognizer.swift:242-249`). Do not add a second state machine or a second threshold.
2. **`dragThresholdMM` stays one shared constant** for tap, swipe and thumb tap (001/002/006 non-goals; `GestureRecognizer.swift:218`).
3. **No main-to-frame channel.** The recognizer never learns whether the frontmost app has windows; eligibility is decided on main, and a swipe with nothing to show "still runs its course" blocked until the lift (006 "Deliberately not done"; 008 R8, decision 4; `Launcher.swift:209`). Never restart devices from a gesture.
4. **Travel crosses the port as one scalar in step millimetres, accelerated by the pointer curve installed at begin, with the tracking speed read on that user event** (006 decision 4; `f38cf95`; `FrameStream.swift:35-38`; `CursorTravel.swift:11-13`). Selection is nearest-item, half a step in; the ends are hard stops with a band that forgets overshoot on the turn (006 R6; 008 R4, decision 3). IOKit and preferences types stay in `LauncherPlatform`.
5. **List fixed at begin; system state pulled at begin and again at the lift; nothing stored; a lift that changes nothing is silent** (006 R4; 008 R5, R7, decisions 1 and 4; 009 Solution). A window closed mid-swipe is the analogue of an app that quit: silent, never re-created. Unrelated reconciles must leave the swipe alone (`Launcher.swift:156-157`).
6. **The on-screen list is model state rendered by `LauncherUI`**, owned by `MenuBarShell`, with the presenter holding nothing the model holds; its panel pre-created and its content ready before any swipe; AppKit layers with Core Animation springs and fades; no SwiftUI content, no code per frame, no `LauncherUI` → `LauncherPlatform` import (006 decisions 8 and 9, Alternatives :1349-1350, Tradeoffs :1337-1338; `docs/architecture.md:19-21`).
7. **Ports: no new one-method port.** A window-list read belongs beside `runningApps()` on `SystemActions` and a raise beside `bringToFront`, with matching fields and recordings on `RecordingSystemActions`; the real read lives in its own small platform type constructible alone, like `RunningAppsReader` (002 :1144; 004 :224; 006 decision 6; 008 decision 2). `AXUIElement`, `CGWindowID`, capture images and `NSImage` never cross a port; parse to domain values at the adapter (`docs/architecture.md:13`).
8. **Permission structure, not prose.** Every new sensitive API gets a `Confined` row naming its one adapter file (and bans for what the feature must never do), the Privacy sentence in `docs/architecture.md:25` and the governing requirement are amended in the plan the way 006 amended 002 R9, and the plan states what the gesture does without each permission, as every gesture since 002 has (002 R9; 006 R12; 009 R9; `SourcePolicyTests.swift:4-5`).
9. **Blocking for the whole swipe** (clicks, scrolling, Dock swipes) and Dock-swipe blocking while a thumb is anchored with another finger down (006 R13; `9044785`).
10. **Testing seams:** `World` over `RecordingSystemActions` fields a test sets, the recognizer over `TouchScript`, in-flight activation by default with `World.activate(_:)` to land one (009 decision 10); the presenter, the panel, real AX and real capture under "Verified without a test" with a stated "why not a test" each (006 :1500; 008 :392; 010 `testing.md:32`).

### Avoid

1. A separate `SwipeRecognizer` or a vertical state machine beside the recognizer (006 Alternatives :1347: two owners of one finger session).
2. Telling the recognizer the window count, or any main-to-frame channel (006 "Deliberately not done"; decision 3).
3. Timers, polling, display links or a model-owned `Task` hop for window state or previews (`docs/architecture.md:20-21`; `SourcePolicyTests` bans; 001/003 rejected hops "whose negative tests could not fail").
4. The window list as `show`/`update`/`hide` effects behind `SystemActions` played by `LauncherPlatform` (006 :1349, rejected as shallow), or sharing panel code by importing `LauncherPlatform` from `LauncherUI` (006 :1337; 008 dev log).
5. SwiftUI content for the list (006 :1350; the animation rule).
6. A one-method `WindowList`/`WindowRaiser` port (002 :1144; 006 decision 6), or letting AX or capture types cross a port (`docs/architecture.md:13`).
7. A feature whose core function needs the permission with no stated no-permission behaviour; 002 R9's "Every feature except click blocking MUST work without it" has held for every gesture since.
8. Widening what Accessibility does without amending the requirement and the architecture sentence; the Dock-swipe fix (`9044785`) already left the plan text behind, and a second silent widening would compound it.
9. Re-ordering the list mid-swipe as windows are raised (006 R4; 008 R5), or storing window state in the model between swipes (008 decision 1).
10. Restarting devices (`reconcile`) from a gesture to handle "nothing to show" (008 decision 4 removed exactly that).
11. Changing 3 mm or the 45° split for one axis only (shared constant; 006 T8 boundary rows).
12. Relying on Dock-swipe blocking to keep Mission Control/App Exposé quiet: it exists only with Accessibility granted (`ClickTap`), and its field numbers are unconfirmed on hardware (006 dev log :45).

### Risk

1. **Scrolling under the thumb disappears on the vertical axis.** Every anchored-thumb vertical two-finger scroll past the 3 s window becomes a swipe that suppresses scrolling for its length, for every user (swipe on by default since 008); this is the harm 006 decision 3 built the `swipes` gate to prevent sideways. Needs a product decision, not a default.
2. **macOS's three-finger vertical swipe.** Thumb plus two vertical fingers is the shape of Mission Control/App Exposé; the filter drops it only with trust, and the user already saw the horizontal version fire intermittently (dev log :45). Without the permission the gesture may open Mission Control; with it, the unconfirmed field numbers carry the risk.
3. **Vertical drift flashes.** Two- and three-finger taps that drift vertically past 3 mm would show the window list briefly, doubling an already-accepted sideways cost (`SwitcherOnScreenTests.swift:18`, `:39`).
4. **Mid-swipe raises race the system's state.** Activation lags tens to hundreds of milliseconds (009 :292); raising on each pass changes what the lift's second read compares against and what the next frame's raise sees. The begin/end double-read pattern was designed for one effect at the lift.
5. **A first mandatory permission and a first second permission.** 001's problem statement says apps that ask for Accessibility get uninstalled; 002 made it optional and bounded; 006 chose "one tap and no second permission". Window enumeration and raising through AX cannot be done without trust, and previews need Screen Recording: both contradict 002 R9 and `docs/architecture.md:25` as written. The TCC broadcast is shared (`SystemAccessibilityPermission.swift:5-7`), so a second permission's live follow-up needs its own re-read in or beside that confined file.
6. **The gate is silent on the new APIs.** No `AXUIElement*`, `CGWindowList*`, `ScreenCaptureKit` or screen-capture-access token is banned or confined (`SourcePolicyTests.swift:13-76`), and the list self-check was removed (`12c14b7`); a second file using them would pass the scan while the architecture claims otherwise.
7. **Unverified ground under minimised and other-Space windows.** PR #11's hand checks for hidden, minimised, windowless and other-Space apps were never recorded as done, and the switcher's own on-screen checks (glass in a `.screenSaver` non-key panel, glass on glass, light/dark, Reduce motion) are recorded only as unticked; `kAXMinimized` and cross-Space raising add new untested surface in the same place.
8. **Travel-axis plumbing.** `cursorPoints` is x-only and the 006 open question on where travel starts (~9 mm to the first change) is unanswered; a vertical list with a different pitch will expose both.
9. **Previews versus the readiness rules.** Icons are ready before any swipe because a first draw cost 25 to 220 ms (F4; 008 :260); the frontmost app is known only at begin, so previews either cost at begin (unmeasured; a probe in the plan's style is the precedent) or must be captured ahead for every app's windows (a startup cost the record never contemplated); live refresh collides with "no code of ours runs per frame". [Speculative on magnitudes; the costs are unmeasured.]
10. **Plan-text drift.** Three requirement texts already disagree with shipped behaviour (006 R6 units, 006 R8 end-of-swipe, 006 R12 vs Dock-swipe scope); a change this wide should rewrite, not annotate, or the next investigator inherits a fourth.
