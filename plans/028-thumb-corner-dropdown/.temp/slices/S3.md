# S3: The illustrations draw the thumb in the chosen corner

## Goal

With a bottom corner chosen, every illustration in the settings window (the five gesture rows, *Switch recent*, *Switch windows* and *Toggle recent*) shows the thumb's mark in that corner of its card: the top-corner picture with its marks flipped top to bottom, on the same paper card with the same lighting. Switching corners redraws them at once. The script that draws the derived copies is generalised and renamed, the 16 new PNGs are committed (by the orchestrator, from your working tree), and the view maps the corner to its file.

The repository root is `/Users/dominykasgrubys/code/trackpad-launcher/.claude/worktrees/028-thumb-corner-dropdown`; the Swift package is `LauncherKit/`. Work only there.

## Requirements

**R6. Illustrations draw the thumb in the chosen corner.** Every illustration in the settings window MUST show the thumb in the chosen corner: the five gesture rows, *Switch recent*, *Switch windows* and *Toggle recent*. Bottom-corner illustrations are the top-corner ones with their marks flipped top to bottom, on the same paper card with the same lighting.
- Given *Bottom right*, every illustration shows the thumb's mark in the bottom-right of its card.
- Given the window open with *Top left*, picking *Bottom left* makes every illustration show the thumb's mark bottom-left at once.

## Design

### The assets (`assets/`)

Four resource names per illustration, for each of the 8 names `gesture-1` … `gesture-5`, `swipe`, `window-swipe`, `thumb-tap`:
- `<name>.png`: the Figma original, thumb top-left. Never written by the script.
- `<name>-inverse.png`: top-right; marks mirrored in x. Exists today.
- `<name>-bottom.png`: bottom-left; marks mirrored in y. New.
- `<name>-bottom-inverse.png`: bottom-right; marks mirrored in x and y. New.

16 new PNGs beside the 16 that exist, at the same pixel size and DPI. `project.yml` already bundles `assets/*.png` into the app, so no project change is needed.

### The script (`Scripts/inverse-illustrations.swift` → rename with `git mv` to a name that says it draws every corner, e.g. `Scripts/corner-illustrations.swift`)

Generalise it to draw all three derived copies of each original by the same method it uses today: compute the mark mask (luminance below 180 inside a 5 px rim, grown by 2 px), infill the card behind the marks, then write card × (1 − mirrored coverage), so the card and its lighting are untouched. The mirror is applied in x, in y, or both (one loop parameterised by the mirror, not three copies of the loop). Its "a mirrored mark falls off the card" precondition applies in every direction. It reports each file it writes (`print("assets/<file>.png")`), 24 lines. Update its header comment (it now draws every corner's copy, and says the bottom copies are mirrored top to bottom) and its usage line. Compute the card infill once per original and reuse it for the three copies.

Run it from the repository root (`swift Scripts/<new-name>.swift`) to produce the files. Then check that the 8 `-inverse` files it rewrote decode to the same pixels as the committed ones (T23's reader does this, or `git diff --stat assets/` shows them unchanged if the encoder is byte-stable); if they differ, the generalisation changed the top-right output, which it must not.

### The view (`LauncherKit/Sources/LauncherUI/SettingsView.swift`)

The illustration-name rule on `ThumbCorner` (internal to `LauncherUI`, today `func illustration(_ name: String) -> String` mapping left corners to `name` and right corners to `name-inverse`) maps:
- `topLeft` → `name`
- `topRight` → `name + "-inverse"`
- `bottomLeft` → `name + "-bottom"`
- `bottomRight` → `name + "-bottom-inverse"`

`Illustration` already reads the corner through this rule and the views already pass `launcher.thumbCorner`, so a corner change redraws every illustration at once through observation; confirm the wiring, don't add a second path. A missing resource still draws nothing rather than failing (`BundleImage.view` behaviour; keep it). Update the comments that describe the rule (e.g. `SwitcherSection.swift`'s "the inverse copy for the top-right corner").
