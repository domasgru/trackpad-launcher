## 1. Facts a design decision rests on

### The platform: what an event tap costs and what `tapEnable` does

| # | Fact | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | A tap disabled with `CGEvent.tapEnable(tap:enable:false)` is sent no events (0 of 2000 posted scroll events reached the callback) and its thread uses no CPU (0.000 ms), while the process cost stayed at the no-tap baseline. **Conclusion:** a disabled tap costs what no tap costs; disabling is the mechanism that reaches R1's budget. | probed: `probes/tap-gating/main.swift` phase C, runs 1 and 2 |

| P3 | `tapEnable` called from a thread that is neither main nor the tap's thread works: the tap read disabled/enabled accordingly, the callback count followed, and an event posted immediately after `tapEnable(true)` returned was delivered. ## 2. Sources by design question

**Q-A. What stops the events, and what does it cost?** P1-P5, P7, P11. A single tap toggled with `tapEnable` is sufficient and complete; reinstalling the tap per transition is unnecessary (P3 shows a toggle at ~0.1 ms with synchronous effect); changing the mask is impossible (P11). A permanently enabled tap over presses only is free during ordinary use (P2). Settled.

**Q-B. Which types may ever be gated?** C7 (the press rule), C1 (volumes), R1's list (6, 7, 27, 34, 22, 29, 30). Presses and releases must reach the filter whenever a tap gesture or swap could complete; P2 makes keeping them always on free. Whether that means two taps (one permanent over 1, 2, 3, 4, 25, 26; one gated over the rest) or one tap with a gate that is open whenever a thumb is anchored is a design choice; the facts to weigh are C7 (a single gated tap must never leave `holding == .nothing` while a press passed unseen could be read), C5 (no warning frame before blocking), C16 (a second `tapCreate` in the same file passes the policy; the rule label changes) and C2 (one thread can hold two sources). Open, with the evidence above.

**Q-C. When must the gate be open?** C4, C5, C6, C8, C9, C11, C18, R1's wording ("no thumb anchored, no swipe or window swipe in progress, no swap series" and "no filter has a press, scroll gesture (including its momentum) or Dock swipe still withheld or altered in flight"). Two inputs: (1) per trackpad, a new recognizer output that is true whenever the state holds a thumb (`.armed/.tapping/.resting/.spoiled`, `rest.thumb`, an unconfirmed swipe's thumb) or is a confirmed swipe, window swipe or series, so it is already true on the frame before any blocking verdict can appear and on the first-frame cases; the invariant `blocksClicks ⇒ blocksDockSwipes ⇒ this` should be encoded, not commented. (2) Per filter, "something withheld or altered in flight": any withheld `ClickFilter` entry; a `ScrollFilter` stage in `.dropped` or `.stilled` (touching or coasting, through `momentum(.ended)`, about a second after the lift, P10); any `DockSwipeFilter` with `current == .drop`. The gate is the OR. Settled as facts; the shape is the design's.

**Q-D. Who applies `tapEnable`, and how are the two input writers serialized?** C3, C14, C15, P3, P4, P6. The gate's inputs are written by frame threads (verdicts) and the tap thread (filter memory). The ordering race to close: the frame thread sees the verdict drop and nothing in flight at the same moment the tap thread is committing a drop or a still for an event it read the raised flag for; if the two decisions are not one critical section, the gate closes on an in-flight gesture (R3). Two shapes satisfy the facts: (i) one `Mutex` holding the per-trackpad wants, the filters' memory and the applied state, with the thread that changes an input applying `tapEnable` under the lock (P3 and P4 show the call is cheap and does not wait on our own callback, so holding the lock across it cannot deadlock on the tap thread; this makes two threads callers of `tapEnable`, which bends C14's letter and plan 002's rule, C15); (ii) the tap thread as the sole owner of `tapEnable`, woken by a run-loop source the frame threads signal when their want changes, applying the gate when it wakes or after each event (keeps C14 and 002:437 intact; adds a wake-up of ~0.1 ms to the open latency). Open; the design must pick and say why.

**Q-E. What happens to the filters when the gate closes and reopens?** C8-C11, C12, P5, P10, `why.md` Avoid 2 (do not reset memory mid-gesture; the gate closes only when nothing load-bearing is in flight, so what remains is `passed`/`pass` memory, C8). On reopen the filters have missed events. The rule that reproduces today's outcomes: a `mayBegin` is a fresh start (dropped under the flag, as T71 pins); a `began` first seen after a reopen under the flag is a gesture whose `mayBegin` apps already got (P10) and is stilled, as today (C11); mid phases still; momentum passes. A plain reset to nil gets C11 wrong; stale `.touching(.passed)` gets `mayBegin` wrong. For the Dock filter the same question hangs on D2 (whether Dock swipes send a `mayBegin`); the conservative choice is to keep nil semantics (drop an opening phase under the flag) and verify on hardware. Open for the Dock filter; settled for scroll.

**Q-F. Timeouts, our own disable's echo, lifecycle.** P6, P7, C12, C13, R4. The handler must distinguish the echo of our own disable (ignore: nothing was missed, the gate is already off) from a system disable (forget memory as today, then re-apply the gate's wish, enabling only if it wants on). `run` keeps replacing the tap and filters; a fresh tap starts with the gate closed and opens on the first frame that shows a thumb, which covers the first-frame cases (C18: the press right after a grant with no frame before the thumb lands on the thumb's frame, so the gate opens on the step that precedes the sampling of that press only if the gate opens before the press is decided; see Q-B). Settled, except the latency point folded into Q-B.

**Q-G. What the behavioural suite needs.** C17, R2 ("the behavioural suite MUST also hold with the tap's gating modelled"). The fake must model a tap that exists but is not receiving events: events reach its filters only while the pure gate is open, and a test must be able to tell "never delivered" from "passed". The pure gate needs its own unit seam. `TapEventTests` pins the mask(s). Open to the design: the handle it gives the fake.

**Q-H. Docs.** C19: `docs/architecture.md:20` must say when the tap receives events (R5); `:18` only if the design bends the one-writer rule (Q-D (i)). `docs/domain-model.md` *Click blocking* and *Blocking window* describe behaviour that does not change.

## 3. What a test can produce for real, and what needs a fake or a manual check

- **Real, in the behavioural suite:** every verdict in C6-C9 through the real filters; the recognizer's new output and the invariant `blocksClicks ⇒ blocksDockSwipes ⇒ wants-tap` per frame; the pure gate's OR and its transitions from scripted inputs; the modelled gate in the fake (events reach the filters only while open; the R1 scenario asserts no scroll or gesture event is delivered during ordinary pointer and scroll use; the R3 scenarios assert the gate stays open through a withheld press's release, a dropped scroll's momentum, a dropped Dock swipe's end); `run`/revoke/hot-plug/wake/setting paths through `Launcher.reconcile` with the in-memory adapters.
- **Real, in the platform suite:** mask bits and parse tables with synthetic `CGEvent`s (`TapEventTests`); `DeviceSession.receive` flag writes with `clickTap: nil`.
- **Fake or scripted:** the frame-thread/tap-thread interleaving (the fake's one-sided model: a press edge arrives between frames and is decided on the previous frame's flags); the echo and the timeout notifications (a test can feed the gate's state machine the two notification kinds, not the real port).
- **Manual only:** the Release-build CPU per frame against the 0.088 ms budget (proc_pid_rusage, the audit's method); real scroll, Dock swipe and momentum delivery with the gate; the debugger-pause timeout recovery; a disable's echo on the real app.
