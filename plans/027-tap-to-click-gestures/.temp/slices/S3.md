# S3: A tap under the anchored thumb never clicks

Worktree: `/Users/dominykasgrubys/code/trackpad-launcher/.claude/worktrees/027-tap-to-click-gestures` (branch `feature/027-tap-to-click-gestures`). Use absolute paths. Do not commit; the orchestrator commits your work. All source paths below are under `LauncherKit/`.

## Goal

With Tap to click on and the Accessibility permission, every gesture, swipe, window swipe and swap leaves no click behind: the click macOS makes at the lift, the right click it makes up to 0.3 s later, a click after a thumb rested for seconds, three quick gestures in a row, and a tap-and-hold drag begun within half a second are all swallowed, whether or not the thumb is still down. A tap with no thumb, a tap a second after the thumb has gone, and a physical press after the thumb's first 3 s click as they do today.

The mechanism is a **lift window**: the recognizer flags a frame on which a finger that macOS could read as a tap lifted inside a thumb touch; the session stamps the window's end (receipt time + 0.5 s) on the process's monotonic clock; the click tap blocks presses while any trackpad's window is open (only when the run asked to block tap clicks) and holds its gated port on through the window.

## Requirements

- **R2.** While Tap to click is on, a click that macOS makes for a tap of other fingers that landed while the thumb was anchored MUST NOT reach apps. This holds for every finger count, whether a left or a right click, whether or not the gesture has an app assigned, and however long the thumb has rested. It also holds for fingers that start a swipe or window swipe but lift as a tap. A click that arrives up to 0.5 seconds after those fingers lift MUST be blocked too, whether or not the thumb is still down. This is a plain time window. Any click within it is blocked, including a deliberate tap elsewhere made within 0.5 seconds after the gesture. That cost is accepted. A press that starts in the window, such as a tap-and-hold drag with the Dragging setting on, is blocked whole, from press to release, so nothing is dragged and no button stays down. Its scenarios include a swap under Tap to click making no click.
- **R3.** Outside R2, a tap MUST click as macOS makes it. That covers a tap with no thumb anchored, and a click that arrives more than 0.5 seconds after a tap under the thumb once the thumb has lifted. A physical press (pressing the pad down until it clicks) is unchanged. Under an anchored thumb with another finger down, it is blocked during the first 3 seconds after the thumb landed, and reaches apps after that. During a swipe, window swipe or swap series, it is blocked throughout.
- **R6 (remaining part).** Granting the permission MUST make gestures active within 5 seconds, with no relaunch, and the next gesture fires and makes no click.

Non-goals that constrain you: physical click blocking is unchanged (the 3 s window stays); with Tap to click off nothing new is blocked (no click comes from a tap, so a deliberate press within 0.5 s of a gesture must still pass); scrolling is never suppressed by the lift window.

## Design

### Recognizer (core, clock-free)

- `Recognition` (`Sources/LauncherCore/GestureRecognizer.swift`) gains `opensLiftWindow: Bool`: true on a frame where a thumb touch was on before the frame and a contact other than the thumb lifted that macOS could read as a tap:
  - it had been down for strictly less than `GestureRules.tapActionHoldLimit` (frame-time comparison, like the existing `maxTapDuration` checks); a contact already resting when recognition started (the book's `isRealLanding == false`, unknown landing) never qualifies;
  - and no press, delivered or withheld, was seen on any frame while it was down (`frame.press` on any frame of its life; a finger that physically clicked is no tap to macOS, so its lift makes no tap action). You will need a per-contact "pressed while down" mark in the recognizer's contact book.
  - "The thumb" is the one the state knows: the anchor, the thumb under a rest, or a slide's thumb. A contact that is not that thumb counts.
  - So the window opens on a tap's fingers lifting (each lift reopens it; the last lift sets its end), on a swipe's or window swipe's fingers lifting quickly, and on a short-lived finger that loses contact mid-slide. It does NOT open on a thumb lifting alone, on fingers that rested or scrolled past the hold limit (a swap series' fingers, a two-finger scroll under a rested thumb), on a finger that physically pressed (a double click under a rested thumb keeps both clicks), nor on any lift outside a thumb touch. Edge contacts are ignored as everywhere else.
- `GestureRules` (`Sources/LauncherCore/GestureRules.swift`) gains:
  - `liftWindow = Duration.milliseconds(500)`: "strictly less is inside", like `clickBlockingWindow`.
  - `tapActionHoldLimit = Duration.milliseconds(400)`: the longest hold for which macOS still makes a tap action, measured on hardware (S1: under the thumb, one-finger touches held 40–169 ms clicked; every touch held 297–763 ms did not; three-finger taps held 40–120 ms looked up; 400 ms is above the shortest non-acting hold, 297 ms, with margin). Strictly less qualifies. Do NOT reuse `maxTapDuration` (also 400 ms) on purpose: that says what fires, this says what macOS clicks. Doc comment says so.
- Nothing in core turns the window into a frame-time deadline; the recognizer never reads a clock.

### `Uptime` (core value) and the clock (platform)

- New core value type `Uptime`: monotonic seconds of the process's own clock (what `ProcessInfo.systemUptime` reads), with `+ Duration` and `Comparable`. Distinct from `FrameTime` (the multitouch driver's clock) so comparing the two clocks is a compile error. Core defines the value and its arithmetic and never reads the clock.
- `LauncherPlatform` reads `now` in one small extension (e.g. `extension Uptime { static var now: Uptime }` in its own file). The source-scan test (`Tests/LauncherCoreTests/SourcePolicyTests.swift`) confines `systemUptime` to that file (add it to its list in the style already there).
- `DeviceSession` (`Sources/LauncherPlatform/FrameStream.swift`) takes its clock as a dependency: a `@Sendable () -> Uptime` closure the real adapter (`MultitouchTrackpads.run`) fills with that read, so a platform test can move time without sleeping. The click tap (`Sources/LauncherPlatform/ClickTap.swift`) reads the same clock once per event it decides.

### What a trackpad's frames have told the tap: one core value, used by both adapters

- The session's shared state, today a bare `Mutex(Blocking.none)` (`level`), becomes a small core struct (suggested name `TrackpadTapState`; pick a glossary-consistent name) holding the level (`Blocking`) and the end (`Uptime?`) of the lift window last opened on that trackpad, with three operations:
  - take one `Recognition` at `now`: replace the level; when the recognition opens a window, stamp `now + GestureRules.liftWindow`; once the stored end has passed, clear it (so an ordinary touch reads no clock);
  - `liftWindowIsOpen(at: Uptime) -> Bool` (strictly less than the end is open);
  - `wantsTap(at: Uptime) -> Bool`: the level's `wantsTap`, or an open window.
- The frame thread compares `wantsTap` between frames to wake the tap thread, as it compares `blocking.wantsTap` today, so the wake also fires on the first frame after a window ended: that frame is what lets the gate close. The clock is read once per frame only while a window end is pending (or when the recognition opens one), and once per event the tap decides.
- A pure merge over every running trackpad's value at `now` gives the tap its context: the highest level and whether any window is open. Today `mergedBlocking()` in `FrameStream.swift` does the level half.
- `TapContext` (`Sources/LauncherCore/TapEvent.swift`) gains `liftWindowIsOpen: Bool`; `estimated(from:)` gains the same flag and keeps deriving its scroll estimate from the level alone (a window never opens a closed gate: the lifting contact was down, so the port was already on).

### The tap's rules (`Sources/LauncherCore/TapFilters.swift`)

- `TapFilters(blocksTapClicks:)` already exists from S2 (stored, unread). Now: a press is blocked when the level blocks clicks, OR when `blocksTapClicks` and `context.liftWindowIsOpen`. Pass that combined boolean to `ClickFilter` as its `trackpadBlocking`. `ClickFilter` itself is untouched, so a press that starts in the window is withheld whole: its drags become moves and its release is dropped (R2's tap-and-hold drag).
- Scrolls and Dock swipes: unchanged; the window never suppresses scrolling.
- The gate: `settle`'s predicate gains "a lift window is open" (`context.liftWindowIsOpen`), so the gated port stays on through the window after the pad empties. The port closes lazily, on the next frame (the frame thread wakes on the flip) or the next event the tap is sent, whichever comes first. Update the invariant comment on `TapFilters` to name the lift window.
- Put a short comment at the press rule recording the hardware assumption it rests on: under a thumb rested past 3 s the level is `.dockSwipes`, where a physical press passes, so a tap's click is caught only because its lift frame reaches the session before the click reaches the tap (S1 measured every click after its lift frame, smallest gap 1.7 ms over ~75 taps). Keep it to two or three lines.

### The fake (`Tests/LauncherCoreTests/InMemoryAdapters.swift`, `InMemoryTrackpads`)

- Keeps one `TrackpadTapState` (or your name) per running trackpad in place of `levels`, and uses the same merge.
- Owns a monotonic `Uptime` clock that never goes backwards, follows the latest frame time it is handed on any trackpad (convert the frame's time offset to `Uptime` seconds), and can be advanced by a row without frames (`advanceClock(by: Duration)`). So a scripted `wait` of empty frames after a lift is time passing with the pad empty (the "next frame" path), and an advance without frames is time passing with nothing arriving (the real "next event" path).
- `offer(.pointer(.press(_)))` is decided at that clock: the fake takes a press from `offer` as the window server would hand it (a tap's click is never seen by a frame, so it does not ride the frames' `press` field); `pressVerdicts` keeps recording only the frames' presses.
- Its wake rule mirrors the real session's (compare `wantsTap(at:)` between frames).

## Rules that apply

- architecture.md, Shape: "`LauncherCore` (domain types, pure policy, ports, the models; imports Foundation and Observation only)". `Uptime` and the state value live in core and never read a clock.
- architecture.md, Shape: "Framework and wire types never cross a port" and "Every port has exactly two adapters: the real one in `LauncherPlatform` and an in-memory one in the tests." Both adapters use the same core state value and merge.
- architecture.md, Concurrency: "Multitouch frames are recognised on the framework's own thread … State shared with that thread lives behind a `Mutex` with one writer." The session's state value has one writer (its frame callback); the tap thread only reads. "Tap thread: … Copies the sessions out first: never two locks at once." Keep that.
- architecture.md, Concurrency: "No timers, no polling, no background loops … The one deadline outside both is the lift window: a frame thread stamps its end on the process's monotonic clock (read at receipt, never the frame's own time, which is the driver's clock) and the click tap's thread compares the stamp with that clock when an event arrives; nothing fires at the end, and the gate it held open closes on the next frame or event. … A lift window's end is noticed on the next frame or the next event, never by a timer … A trackpad's frame thread only wakes that thread when what it needs flips, a lift window ending included; no frame thread enables or disables a tap." No timer, no dispatch-after, no sleeping in production code or in the platform test.
- architecture.md, Privacy: "The permission checks and their TCC push, screen capture, the event taps and the scroll-event API are each confined to one file by the source-scan test … add to its list rather than adding prose." Confine `systemUptime` the same way.
- domain-model.md, *Lift window*: "The first 0.5 seconds after a contact other than the thumb lifts inside a thumb touch, when macOS could read that contact as a tap: it was down for less than the *tap action hold limit* … and no press was seen while it was down. A tap's fingers, a swipe's or a window swipe's fingers that lift quickly, and a finger that loses contact for a moment open it; each such lift restarts it, so it ends 0.5 s after the last one. Whether the thumb is still down changes nothing. A thumb lifting alone opens none; nor do fingers that rested or scrolled past the hold limit (a swap series' fingers, a scroll under a rested thumb), a finger that pressed the pad down, or any lift outside a thumb touch. During it every tap action is blocked: a click, a deliberate one included, and a press that starts in it is blocked whole … Scrolling and the physical press rules are not changed by it. It is measured on the process's clock from the moment the lift's frame arrives; strictly less than 0.5 s is inside. The gated port stays on through it and closes on the next frame or event after it ends."
- domain-model.md, *Tap action*: "A tap's click is blocked only while Tap to click is on for a connected trackpad … A physical press is not a tap action and keeps the click blocking rules."
- domain-model.md, *Thumb touch*: "The stretch of frames from one with a thumb in the anchor corner … until the first frame with no contact on the trackpad, edge contacts aside."
- Use the glossary words in names and comments: *lift window*, *tap action*, *tap action hold limit*, *thumb touch*.
